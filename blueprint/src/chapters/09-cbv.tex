\chapter{Beyond the paper: a call-by-value model (Melli\`es \S7.4)}%
\label{ch:cbv}

\paragraph{This chapter is beyond the Ehrhard--Geoffroy paper.}
Their \S9 is the LNL/Seely model of intuitionistic linear logic
(Chapter~\ref{ch:exponential}); a \emph{call-by-value} reading is the
paper's explicitly \emph{stated future work} --- the conclusion writes
that ``in future work we will explain how this model can be used for
interpreting call-by-value or even call-by-push-value $\ldots$
languages.'' The development of this chapter takes a step in that
direction, following Melli\`es' \emph{Categorical Semantics of Linear
Logic}~\cite{mellies-categorical-semantics}, \S7.4: the
\emph{Eilenberg--Moore} (coalgebra) route to the exponential gives a
\emph{call-by-value} model, exactly as the co-Kleisli route gives
call-by-name (the latter is the cartesian closed $\mathbf{SCones}$ of
Chapter~\ref{ch:stable}). Nothing here is filed as a paper-\S\ result; it
is a \emph{beyond-the-paper} construction on top of the axiom-free Seely
category of \autoref{thm:seely}.

\paragraph{What is, and is not, done.}
The CBV model is built and \textbf{axiom-free} (only the three classical
\texttt{boolp} axioms): the \emph{full} cartesian Eilenberg--Moore
category, the (lax symmetric) monoidal adjunction
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.cbv_adjunction.ICones_CBV}, and a small first-order
CBV calculus interpreted into it. The cartesianness of
$\mathrm{EM}(\mathord{!})$ is now \emph{unconditional} --- the
predicate \rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.EMComon} holds on every
$\mathord{!}$-coalgebra (\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.EMComon_all}),
following Melli\`es' Proposition 26--28 / Corollary 17/20 by the
structural retraction-and-lifting argument (\emph{not} the naïve
promoted-point reduction, which only computes on $x!$ and stalls on a
general carrier); $U \dashv \widetilde{\mathord{!}}$ is therefore a
\emph{linear / non-linear} adjunction with \emph{the} category of
$\mathord{!}$-coalgebras as the cartesian non-linear / value side.

\paragraph{Higher-order direct-style CBV via EM(!) Kleisli exponentials.}
The value category $\mathrm{EM}(\mathord{!})$ is cartesian but
\emph{not} cartesian closed --- a structural fact about EM categories of
linear-exponential comonads, not a missing diagram chase. What
\emph{direct-style} (Plotkin/Girard) CBV actually requires, however, are
not full internal homs but \emph{Kleisli exponentials} for the CBV monad
$T = \widetilde{\mathord{!}} \circ U$, and those
$\mathrm{EM}(\mathord{!})$ does have. The natural-bijection chain
%
\begin{align*}
  \mathrm{Hom}_{EM}(C \times A,\, T B)
  &\;\cong\;\mathrm{Hom}_{IC}(U(C \times A),\, U(T B))
        & \text{cofree } U \dashv \widetilde{\mathord{!}}\\
  &\;\cong\;\mathrm{Hom}_{IC}(U C \otimes U A,\, U(T B))
        & U \text{ strict monoidal}\\
  &\;\cong\;\mathrm{Hom}_{IC}(U C,\, U A \multimap U(T B))
        & \text{SMCC closure of } \ICones\\
  &\;\cong\;\mathrm{Hom}_{EM}(C,\, \widetilde{\mathord{!}}(U A \multimap U(T B)))
        & \text{cofree } U \dashv \widetilde{\mathord{!}}
\end{align*}
%
uses only the cofree adjunction, the strict monoidality of $U$
($U(C \times A) = U C \otimes U A$ definitionally,
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.cbv_adjunction.cbv_U_prod}), and the SMCC closure of
$\ICones$; it does \emph{not} use any cartesian closure of
$\mathrm{EM}(\mathord{!})$. The function-type interpretation that
results is $\sem{\mathtt{tfun}\,A\,B} =
\widetilde{\mathord{!}}\,(U A \multimap U(T B))$ (\autoref{sec:cbv-ppl}) ---
with the $T$ on the codomain encoding the fact that every function call
is potentially effectful in CBV.
This is \texttt{theories/programs/ppl.v}: a higher-order,
\emph{direct-style} (no value/computation term split, no \texttt{tprob}
type marker, no syntactic \texttt{return}, no \texttt{bind}), \emph{single-sort},
\emph{multi-variable}, \emph{named-variable} QBS-mirror calculus --- the
modern PPL surface that \texttt{mathcomp-qbs/ppl} provides and that the
Heunen--Kammar--Staton--Yang LICS 2017 line of work describes ---
interpreted through the Kleisli-exponential chain above and fully
axiom-free. The QBS-paper showcase \texttt{ex\_random\_constant}
--- a distribution over a function space, with source type
$\mathtt{tfun}\,\mathtt{tR}\,\mathtt{tR}$ (\emph{not}
$\mathtt{tprob}\,(\mathtt{tfun}\,\mathtt{tR}\,\mathtt{tR})$) --- and the
``random linear'' killer demo \texttt{ex\_random\_linear} ---
the distribution over $\lambda x.\,m\cdot x + b$ for $m, b \sim \mu$,
again with source type $\mathtt{tfun}\,\mathtt{tR}\,\mathtt{tR}$ ---
both interpret soundly via this Kleisli-exponential structure (see
\texttt{theories/programs/examples.v} for the surface-syntax definitions
and reduction lemmas). The Dirac/integration view of the Moggi-Kleisli
bind,
\[
  \mathtt{bind}(m,\,k)(\gamma)
    \;\;=\;\;
    \int k(\gamma,\,a)\;\mathrm{d}m(\gamma)(a),
\]
together with the FMeas lax monoidal map (\autoref{thm:cbv-fmeas-lax}),
is what lets \texttt{ne\_add} / \texttt{ne\_mul} recover the QBS-style
``distribution of $\lambda x.\,m\cdot x + b$'' reading on Dirac inputs.

\paragraph{No further open milestones for higher-order CBV.}
In the direct-style QBS-mirror surface of \texttt{ppl.v} every term is
interpreted as a Kleisli arrow through $T = \widetilde{\mathord{!}} \circ U$
--- there is \emph{no} \texttt{tprob} type-level marker in the source
language; the monad lives entirely in the term interpretation
\texttt{eD}, and pure constructors are made into Kleisli arrows by
post-composition with the unit \texttt{tunit\_eta} (the implicit return
that direct-style CBV needs). Applying a function value to an argument
is monadic application by construction, with the $T$ on the codomain of
$\sem{\mathtt{tfun}\,A\,B}$ ensuring every call returns a computation.
EM(!) being non-closed is structural and irrelevant. Adequacy,
normalization, and full-abstraction are out of scope here, as are
booleans / coproducts (no native sum type in the cones model) and the
QBS-paper's \texttt{qbs\_normalize} / Bayesian-posterior showcase (a
different observable than the unnormalised prior/score/observe shape
covered by \texttt{ex\_bayes\_linear}).

\paragraph{Where the material lives.}
\texttt{theories/homs/em\_cat.v} (the EM category, the cofree functor and
the cofree/forgetful adjunction), \texttt{theories/homs/em\_seely\_comonoid.v}
(the Seely comonoid on $\mathord{!}A$ and Melli\`es' conditions
LC2--LC4), \texttt{theories/homs/em\_cartesian.v} (the \emph{full}
cartesian $\mathrm{EM}(\mathord{!})$ carried by the linear $\otimes$,
with the unconditional \rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.EMComon_all}),
\texttt{theories/programs/infra/cbv\_adjunction.v} (the LNL monoidal adjunction
and the \texttt{CBV\_Model} bundle), \texttt{theories/homs/fmeas\_lax.v}
(the FMeas lax symmetric monoidal map at the
\texttt{icones\_hom} level, built on the discharged
\rocq{Icones.homs.bilin.int_to_linhom_pres_path_in_cone}),
\texttt{theories/programs/cbv.v} (the first-order CBV calculus and its
interpretation), \texttt{theories/programs/ppl.v} (the higher-order
direct-style multi-variable named-variable QBS-mirror calculus), and
\texttt{theories/programs/examples.v} (the three QBS-style examples
\texttt{ex\_random\_constant} / \texttt{ex\_random\_linear} /
\texttt{ex\_bayes\_linear} in surface syntax, with their denotation
reduction lemmas).

\section{The Eilenberg--Moore category and the cofree adjunction}%
\label{sec:cbv-em}

The exponential comonad $(\mathord{!}, \mathrm{der}, \mathrm{dig})$ of
\autoref{thm:comonad} has an Eilenberg--Moore category
$\mathrm{EM}(\mathord{!})$: its objects are $\mathord{!}$-coalgebras
$(A, a : A \to \mathord{!}A)$ (the minimal coalgebra layer of
\texttt{coalgebra.v}, paper Theorem 9.7), and its morphisms are coalgebra
morphisms. This section packages that category and the cofree/forgetful
adjunction; everything is a diagram chase from the comonad and coalgebra
laws --- no new analysis.

\begin{definition}[The Eilenberg--Moore category $\mathrm{EM}(\mathord{!})$]%
\label{def:cbv-em-cat}
\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cat.coalg_hom}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cat.EM_Cat}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cat.ICones_EM}\rocqok
\uses{thm:comonad, def:comonad-record}
A bundled coalgebra morphism \rocq{Icones.homs.em_cat.coalg_hom} is an
$\ICones$-morphism together with the coalgebra-morphism side-condition.
The identity and composition (\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cat.coalg_id},
\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cat.coalg_comp}) satisfy the category laws
(\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cat.coalg_compIl},
\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cat.coalg_compIr},
\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cat.coalg_compA}), bundled in the record
\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cat.EM_Cat} with canonical witness
\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cat.ICones_EM} --- mirroring the
\rocq{Icones.homs.bang.Comonad} record-with-witness convention.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[The cofree functor $\widetilde{\mathord{!}}$
and the forgetful functor $U$]\label{def:cbv-cofree}
\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cat.bang_cofree}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cat.U_obj}\rocqok
\uses{def:cbv-em-cat, def:struct-maps}
The cofree coalgebra on $B$ is
$\widetilde{\mathord{!}}B = (\mathord{!}B, \mathrm{dig}_B)$
(\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cat.bang_cofree}), a coalgebra by the counit /
coassociativity laws \autoref{thm:comonad}; its action on a morphism is
$\widetilde{\mathord{!}}f = \mathord{!}f$
(\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cat.bang_cofree_hom}, a coalgebra morphism by
naturality of $\mathrm{dig}$), with functoriality
\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cat.bang_cofree_fmap_id} /
\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cat.bang_cofree_fmap_comp}. The forgetful functor
$U : \mathrm{EM}(\mathord{!}) \to \ICones$ takes $(A, a)$ to its carrier
$A$ (\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cat.U_obj}, \rocq{Icones.homs.em_cat.U_mor}).
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[The cofree adjunction
$U \dashv \widetilde{\mathord{!}}$]\label{thm:cbv-cofree-adj}
\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cat.adj_phi}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cat.adj_psi}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cat.adj_triangleL}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cat.adj_triangleR}\rocqok
\uses{def:cbv-cofree}

$U \dashv \widetilde{\mathord{!}}$, presented by the natural
bijection of hom-sets
\[
  \mathrm{EM}(\mathord{!})\bigl(\gamma,\ \widetilde{\mathord{!}}B\bigr)
  \;\cong\;
  \ICones\bigl(U\gamma,\ B\bigr),
\]
namely \rocq{Icones.homs.em_cat.adj_phi} and
\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cat.adj_psi}, mutually inverse
(\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cat.adj_phiK},

(\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cat.adj_phi_natL},
\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cat.adj_phi_natR}). The unit is the coalgebra
structure map (\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cat.adj_unit}) and the counit is the
dereliction $\mathrm{der}$ (\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cat.adj_counit}); the
triangle identities are \rocq{Icones.homs.em_cat.adj_triangleL} and
\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cat.adj_triangleR}.
\end{theorem}

\section{The Seely comonoid on \texorpdfstring{$\mathord{!}A$}{!A} (LC2--LC4)}\label{sec:cbv-comonoid}

To organise the cartesian structure we first equip every cofree object
$\mathord{!}A$ with the commutative-comonoid structure of a Melli\`es
\emph{linear category} (conditions LC2--LC4 of
\cite{mellies-categorical-semantics}, \S7.4), transported from the
cartesian diagonal / terminal of $(\mathbin{\&}, \top)$ through the Seely
isomorphisms $\mathrm{Seely2}$ / $\mathrm{Seely0}$ of
\autoref{def:seely2}. As throughout \autoref{ch:exponential}, the laws
are diagram chases on promoted points, reduced by the
$\texttt{bang\_ext}$ / $\texttt{tens\_excl\_charact}$ extensionality and
the $\mathrm{Seely2E}$ / $\mathrm{Seely0E}$ computation laws.

\begin{definition}[The comultiplication $d$ and counit $e$ on
$\mathord{!}A$]\label{def:cbv-d-e}
\rocq{Icones.homs.em_seely_comonoid.d_bang}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.homs.em_seely_comonoid.e_bang}\rocqok
\uses{def:seely2, def:cbv-cofree}
The comultiplication
$d_A : \mathord{!}A \lin \mathord{!}A \otimes \mathord{!}A$ is
$\mathrm{Seely2}^{-1} \circ \mathord{!}(\Delta_A)$ for the cartesian
diagonal $\Delta_A$ (\rocq{Icones.homs.em_seely_comonoid.diag}), and the
counit $e_A : \mathord{!}A \lin 1$ is
$\mathrm{Seely0}^{-1} \circ \mathord{!}(\langle\rangle_A)$ for the
terminal map (\rocq{Icones.homs.em_seely_comonoid.term}). Their
computation laws on a promoted point $x!$ (for $\cnorm{x} \leq 1$) are
$d_A(x!) = x! \otimes x!$ (\rocq{Icones.homs.em_seely_comonoid.d_bang_prom})
and $e_A(x!) = 1$ (\rocq{Icones.homs.em_seely_comonoid.e_bang_prom}).
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[LC2: $(\mathord{!}A, d_A, e_A)$ is a commutative
comonoid]\label{lem:cbv-lc2}
\rocq{Icones.homs.em_seely_comonoid.comonoid_coassoc}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.homs.em_seely_comonoid.comonoid_counitL}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.homs.em_seely_comonoid.comonoid_counitR}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.homs.em_seely_comonoid.comonoid_cocomm}\rocqok
\uses{def:cbv-d-e, lem:tens-excl-charact}
The triple satisfies coassociativity
(\rocq{Icones.homs.em_seely_comonoid.comonoid_coassoc}), the two counit
laws (\rocq{Icones.homs.em_seely_comonoid.comonoid_counitL},
\rocq{Icones.homs.em_seely_comonoid.comonoid_counitR}) and
cocommutativity (\rocq{Icones.homs.em_seely_comonoid.comonoid_cocomm}).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Each law is reduced, by \autoref{lem:tens-excl-charact}, to agreement on
a promoted pure tensor; both sides then compute by
\rocq{Icones.homs.em_seely_comonoid.d_bang_prom} /
\rocq{Icones.homs.em_seely_comonoid.e_bang_prom} and the structural-iso
laws of the tensor.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[LC3 / LC4: $d$, $e$ are coalgebra morphisms and
$\mathrm{dig}$ is a comonoid morphism]\label{lem:cbv-lc34}
\rocq{Icones.homs.em_seely_comonoid.d_bang_is_coalg_mor}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.homs.em_seely_comonoid.e_bang_is_coalg_mor}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.homs.em_seely_comonoid.dig_comonoid_mult}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.homs.em_seely_comonoid.dig_comonoid_counit}\rocqok
\uses{lem:cbv-lc2}
LC3: $d_A$ and $e_A$ are morphisms of coalgebras
(\rocq{Icones.homs.em_seely_comonoid.d_bang_is_coalg_mor},
\rocq{Icones.homs.em_seely_comonoid.e_bang_is_coalg_mor}), with the
target coalgebras the transported $\mathord{!}A \otimes \mathord{!}A$
(\rocq{Icones.homs.em_seely_comonoid.tens_cofree}) and $1$
(\rocq{Icones.homs.em_seely_comonoid.unit_cofree}). LC4: the
comultiplication $\mathrm{dig}_A$ is a comonoid morphism, compatible with
$d$ (\rocq{Icones.homs.em_seely_comonoid.dig_comonoid_mult}) and with $e$
(\rocq{Icones.homs.em_seely_comonoid.dig_comonoid_counit}).
\end{lemma}

\begin{remark}[The symmetric-monoidal level only]
The objects $\mathord{!}A \otimes \mathord{!}A$
(\rocq{Icones.homs.em_seely_comonoid.tens_cofree}) and $1$
(\rocq{Icones.homs.em_seely_comonoid.unit_cofree}) carry coalgebra
structures inherited across $\mathrm{Seely2}$ / $\mathrm{Seely0}$ --- the
symmetric-monoidal (LC1-level) structure of $\mathrm{EM}(\mathord{!})$.
This is \emph{not yet} the cartesian product, which is the subject of the
next section.
\end{remark}

\section{The cartesian \texorpdfstring{$\mathrm{EM}(\mathord{!})$}{EM(!)} via the linear \texorpdfstring{$\otimes$}{tensor}}\label{sec:cbv-cartesian}

This section equips the \emph{full} Eilenberg--Moore category
$\mathrm{EM}(\mathord{!})$ with a cartesian structure whose binary
product is carried by the \emph{linear} tensor $\otimes$ (\textbf{not} the
cartesian $\mathbin{\&}$) and whose terminal object is the tensor unit
$1$. This is the hallmark of the EM/CBV reading: in the co-Kleisli (CBN)
category the product is $\mathbin{\&}$, while in the Eilenberg--Moore
(CBV) category the product is $\otimes$. The non-trivial point is that
this works on \emph{every} $\mathord{!}$-coalgebra, not only on a rich
sub-class --- Melli\`es' Proposition 26--28 and Corollary 17/20 ---
proved here by his structural retraction-and-lifting argument.

\begin{definition}[The lax-monoidal map, the product and terminal
coalgebras]\label{def:cbv-prod}
\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.m_bang}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.EM_prod}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.EM_term}\rocqok
\uses{thm:cbv-cofree-adj, lem:cbv-lc34, lem:tens-excl-charact}
The lax symmetric-monoidal structure map of $\mathord{!}$ is
$m_{A,B} : \mathord{!}A \otimes \mathord{!}B \lin \mathord{!}(A \otimes B)$
(\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.m_bang}), with
$m(x! \otimes y!) = (x \otimes y)!$
(\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.m_bang_prom}); it is a coalgebra morphism
(\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.m_bang_is_coalg_mor}). The product
coalgebra is $(A \otimes B,\ m \circ (a \otimes b))$
(\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.EM_prod}) and the terminal coalgebra is
$(1, \cdot)$ (\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.EM_term}, the
$\texttt{unit\_cofree}$ coalgebra). The Eq-88 transported comonoid maps
on \emph{every} coalgebra are
\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.coalg_d} /
\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.coalg_e}; these definitions are general,
but their laws are per-object (next).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[The comonoidality predicate $\mathtt{EMComon}$]%
\label{def:cbv-comon}
\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.EMComon}\rocqok
\uses{def:cbv-prod}
A coalgebra $P$ is \emph{comonoidal} ---
\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.EMComon} holds of it --- when the
transported maps \rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.coalg_d} /
\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.coalg_e} make its carrier a commutative
comonoid \emph{and} are coalgebra morphisms (Melli\`es' Propositions 26 /
27 and Corollary 20). This is the per-object property the cartesian
construction needs --- and it turns out to hold on every coalgebra
(\autoref{lem:cbv-emcomon-all}).
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[$\mathtt{EMComon}$ is unconditional --- Melli\`es
Proposition 26--28]\label{lem:cbv-emcomon-all}
\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.EMComon_all}\rocqok
\uses{def:cbv-comon, lem:cbv-lc2, lem:cbv-lc34}
For \emph{every} $\mathord{!}$-coalgebra $P = (A, a)$, the transported
maps \rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.coalg_d} /
\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.coalg_e} satisfy all the comonoid /
coalgebra-morphism laws --- i.e.\ \rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.EMComon_all}.
The cofree coalgebras $\widetilde{\mathord{!}}B$
(\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.EMComon_cofree}) and the Theorem-9.7
coalgebras $\FMeas(X)$
(\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.EMComon_FMeas}) are corollaries.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The naïve approach --- reduce everything to \emph{promoted points} via
\rocq{Icones.homs.em_seely_comonoid.d_bang_prom} --- cannot work for a
general $P = (A, a)$, because an image $a\,x$ is not promoted: the
defining computation of $d_A$ on $x!$ does not apply, and the laws fail
to reduce. We follow Melli\`es' \emph{structural} proof instead, a
retraction-and-lifting argument that never computes on points.

The first ingredient is that every coalgebra $P = (A, a)$ is a
\emph{retract} of its cofree coalgebra $\widetilde{\mathord{!}}\,UA$
inside $\mathrm{EM}(\mathord{!})$ (Melli\`es' Proposition 26): the
structure map $a : P \to \widetilde{\mathord{!}}\,UA$ is a coalgebra
morphism by the coassociativity of $P$, and the counit
$\varepsilon = \mathrm{der}_A$ retracts it
($\mathrm{der}_A \circ a = \mathrm{id}_A$ is the coalgebra counit law).
\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.diagram81} records the matching square
$(a \otimes a) \circ \mathrm{coalg\_d}\,P = d_{\mathord{!}A} \circ a$
on the nose --- not by point computation but by instantiating the
\emph{generally} valid \rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.coalg_mor_d} at
the coalgebra morphism $a$, then folding through
\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.coalg_d_cofree}.

The second ingredient is that retracts of commutative comonoids are
commutative comonoids (Proposition 27). The four laws --- counit, left
counit, cocommutativity, coassociativity --- are transported along the
retraction by generic SMC cancellation
(\texttt{lcancel\_mono}) combined with the naturality of the associator,
braiding, and unitors against Diagram (85)
(\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.diagram81} above) and the cofree
comonoid laws on $\widetilde{\mathord{!}}\,UA$
(\autoref{lem:cbv-lc2}).

The hard step is showing the transported diagonal $\mathrm{coalg\_d}\,P$
is itself a coalgebra morphism into the product coalgebra $P \otimes P$
--- Melli\`es' Corollary 20, the one he flags as ``not so immediate''.
We package it as a generic \emph{lifting lemma}
\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.coalg_mor_lift} (Melli\`es' \S6.11
Proposition 20): if $i : X \to Y$ is a coalgebra morphism with a
retraction $r$ on the underlying carrier, and a candidate $f$ satisfies
$i \circ f$ is a coalgebra morphism, then $f$ is a coalgebra morphism.
Instantiated at $i = a \otimes a$ (a coalgebra morphism with retraction
$\mathrm{der} \otimes \mathrm{der}$) and $f = \mathrm{coalg\_d}\,P$, the
hypothesis $i \circ f = d_{\mathord{!}A} \circ a$ is exactly
\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.diagram81}, and a composite of two
coalgebra morphisms ($d_{\mathord{!}A}$ on the cofree side from
\autoref{lem:cbv-lc34}, and $a$); hence
$\mathrm{coalg\_d}\,P : P \to P \otimes P$ is a coalgebra morphism
(\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.coalg_d_is_mor_gen}). The easy half for
the counit is a composite of coalgebra morphisms
(\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.coalg_e_is_mor_gen}). Bundling the
four comonoid laws and the two coalgebra-morphism facts gives
\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.EMComon_all}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[$\mathrm{EM}(\mathord{!})$ is cartesian]%
\label{thm:cbv-cartesian}
\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.ICones_EM_cartesian}\rocqok
\uses{def:cbv-prod, lem:cbv-emcomon-all, def:cbv-em-cat}
The linear tensor $\otimes$ is the categorical product of the full
$\mathrm{EM}(\mathord{!})$ and the tensor unit $1$ is terminal. The
data --- projections \rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.em_proj1_mor} /
\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.em_proj2_mor}, pairing
\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.em_pair} (no $\mathtt{EMComon}$
hypothesis needed, by \autoref{lem:cbv-emcomon-all}), the
$\beta$-laws \rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.em_proj1_pair} /
\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.em_proj2_pair}, and the terminal
universal property \rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.em_term_mor} /
\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.em_term_unique} --- is bundled in the
record \rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.EM_Cartesian} with witness
\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.ICones_EM_cartesian} (axiom-free), whose
product/terminal field types reference the \emph{linear} $\otimes$, not
$\mathbin{\&}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Each coalgebra $P$ is comonoidal by \autoref{lem:cbv-emcomon-all}, so
Melli\`es' Corollary 17 applies: the projections, diagonal and pairing
out of $P$ are built from the comonoid data of $P$, $Q$. The
$\beta$-laws and terminal uniqueness follow from the comonoid laws of
\autoref{lem:cbv-lc2} as transported by
\autoref{lem:cbv-emcomon-all}.
\end{proof}

\section{The cartesian \texorpdfstring{$\eta$}{eta}-rule on
\texorpdfstring{$\mathrm{EM}(\mathord{!})$}{EM(!)} (Fox 1976 /
Melli\`es Prop 28)}\label{sec:cbv-cart-eta}

The $\beta$-laws \rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.em_proj1_pair} /
\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.em_proj2_pair} of
\autoref{thm:cbv-cartesian} establish the universal property of the
binary product \emph{out of} an arbitrary coalgebra $Z$. To complete the
cartesian structure we also need the $\eta$-rule
$\langle \pi_1, \pi_2 \rangle = \mathrm{id}$ at the linear level
$\ICones$. This is genuinely additional content --- it is
\textbf{Fox's 1976 theorem} that the EM category of a linear-exponential
comonad is cartesian under the linear tensor (Melli\`es Proposition 28,
icones level\footnote{The icones-level statement of the cartesian
$\eta$-rule has, to our knowledge, no published machine-checked
formalisation; this is the first Coq / Rocq proof we are aware of.}).
The proof follows the same retract-and-lift recipe as
\autoref{lem:cbv-emcomon-all}: reduce to the cofree case, then transport
along the retraction.

\begin{lemma}[$\eta$-rule, cofree case]\label{lem:cbv-cart-eta-cofree}
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.cbv_adjunction.em_pair_mor_proj_id_cofree}\rocqok
\uses{thm:cbv-cartesian, lem:tens-excl-charact}
For every $A, B : \ICones$, the pair-of-projections on the cofree
product equals the identity at the icones level:
\[
  \mathtt{em\_pair\_mor}\;(\pi_1)\;(\pi_2)
  \;=\;
  \mathrm{id}_{\mathord{!}A \otimes \mathord{!}B}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By \rocq{Icones.homs.em_seely_comonoid.tens_excl_charact}, it suffices
to check both sides agree on the promoted pure tensor $x! \otimes y!$
for $\cnorm{x} \leq 1$, $\cnorm{y} \leq 1$. On the cofree pair the
diagonal $\mathtt{coalg\_d}$ unfolds via
\rocq{Icones.homs.em_seely_comonoid.d_bang_prom} /
\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.m_bang_prom} /
\rocq{Icones.homs.bang.dig_prom} to
$(x! \otimes y!) \otimes (x! \otimes y!)$, and the projections
\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.em_proj1_mor} /
\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.em_proj2_mor} reduce on
$x! \otimes y!$ via the unitor + counit + \rocq{Icones.homs.em_seely_comonoid.e_bang_prom}
to $x!$ / $y!$ respectively.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The cartesian $\eta$-rule
$\langle \pi_1, \pi_2 \rangle = \mathrm{id}$]\label{thm:cbv-cart-eta}
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.cbv_adjunction.em_pair_mor_proj_id}\rocqok
\uses{lem:cbv-cart-eta-cofree, lem:cbv-emcomon-all}
For every pair $(P, Q)$ of $\mathord{!}$-coalgebras, the
pair-of-projections of the binary product $P \otimes Q$ in
$\mathrm{EM}(\mathord{!})$ equals the identity on the underlying carrier:
\[
  \mathtt{em\_pair\_mor}\;(\pi_1)\;(\pi_2)
  \;=\;
  \mathrm{id}_{cP \otimes cQ}
  \quad : \quad
  \mathrm{Hom}_{\ICones}(cP \otimes cQ,\, cP \otimes cQ).
\]
Combined with the $\beta$-laws of \autoref{thm:cbv-cartesian}, this
completes the universal property of the EM(!) binary product
$(P \otimes Q,\, \pi_1,\, \pi_2,\, \langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The structure maps
$(\mathtt{coalg\_str}\,P,\,\mathtt{coalg\_str}\,Q) : (P, Q) \to
(\widetilde{\mathord{!}}\,cP,\,\widetilde{\mathord{!}}\,cQ)$
are coalgebra morphisms (the coassociativity of $P$ and $Q$), and the
counit $\mathrm{der}_{cP} \otimes \mathrm{der}_{cQ}$ on the carrier
side retracts them ($\mathrm{der}_{cA} \circ a = \mathrm{id}_{A}$ is
the coalgebra counit). The tensored
$\mathtt{coalg\_str}\,P \otimes \mathtt{coalg\_str}\,Q$ on
$cP \otimes cQ$ is therefore a split mono with retraction
$\mathrm{der}_{cP} \otimes \mathrm{der}_{cQ}$. The $\eta$-rule on
$(P, Q)$ then follows from the cofree case
(\autoref{lem:cbv-cart-eta-cofree}) by naturality of $\pi_1, \pi_2$
along this retraction: $\langle \pi_1, \pi_2 \rangle$ at $(P, Q)$ is
the transport of $\langle \pi_1, \pi_2 \rangle$ at the cofree pair,
which equals the identity by \autoref{lem:cbv-cart-eta-cofree}, and the
retraction transports identities to identities.
\end{proof}

\section{The monoidal adjunction and the CBV model}\label{sec:cbv-model}

The last step assembles the \emph{(lax symmetric) monoidal adjunction}
$U \dashv \widetilde{\mathord{!}}$ (Melli\`es' Proposition 29, via
Lack's lifting of the tensor to $\mathrm{EM}(\mathord{!})$), bundled with
the cartesian value category of \autoref{thm:cbv-cartesian}. Most of this
is assembly; the one block of new content is the lax-monoidal coherence
of $\widetilde{\mathord{!}}$ at the $m$-level, proved on promoted
points by the same $\texttt{tens\_excl\_charact}$ engine as
\autoref{ch:exponential}.

\begin{lemma}[Lax-monoidal coherence of the comparison
$m$]\label{lem:cbv-lax}
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.cbv_adjunction.m_bang_assoc}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.cbv_adjunction.m_bang_lunit}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.cbv_adjunction.m_bang_runit}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.cbv_adjunction.m_bang_braid}\rocqok
\uses{def:cbv-prod, lem:tens-excl-charact}
The comparison map $m$ (\autoref{def:cbv-prod}) satisfies the
associativity \rocq{Icones.programs.infra.cbv_adjunction.m_bang_assoc}, the unitors
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.cbv_adjunction.m_bang_lunit} /
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.cbv_adjunction.m_bang_runit} and the symmetry
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.cbv_adjunction.m_bang_braid}, against the
$\alpha$ / $\lambda$ / $\rho$ / $\sigma$ of the tensor
(\autoref{thm:thm515}). Each is reduced by
\autoref{lem:tens-excl-charact} (and its ternary form) to agreement on
promoted pure tensors, then computed by
\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.m_bang_prom}.
\end{lemma}

\begin{theorem}[The CBV model on $\ICones$]\label{thm:cbv-model}
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.cbv_adjunction.CBV_Model}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.cbv_adjunction.ICones_CBV}\rocqok
\uses{thm:cbv-cofree-adj, thm:cbv-cartesian, lem:cbv-lax, thm:thm515}
The record \rocq{Icones.programs.infra.cbv_adjunction.CBV_Model} bundles the CBV /
LNL data on $\ICones$, mirroring the
\rocq{Icones.homs.seely.SeelyCategory} convention; its canonical witness
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.cbv_adjunction.ICones_CBV} is \textbf{axiom-free}
(modulo the three classical \texttt{boolp} axioms, as for
\rocq{Icones.homs.seely.ICones_Seely}). It packages:
\begin{itemize}
  \item the symmetric monoidal closed structure
    $(\ICones, \otimes, 1)$ (\rocq{Icones.homs.smcc.ICones_SMCC},
    \autoref{thm:thm515});
  \item the Eilenberg--Moore category $\mathrm{EM}(\mathord{!})$
    (\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cat.ICones_EM}, \autoref{def:cbv-em-cat});
  \item the cartesian \emph{value} category --- the \emph{full}
    $\mathrm{EM}(\mathord{!})$ (\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.ICones_EM_cartesian},
    \autoref{thm:cbv-cartesian}) with its $\otimes$-product / $1$-terminal,
    unconditional comonoidality witness
    \rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.EMComon_all}
    (\autoref{lem:cbv-emcomon-all}), and the cofree- /
    Theorem-9.7-generator corollaries
    \rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.EMComon_cofree} /
    \rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.EMComon_FMeas};
  \item the monoidal adjunction $U \dashv \widetilde{\mathord{!}}$
    over the \emph{full} $\mathrm{EM}(\mathord{!})$ (it needs no
    cartesianness): the functors, the (co)units, the hom-bijection with
    its round-trips and the triangle identities (\autoref{thm:cbv-cofree-adj});
  \item the monoidality witnesses: $U$ is strong (strict) monoidal --- the
    product / terminal carriers are the $\otimes$-tensor / $1$-unit, so the
    comparisons are identities --- and $\widetilde{\mathord{!}}$ is
    lax monoidal with comparison $m$
    (\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.cbv_adjunction.bang_m}) and unit
    (\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.cbv_adjunction.bang_e0}), its lax coherence
    (\autoref{lem:cbv-lax}) and the monoidal counit laws
    \rocq{Icones.programs.infra.cbv_adjunction.adj_counit_monoidal2} /
    \rocq{Icones.programs.infra.cbv_adjunction.adj_counit_monoidal0}.
\end{itemize}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Each field is one of the proved lemmas above; the witness
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.cbv_adjunction.ICones_CBV} just assembles them. The
monoidal \emph{unit} laws \rocq{Icones.programs.infra.cbv_adjunction.adj_unit_monoidal2}
/ \rocq{Icones.programs.infra.cbv_adjunction.adj_unit_monoidal0} are recorded as
standalone facts (they are definitional --- the composite $m \circ (\eta
\otimes \eta)$ \emph{is} the product structure map), kept outside the
record to avoid the dependent-type plumbing. The cartesian fields now
hold on the \emph{full} $\mathrm{EM}(\mathord{!})$ unconditionally
(\autoref{lem:cbv-emcomon-all}), so $U \dashv \widetilde{\mathord{!}}$
is a genuine linear / non-linear adjunction with the (full) category of
$\mathord{!}$-coalgebras as the cartesian non-linear / value side.
\end{proof}

\section{A first-order CBV calculus in the model}\label{sec:cbv-calculus}

With \autoref{thm:cbv-model} the model is built; this section interprets
a small first-order CBV calculus into it, as the minimal Moggi-CBV
demonstration. The higher-order companion using the EM(!)
Kleisli exponentials follows in \autoref{sec:cbv-ppl}. The deliverable here is a sound
compositional semantics with the probabilistic primitive
$\mathtt{sample}$ wired to the Theorem-9.7 integral, not an
adequacy/normalization theorem.

\paragraph{The calculus.}
Intrinsically-typed Rocq inductives. Types are unit, a measurable base
type $\base\,X$ for $X \in \mathbf{Ar}$, and binary products. Values are
variables, the unit value, pairs and projections. Computations are
$\creturn V$, $\clet x = M \mathbin{\mathtt{in}} N$, and the
probabilistic primitive $\csample V$ on a value of base type.

\paragraph{The interpretation.}
Types denote coalgebras: $\base\,X \mapsto \FMeas(X)$ (the Thm-9.7
coalgebra), $\mathtt{1} \mapsto \EMterm$, $s \times t \mapsto
\EMprod\,\sem{s}\,\sem{t}$. The CBV \emph{computation monad} is the
monad induced by the adjunction
$U \dashv \widetilde{\mathord{!}}$ of \autoref{thm:cbv-cofree-adj}:
$T = \widetilde{\mathord{!}} \circ U$
(\rocq{Icones.programs.cbv.Tobj}), with unit
\rocq{Icones.programs.cbv.tunit_eta} (the adjunction unit) and Kleisli
composition \rocq{Icones.programs.cbv.kcomp}. Values denote
coalgebra-homomorphisms $\sem{\Gamma \vdash V : t} :
\sem{\Gamma} \to \sem{t}$ (\rocq{Icones.programs.cbv.vlD}); computations
denote homomorphisms $\sem{\Gamma \vdash M : t} :
\sem{\Gamma} \to T\,\sem{t}$ (\rocq{Icones.programs.cbv.cpD}). Both are
total by structural recursion --- the well-definedness half of
soundness.

\begin{theorem}[Soundness]\label{thm:cbv-soundness}
\rocq{Icones.programs.cbv.kcomp_etaR}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.cbv.kcomp_etaL}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.cbv.kcomp_A}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.cbv.vlD_fst_pair}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.cbv.vlD_snd_pair}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.cbv.cpD_sample_var_dirac}\rocqok
\uses{thm:cbv-model}
The interpretation $\sem{-}$ validates:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item The three monad laws for $T$ ---
    \rocq{Icones.programs.cbv.kcomp_etaR},
    \rocq{Icones.programs.cbv.kcomp_etaL},
    \rocq{Icones.programs.cbv.kcomp_A} --- which give the standard
    $\mathtt{let}$ equations at term level
    (\rocq{Icones.programs.cbv.cpD_let_retvar},
    \rocq{Icones.programs.cbv.cpD_letret},
    \rocq{Icones.programs.cbv.cpD_letassoc}).
  \item The product $\beta$-laws
    \rocq{Icones.programs.cbv.vlD_fst_pair} /
    \rocq{Icones.programs.cbv.vlD_snd_pair}.
  \item \textbf{$\mathtt{sample}$ is the integral.} On a Dirac
    value, $\sem{\csample \delta_r}$ is the promoted point $(\delta_r)!$
    --- \rocq{Icones.programs.cbv.cpD_sample_var_dirac} ---, and the general
    case agrees with the Thm-9.7 Coalg structure map
    \rocq{Icones.programs.cbv.cpD_sample_mor} /
    \rocq{Icones.programs.cbv.cpD_sample_is_integral}, which is in turn the
    integration-of-Diracs map of $\FMeas$ (the
    \rocq{Icones.homs.coalgebra.Coalg_dirac} +
    \rocq{Icones.homs.coalgebra.dirac_dense} engine of Theorem 9.7).
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The monad laws come from the adjunction U\,$\dashv$\,$\widetilde{!}$ via
$\mathrm{adj\_phi}$ being functorial $\mathbf{Kleisli}(T) \to \ICones$
(\rocq{Icones.programs.cbv.adj_phi_kcomp}); the product $\beta$-laws come
from the projection $\beta$-laws of \autoref{thm:cbv-cartesian}; the
$\mathtt{sample}$ semantics comes from
\rocq{Icones.homs.coalgebra.Coalg_dirac} on the base case and from the
identification of \rocq{Icones.homs.coalgebra.Coalg} with the
integration-of-Diracs map otherwise.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{A worked example.}
The program below exercises $\csample$, $\clet$-sequencing, pairing, and
the unit value in one well-typed first-order term:
\[
   \Gamma \;=\; x : \base\,X
   \;\;\vdash\;\;
   \clet\,y = \csample\,x\,\mathtt{in}\,\creturn(y, ())
   \;\;:\;\;
   (\base\,X) \times \mathtt{1}.
\]
In the calculus's intrinsic syntax this is
\rocq{Icones.programs.cbv.ex_prog}, of type
$\mathtt{cp}\,(\base\,X)\,(\base\,X \times \mathtt{1})$, and its
denotation
\rocq{Icones.programs.cbv.ex_denot} : $\mathtt{coalg\_hom}\,\FMeas(X)\,
(T\,(\EMprod\,\FMeas(X)\,\EMterm))$
is total by construction (\rocq{Icones.programs.cbv.cpD}).
\begin{lemma}[Reduction of the worked example]%
\label{lem:cbv-example}
\rocq{Icones.programs.cbv.ex_denot_E}\rocqok
\uses{thm:cbv-soundness}
On a measure type the $\mathord{!}$-induced monad identifies
$\csample$ with $\creturn$ (\rocq{Icones.programs.cbv.cpD_sample_ret}), so
the left-unit law collapses the binder and
$\sem{\mathtt{ex\_prog}}$ rewrites to the continuation pairing-with-unit,
precomposed with the value $x$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\rocq{Icones.programs.cbv.cpD_sample_ret} rewrites
$\csample\,V$ as $\creturn\,V$ on a value of base type; the
$\mathtt{let}$/$\mathtt{return}$ left-unit law
(\rocq{Icones.programs.cbv.cpD_letret}, from
\rocq{Icones.programs.cbv.kcomp_etaL} of \autoref{thm:cbv-soundness})
then collapses the binder.
\end{proof}

\section{The boolean cone and the if-then-else combinator}%
\label{sec:cbv-bool}

\paragraph{This section is beyond the paper.}
To support the boolean elimination $\mathtt{ne\_if}$ in the higher-order
direct-style PPL (\autoref{sec:cbv-ppl}) we add a 2-point sub-probability
ICone, recognise it as paper \S4.4 / Theorem 4.24's coproduct
$\mathtt{cone\_one} \oplus \mathtt{cone\_one}$, package its universal
co-pairing as an icones-hom, and assemble an EM(!) Kleisli-level
$\mathtt{case\_em}$ combinator. The 2-point cone itself is a paper
construction (\S4.4); its concrete carrier
$\{\mathrm{nonneg}\,R\} \times \{\mathrm{nonneg}\,R\}$ through the HB
tower and the unit-ball-free icones-hom packaging are new infrastructure
in the spirit of the PPL.

\begin{definition}[The 2-point sub-probability cone]\label{def:cbv-bool-cone}
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.bool_cone.bool_cone_car}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.bool_cone.bool_dirac_true}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.bool_cone.bool_dirac_false}\rocqok
\uses{def:icone}
The carrier $\mathtt{bool\_cone\_car}$ is a thin record
$\{\mathtt{bc\_t} : \{\mathrm{nonneg}\,R\};\; \mathtt{bc\_f} :
\{\mathrm{nonneg}\,R\}\}$ with pointwise operations and norm
$\cnorm{(p, q)} = p + q$; the unit ball is exactly the set of
sub-probability distributions on a 2-point space. The full HB tower
$\mathtt{isPrecone} \to \mathtt{isCone} \to \mathtt{isMCone} \to
\mathtt{isICone}$ is established, with the two Dirac points
$\mathtt{bool\_dirac\_true} = (1, 0)$ and
$\mathtt{bool\_dirac\_false} = (0, 1)$.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Recognition as paper \S4.4 / Thm 4.24 coproduct]
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.bool_cone.bool_case_true}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.bool_cone.bool_case_false}\rocqok
The 2-point cone is the categorical coproduct
$\mathtt{cone\_one\_car} \oplus \mathtt{cone\_one\_car}$ of paper
\S4.4 / Theorem 4.24, with injections
$\mathtt{bool\_dirac\_true} / \mathtt{bool\_dirac\_false}$ playing the
role of $\mathtt{in\_t}(1) / \mathtt{in\_f}(1)$ and the universal
co-pairing $\mathtt{bool\_case}$ playing the role of $[a, b]$.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[The universal co-pairing
$\mathtt{bool\_case}$]\label{def:cbv-bool-case}
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.bool_cone.bool_case}\rocqok
\uses{def:cbv-bool-cone}
For any iCone $A$ and any $a, b \in A$, set
$\mathtt{bool\_case}\;x\;a\;b\;=\;\mathtt{bc\_t}(x)\cdot a +
\mathtt{bc\_f}(x)\cdot b$. This is the unique
$\ICones$-morphism satisfying the coproduct universal property:
$\mathtt{bool\_case}\;\mathtt{bool\_dirac\_true}\;a\;b = a$ and
$\mathtt{bool\_case}\;\mathtt{bool\_dirac\_false}\;a\;b = b$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Universal-property witnesses for
$\mathtt{bool\_case}$]\label{lem:cbv-bool-case-witnesses}
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.bool_cone.bool_case_linear}\rocqok

\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.bool_cone.bool_case_pres_path}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.bool_cone.bool_case_pres_int}\rocqok
\uses{def:cbv-bool-case}
For $a, b \in A$ in the unit ball ($\cnorm{a} \leq 1$, $\cnorm{b} \leq 1$),
the map $x \mapsto \mathtt{bool\_case}\;x\;a\;b$ is linear, $\omega$-continuous,
of operator norm $\leq 1$, preserves measurable paths, and preserves
the Pettis integral against any finite measure. These are the
universal-property witnesses that upgrade the co-pairing to a full
$\ICones$ morphism (paper Def 4.10).
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[Unit-ball-free variants and the test-measurability

A general-cone-element variant of test measurability,
\rocq{Icones.mcones.mcone.test_meas_gen}, drops the unit-ball
hypothesis on the test scrutinee via scaling, exposing the test for

\end{lemma}

\begin{definition}[Icones-hom packaging of $\mathtt{bool\_case}$]%
\label{def:cbv-bool-case-hom}
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.bool_case_hom.bool_case_linhom}\rocqok

(\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.bool_case_hom.bool_case_linhom}) and, via
\rocq{Icones.homs.seely.linhom_icones}, as a full
$\mathtt{icones\_hom}$

unit-ball hypotheses on $a, b$. The unit-ball-free
$\mathtt{bool\_case\_linhom\_gen}$ drops these hypotheses. Although
$(a, b) \mapsto \mathtt{bool\_case}\;\cdot\;a\;b$ is not bilinear in
$(a, b)$, it decomposes as a sum

$\beta(x, b) = \mathtt{bc\_f}(x)\cdot b$ of two separately-bilinear

\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[$\mathtt{case\_em}$ --- the EM(!) Kleisli
if-then-else combinator]\label{def:cbv-case-em}
\rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.case_em}\rocqok
\uses{def:cbv-bool-case-hom, thm:cbv-cofree-adj, thm:cbv-cartesian}

$\mathtt{case\_em}\,a\,b : \mathtt{coalg\_hom}\,(\mathtt{EM\_prod}\,G\,
(\widetilde{\mathord{!}}\,\mathtt{bool\_cone\_car}))\,
(\mathtt{Tobj}\,(\mathtt{tyD}\,A))$ is built by
$\mathtt{adj\_phi}$-ing the branches to icones-homs out of
$U\,\mathtt{EM\_prod}\,G\,(\mathtt{tyD}\,A)$, packaging them
through $\mathtt{bool\_case\_linhom}$ on the
$\mathtt{bool\_cone\_car}$ source, tensor-uncurrying and braiding to
consume the boolean scrutinee, and finally $\mathtt{adj\_psi}$-ing
back into the codomain. The branches' operator norms are bounded by
1 by the hom-cone insight ($\mathtt{coalg\_hom}$s of unit-ball
codomain are automatically norm-$\leq$-1 as icones-homs), so the
unit-ball hypotheses on the branches in
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.bool_case_hom.bool_case_linhom} fire on the nose.
\end{definition}

\section{The CBV value-fixpoint at function types}\label{sec:cbv-yfix-fun}

\paragraph{This section is beyond the paper.}
The fixpoint operators of paper \S9.2
(\rocq{Icones.stable.fixpoint.lfp}, \rocq{Icones.stable.fixpoint.Yfix})
live on $\mathbf{SCones}$ (the cartesian-closed / CBN side) and operate
on stable maps. To support the OCaml-style $\mathtt{let\,rec}$ of the
direct-style PPL (\autoref{sec:cbv-ppl}), we add a \emph{CBV
value-fixpoint at function types} on $\ICones$: a fixpoint of an
endo-operator on the \emph{function-value} cone, packaged as a
$\mathtt{coalg\_hom}$ via the cofree adjunction.

\begin{remark}[Folklore, but new in machine-checked form]
The CBV value-fixpoint at function types is, to our knowledge, not
established in the literature as a formal theorem at the icones / EM(!)
level. Per consultation with P.-A. Melli\`es (2026-05-31), it is
\emph{folklore} --- well-understood as a recipe but not published. To
our knowledge this is the first Coq / Rocq formalisation.
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[$\omega$-continuity prerequisites]\label{lem:cbv-yfix-cont}
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_continuity.linhom_post_icones_sup}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_continuity.linhom_pre_icones_sup}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_continuity.bang_fmap_lin_omega_cont}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_continuity.prom_omega_cont}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_continuity.tensor_mor_omega_cont_R}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_continuity.tensor_mor_R_lin_incr}\rocqok
\uses{def:icone, thm:thm515}
At the $\mathtt{linhom\_car}$ level, the supremum of an
$\omega$-chain in the unit ball commutes with post-composition by an
$\ICones$ morphism, with pre-composition by an $\ICones$ morphism, with
$\mathtt{bang\_fmap}$ of a linear map, and with the $\mathtt{prom}$-style
action; the right-tensor action $\mathtt{tensor\_mor}\,(\mathrm{id},\,\cdot)$
is $\omega$-continuous and monotone in its right argument. These are
the workhorses that let the Kleene supremum of the fixpoint iteration
pass through the $\mathtt{is\_coalg\_mor}$ equation.
\end{lemma}

\begin{definition}[The Kleene iteration $\Phi_{\mathrm{fun}}$ at the
linhom level]\label{def:cbv-phi-fun}
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix.Phi_fun}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix.extract_fun}\rocqok
\uses{def:cbv-cofree, lem:cbv-yfix-cont}
Fix coalgebras $G, A, B$ (the ambient context, the argument type, the
result type). Set $L = U A \multimap U(T B)$ for the CBV monad
$T = \widetilde{\mathord{!}} \circ U$ (the Kleisli exponential) and
$\mathtt{funT} = \widetilde{\mathord{!}}\,L$ (the function-value coalgebra
$\sem{\mathtt{tfun}\,A\,B}$). For a body
$M : \mathtt{coalg\_hom}\,(\mathtt{EM\_prod}\,G\,\mathtt{funT})\,
(\mathtt{Tobj}\,\mathtt{funT})$ (the body of the
$\mathtt{fix\,s.\,\lambda x.\, \ldots}$, evaluated in the extended
context that binds the self-reference $s$), define the Kleene
iteration $\Phi_{\mathrm{fun}}$ on
$\mathtt{linhom\_car}\,(\mathtt{coalg\_obj}\,G)\,(\mathtt{coalg\_obj}\,
\mathtt{funT})$ by
\[
  \Phi_{\mathrm{fun}}(\mathit{prev})
  \;=\;
  \mathtt{bang\_fmap}\,(\mathrm{der}_L)
    \;\circ\;
    \mathtt{ch\_mor}\,M
    \;\circ\;
    \mathtt{tensor\_mor}\,(\mathrm{id}_G,\,\mathit{prev})
    \;\circ\;
    \mathtt{coalg\_d}\,G,
\]
where $\mathtt{extract\_fun} = \mathtt{bang\_cofree\_hom}\,(\mathrm{der}_L)$
is the value-cone extraction (the coalgebra-morphism unfolding of the
recursive reference). $\Phi_{\mathrm{fun}}$ is unit-ball preserving,
monotone in $\mathit{prev}$, and $\omega$-continuous by
\autoref{lem:cbv-yfix-cont}.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[The Kleene fixpoint
$\mathtt{Yfix\_fun\_lin}$]\label{lem:cbv-yfix-lin}
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix.Yfix_fun_lin}\rocqok

\mathtt{linhom\_car}\,(\mathtt{coalg\_obj}\,G)\,
(\mathtt{coalg\_obj}\,\mathtt{funT})$ with
$\cnorm{\mathtt{Yfix\_fun\_lin}} \leq 1$ and
$\Phi_{\mathrm{fun}}\,(\mathtt{Yfix\_fun\_lin}) =
\mathtt{Yfix\_fun\_lin}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{theorem}[$\mathtt{Yfix\_fun\_T}$ --- the CBV value-fixpoint
at function types]\label{thm:cbv-yfix-fun}
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix.Yfix_fun_T}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix.Yfix_fun_T_mor}\rocqok

$\mathtt{adj\_psi}$ of the cofree adjunction

\emph{coalgebra} morphism

  \mathtt{Yfix\_fun\_T}\,M
  \;:\;
  \mathtt{coalg\_hom}\,G\,(\mathtt{Tobj}\,\mathtt{funT}).
\]
$\mathtt{adj\_psi}$ is unconditionally available on any norm-$\leq$-1
icones-hom, so no separate $\mathtt{is\_coalg\_mor}$ obligation arises.
This is the cones-side semantic primitive used by the PPL constructor

\end{theorem}

\autoref{lem:cbv-yfix-lin}; the norm bound gives a

which $\mathtt{adj\_psi}$ produces the coalg-hom
$\mathtt{Yfix\_fun\_T}\,M$ in $\mathtt{coalg\_hom}\,G\,
(\widetilde{\mathord{!}}\,L) = \mathtt{coalg\_hom}\,G\,
(\mathtt{Tobj}\,\mathtt{funT})$.
\end{proof}

\section{A higher-order direct-style QBS-mirror PPL via EM(!) Kleisli exponentials}%
\label{sec:cbv-ppl}

\autoref{sec:cbv-calculus} stops at first order; the higher-order
companion lives in \texttt{theories/programs/ppl.v} and faithfully
ports the \texttt{ppl} branch of \texttt{LLM4Rocq/mathcomp-qbs} (the
QBS-based PPL) to the cones model, in the surface style of
Saito and Affeldt's APLAS 2023 named-variable
embedding\footnote{Saito and Affeldt, \emph{Towards a practical embedding
of a probabilistic programming language in Coq}, APLAS 2023.}.
It is the modern PPL surface --- \emph{direct-style} in the
Plotkin/Girard sense (no value/computation term split, no
$\mathtt{tprob}$ type marker, no syntactic $\mathtt{return}$, no
$\mathtt{bind}$; the probability monad lives entirely in the
interpretation $\mathtt{eD}$ and in the $T$ on the codomain of
$\sem{\mathtt{tfun}\,A\,B}$), \emph{single sort} (one inductive
$\texttt{named\_expr}$, one type indicator $\texttt{ty}$),
\emph{multi-variable} and \emph{named} (intrinsically-typed contexts
$\mathtt{named\_ctx} := \mathtt{seq}\,(\mathtt{string}
\times \mathtt{ppl\_type}\,\mathbf{Ar})$ with a string identifier at
every binding slot) --- interpreted into the EM(!) Kleisli-exponential
structure for the CBV monad $T = \widetilde{\mathord{!}} \circ U$. All
axiom-free.

\paragraph{The calculus.}
Types (\rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.ppl_type}) are
$\mathtt{tunit}$, $\mathtt{tbase}\,X$ for $X \in \mathbf{Ar}$,
$\mathtt{tprod}\,t_1\,t_2$, the function type
$\mathtt{tfun}\,A\,B$, and the boolean type $\mathtt{tbool}$ (the cofree
coalgebra over the 2-point sub-probability cone of
\autoref{def:cbv-bool-cone}) --- that's all: \emph{no} $\mathtt{tprob}$
type-level marker (the previous iteration of this calculus carried one
as a purely syntactic tag; the direct-style refactor drops it, moving
the monad entirely into the interpretation $\mathtt{eD}$ and the
codomain of $\sem{\mathtt{tfun}\,A\,B}$). A named-variable witness
\rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.named_var} indexes the variables of a
context $\Gamma : \mathtt{named\_ctx}$ (a list of
(string, type) pairs). A single intrinsically typed inductive
\rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.named_expr} carries every term, with the
constructors falling into three groups:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{pure}: \rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.ne_var},
    \rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.ne_tt},
    \rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.ne_pair},
    \rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.ne_fst},
    \rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.ne_snd},
    \rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.ne_lam} (lambda, with a string binder;
    body is just another \texttt{named\_expr} in the extended named
    context --- \emph{not} marked as a computation), the \emph{direct}
    application \rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.ne_app},
    \rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.ne_fix} (OCaml-style $\mathtt{let\,rec}$
    recursion, \emph{restricted to function types}; semantically the
    CBV value-fixpoint \rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix.Yfix_fun_T} of
    \autoref{thm:cbv-yfix-fun}),
    \rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.ne_real} (real literal at $r : R$, type
    \texttt{tR}), \rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.ne_add} and
    \rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.ne_mul} (pointwise sum and product on
    \texttt{tR}-valued computations),
    \rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.ne_true} and
    \rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.ne_false} (boolean constants of type
    $\mathtt{tbool}$);
  \item \emph{sequencer}: \rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.ne_let} (direct-style
    CBV $\mathtt{let}\,x = M\,\mathtt{in}\,K$ with a string binder;
    semantically the extended-context Kleisli bind
    \rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.kbind_ext}). Note that
    $\mathtt{ne\_let}$ is the direct-style CBV sequencer of Plotkin/Girard
    --- it carries no $\mathtt{tprob}$ tag on either argument's type;
  \item \emph{effects}: \rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.ne_sample} (sample from
    a unit-ball measure $\mu : \FMeas(R_{\mathrm{obj}})$ --- DIRECT STYLE:
    returns a PURE $\mathtt{tR}$ expression, the monad is hidden in
    $\mathtt{eD}$), \rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.ne_bernoulli} (sample from
    the 2-point sub-probability distribution $(p, 1-p)$ on
    $\mathtt{bool\_cone\_car}$ of \autoref{def:cbv-bool-cone}, returning
    a PURE $\mathtt{tbool}$ expression),
    \rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.ne_if} (boolean elimination
    $\mathtt{if}\,e\,\mathtt{then}\,M\,\mathtt{else}\,N$ with
    $e : \mathtt{tbool}$, semantically dispatched via the
    \rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.case_em} combinator of
    \autoref{def:cbv-case-em}),
    \rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.ne_score} (term-level score
    by $f(r)$, with $f : \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ measurable and
    pointwise in $[0,1]$, $r$ the value of a
    \texttt{named\_expr}~$\Gamma$~\texttt{tR} --- DIRECT STYLE: returns a
    PURE $\mathtt{tunit}$ expression; the load-bearing constructor for
    genuine Bayesian inference, where the score factor depends on a bound
    variable).
\end{itemize}
There is no separate value/computation syntactic class, no \texttt{vl}
/ \texttt{cp} split, no mutual induction, and no $\mathtt{Ret}$ /
$\mathtt{return}$ syntax. The monadic structure is in the
\emph{interpretation}, not the source.

\paragraph{The interpretation.}
The type interpretation \rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.tyD} sends every
type to a coalgebra of $\mathrm{EM}(\mathord{!})$:
\[
  \begin{array}{r@{\;\;=\;\;}l}
    \sem{\mathtt{tunit}}      & \EMterm \\
    \sem{\mathtt{tbase}\,X}   & \FMeas(X)
        \quad\text{(the Theorem-9.7 coalgebra)} \\
    \sem{\mathtt{tprod}\,t_1\,t_2}
                              & \EMprod\,\sem{t_1}\,\sem{t_2} \\
    \sem{\mathtt{tfun}\,A\,B} &
        \widetilde{\mathord{!}}\,(U\sem{A} \multimap U(T\,\sem{B}))
        \quad\text{(the Kleisli exponential)} \\
    \sem{\mathtt{tbool}}      & \widetilde{\mathord{!}}\,\mathtt{bool\_cone\_car}
        \quad\text{(the cofree coalgebra over \autoref{def:cbv-bool-cone}).}
  \end{array}
\]
The $T = \widetilde{\mathord{!}} \circ U$ on the codomain of
$\sem{\mathtt{tfun}\,A\,B}$ is what makes the source-language function
type a genuine CBV function type: every call is potentially effectful,
and \emph{every} expression's denotation is uniformly a Kleisli arrow
(\rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.eD}):
$\sem{\Gamma \vdash M : t} : \mathtt{coalg\_hom}\,
(\sem{\mathtt{drop\_names}\,\Gamma})\,(T\,\sem{t})$, by structural
recursion \emph{directly} on $\mathtt{named\_expr}$. The pure
constructors ($\mathtt{ne\_var}$, $\mathtt{ne\_tt}$, $\mathtt{ne\_lam}$,
$\mathtt{ne\_real}$, the products) become Kleisli arrows by
post-composition with $\mathtt{tunit\_eta}$ (the implicit return of
direct-style CBV); the sequencer $\mathtt{ne\_let}$ becomes the
extended-context Kleisli bind $\mathtt{kbind\_ext}$
(\rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.eD_let}); the effects $\mathtt{ne\_sample}$
and $\mathtt{ne\_score}$ become the constant and post-composition
arrows of \autoref{lem:ppl-score-tm-lift}. The monad lives uniformly
in $\mathtt{eD}$ and in the $T$ on the codomain of
$\sem{\mathtt{tfun}\,A\,B}$ --- the source language never exposes it.
Contexts are interpreted by \rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.ctxD}
right-to-left with the head on the right, on the name-stripped skeleton
\rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.drop_names}~$\Gamma$; the
$\mathtt{named\_var}$ witness in $\mathtt{ne\_var}$ is projected to
its De Bruijn index by
\rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.named_var_to_has_var} and then dispatched
to \rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.var_lookup}.

\paragraph{Why direct-style CBV is possible at all.}
In older linear-logic / comonad probabilistic models, the function type
$A \to B$ had no non-linear \emph{value-level} interpretation: the
linear function space $A \multimap B$ was the only available exponential,
so call-by-value could only be modelled \emph{monadically} --- function
types had to be Kleisli arrows $A \to T B$, with $\lambda$ and
application mediating computations and the monad surfacing explicitly
at the level of source types (as a $\mathtt{tprob}$ or $T$ marker).
The cones-side EM(!) cartesian structure unlocked by Corollary 20
(\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.EMComon_all}, \autoref{lem:cbv-emcomon-all})
gives a value-CCC --- the \emph{full} cartesian Eilenberg--Moore
category of $\mathord{!}$ (\autoref{thm:cbv-cartesian}) --- and in that
value-CCC the Kleisli exponential
$\widetilde{\mathord{!}}(U A \multimap U(T B))$ is genuinely a value
type, so $\mathtt{tfun}\,A\,B$ interprets as a value type with
$\lambda x.\,M$ a \emph{value} whose body $M$ has type $B$ \emph{at face
value} (not $T B$). This is what direct-style CBV needs and what older
models could not offer; the value-CCC structure discharged in Corollary
20 is the load-bearing structural fact that makes
\texttt{theories/programs/ppl.v}'s direct-style surface --- no
$\mathtt{tprob}$ marker, no $\mathtt{Ret}$, no $\mathtt{ne\_bind}$ ---
possible at all. Concretely, the source-language type
$\mathtt{tfun}\,A\,B$ interprets as the Kleisli exponential
$\widetilde{\mathord{!}}(U A \multimap U(T B))$ ---
\emph{the} source-language function space --- with the $T$ on the
codomain encoding the fact that every function call is potentially
effectful in CBV. The monad is moved out of the \emph{source type} and
into the \emph{interpretation} $\mathtt{eD}$. This is the framing the
paper's outlook gestures at (``in future work we will explain how this
model can be used for interpreting call-by-value\ldots''), and the
direct-style framing the ICones-based PPL inherits.

\paragraph{Lambda, application, monad.}
The function-type interpretation
$\sem{\mathtt{tfun}\,A\,B} = \widetilde{\mathord{!}}(U\sem{A}
\multimap U(T\,\sem{B}))$ is the representing object for
$\mathrm{Hom}_{EM}(- \times A,\, T B)$, via the four-step natural
bijection of \autoref{ch:cbv} (cofree adjunction, $U$ strict
monoidal, SMCC closure of $\ICones$, cofree adjunction again);
$\mathtt{ne\_lam}$ is realised by the curry direction
\rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.lam_coalg} of that chain, and the direct
$\mathtt{ne\_app}$ by the uncurry-and-evaluate direction
\rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.app_kleisli} packaged through
\rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.app_pair}. The direct-style CBV sequencer
$\mathtt{ne\_let}$ uses the \emph{left tensor strength}
\rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.T_str_l} of $T$ to keep the environment, and
is bundled as \rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.kbind_ext}; this is the
direct-style analogue of monadic $\mathtt{bind}$, used by the
\rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.eD_let} reduction. No appeal to a
non-existent cartesian closure of $\mathrm{EM}(\mathord{!})$ is made,
and no ``$\mathrm{ev}$-is-a-coalgebra-morphism'' obligation arises.

\paragraph{The FMeas lax-monoidal map and the arithmetic lifts.}
The interpretation of $\mathtt{ne\_add}$ / $\mathtt{ne\_mul}$ rests on
a genuine lax symmetric-monoidal structure for $\FMeas$ at the
$\ICones$ level (paper \S9), \emph{not} merely a function-level
pushforward: see \autoref{thm:cbv-fmeas-lax}. With its Dirac identity
$\mathtt{fmeas\_lax\_dirac}$ in hand, the arithmetic lifts
\rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.add_lift} and
\rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.mul_lift} are obtained by post-composing
$\mathtt{fmeas\_lax}$ with the $\FMeas$-functorial action of the
measurable $\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$ operation
transported across the carrier cast $R_{\mathrm{obj}} \cong \mathbb{R}$;
their Dirac reductions $\mathtt{add\_lift\_dirac}$ and
$\mathtt{mul\_lift\_dirac}$ are immediate corollaries of
$\mathtt{fmeas\_lax\_dirac}$. The Moggi-Kleisli bind
$\mathtt{bind}(m, k)(\gamma) = \int k(\gamma, a)\,\mathrm{d}m(\gamma)(a)$
then makes the QBS-style ``distribution of $\lambda x.\,m\cdot x + b$
on Dirac inputs'' reading recoverable: the joint pushforward of two
samples followed by the measurable arithmetic is exactly what
$\mathtt{fmeas\_lax} \circ (\mathtt{eD}\,M \otimes \mathtt{eD}\,N)$
implements.

\begin{theorem}[The FMeas lax symmetric monoidal map at \texorpdfstring{$\ICones$}{ICones}]%
\label{thm:cbv-fmeas-lax}
\rocq{Icones.homs.fmeas_lax.fmeas_lax}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.homs.fmeas_lax.fmeas_lax_E}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.homs.fmeas_lax.fmeas_lax_dirac}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.homs.bilin.int_to_linhom_pres_path_in_cone}\rocqok
\uses{thm:cbv-model}
For every $X, Y \in \mathbf{Ar}$ there is an $\ICones$ morphism
$\mathtt{fmeas\_lax}\,X\,Y :
   \FMeas(X) \otimes \FMeas(Y) \;\to\; \FMeas(X \times Y)$
that sends the pure tensor $\mu \otimes \nu$ to the cartesian
product measure $\mu \times \nu$ (\rocq{Icones.homs.fmeas_lax.fmeas_lax_E}),
and so sends Dirac point masses to the Dirac at the paired point,
$\mathtt{fmeas\_lax}\,(\delta_x \otimes \delta_y) = \delta_{(x,y)}$
(\rocq{Icones.homs.fmeas_lax.fmeas_lax_dirac}).
Construction. $\mathtt{fmeas\_lax}$ is obtained by
\rocq{Icones.homs.tensor_construct.tensor_uncurry} of the outer-linhom
$\FMeas(X) \multimap (\FMeas(Y) \multimap \FMeas(X \times Y))$
built by paper Theorem 6.1
(\rocq{Icones.homs.bilin.int_to_linhom}) twice: at the inner level
$\nu \mapsto \mathtt{fmeas\_lax\_pre}\,\delta_x\,\nu$ is a path
into $\FMeas(X \times Y)$ via the Dirac-pushforward path
$\mathtt{dirac\_lax}$, and at the outer level $x \mapsto (\nu \mapsto \cdots)$
is a path into the iCone $\FMeas(Y) \multimap \FMeas(X \times Y)$
of linhoms, which is the
\emph{path-preservation-in-the-cone-variable} step now discharged
as \rocq{Icones.homs.bilin.int_to_linhom_pres_path_in_cone} ---
the paper \S6 follow-up to Theorem 6.1 that the previous header of
\texttt{bilin.v} flagged as deferred.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\rocq{Icones.homs.fmeas_lax.fmeas_lax_E} reduces, on the pure
tensor, to the load-bearing Pettis/Tonelli identity
$\mathtt{fmeas\_lax\_pre\_iterated}$, expressing the pushforward
measure as the iterated icone-integral of $\mathtt{dirac\_lax}$.
The Dirac identity \rocq{Icones.homs.fmeas_lax.fmeas_lax_dirac}
then collapses to the function-level
$\mathtt{fmeas\_lax\_pre\_dirac}$, which is the elementary identity
$\delta_x \times_{\mathrm{meas}} \delta_y =
\delta_{(x,y)}$ along the measurable carrier cast
$\mathtt{ar\_prod\_cast}$. Construction of the outer linhom relies on
\rocq{Icones.homs.bilin.int_to_linhom_pres_path_in_cone}, which is
proved by packaging the cone-variable and the integrand into the
joint state $\mathbf{Ar}(Z) \times \mathbf{Ar}(Y)$ of
\rocq{Icones.icones.icone_integral.icone_integral_joint_measurable}
and specialising via the diagonal $(z, r) \mapsto (z, (z, r))$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{The term-level score lift and its Dirac identity.}
The interpretation of \rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.ne_score} rests on
a lift of the measurable density $f$ into the unit cone, packaged as
an $\ICones$ morphism into the one-dimensional cone $\mathtt{cone\_one\_car}$.
The construction \rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.score_lift} reuses the
paper \S6 follow-up
\rocq{Icones.homs.bilin.int_to_linhom_pres_path_in_cone}
(\autoref{thm:cbv-fmeas-lax}) to promote the path
$r \mapsto f(r) \cdot \mathtt{one1}$ into a $\multimap$-style integral
against $\FMeas(R_{\mathrm{obj}})$. The load-bearing Dirac identity
$\mathtt{score\_lift\_dirac}$ reduces the lift on a Dirac at
$\mathtt{R\_to\_carrier}\,r$ to the scalar
$f(r) \cdot \mathtt{one1}$ --- exactly the packaging that the inner
$\mathtt{ne\_score}$ denotation needs when $r$ comes from a bound
variable through $e$.

\begin{lemma}[The term-level score lift]\label{lem:ppl-score-tm-lift}
\rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.score_lift}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.score_lift_dirac}\rocqok
\uses{thm:cbv-fmeas-lax}
For every measurable $f : \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ with $0 \le f(r) \le 1$
for all $r$, there is an $\ICones$ morphism
$\mathtt{score\_lift}\,f :
   \FMeas(R_{\mathrm{obj}}) \;\to\; \mathtt{cone\_one\_car}$
of operator norm at most one, satisfying the Dirac identity
$\mathtt{Lfun}\,(\mathtt{score\_lift}\,f)\,
   (\delta_{\mathtt{R\_to\_carrier}\,r})
= \mathtt{MkConeOne}\,(\mathtt{NngNum}\,(Hf_{\ge 0}\,r))$,
i.e.\ on a Dirac the lift returns $f(r) \cdot \mathtt{one1}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The path $r \mapsto f(r) \cdot \mathtt{one1}$ is a measurable path
into $\mathtt{cone\_one\_car}$ of norm at most one (pointwise $f(r) \le 1$),
so by paper Theorem 6.1
(\rocq{Icones.homs.bilin.int_to_linhom}) it lifts to an
$\FMeas(R_{\mathrm{obj}}) \multimap \mathtt{cone\_one\_car}$ linhom
$\mathtt{int\_to\_linhom}\,\mathtt{score\_path}$; the
$\ICones$-morphism packaging $\mathtt{linhom\_icones}$ then yields
$\mathtt{score\_lift}$. The Dirac identity follows by
$\mathtt{int\_to\_linhom\_fun\_dirac}$: integration against a Dirac
evaluates the path at the carrier point.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The Bayesian prior/score/observe example]%
\label{lem:ppl-bayes-linear}
\rocq{Icones.programs.examples.ex_bayes_linear}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.examples.ex_bayes_linear_denot_E}\rocqok
\uses{thm:cbv-model, thm:cbv-fmeas-lax, lem:ppl-score-tm-lift}
For $\mu : \FMeas(R_{\mathrm{obj}})$ a unit-ball prior and
$f : \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ a measurable likelihood with
$0 \le f(r) \le 1$ for all $r$ (think a clipped Gaussian
$\mathtt{gaussian\_lik}\,y$ of a fixed observation $y$, restricted
to the unit interval to fit the integrable-cone discipline), the
direct-style PPL term
\[
  \mathtt{ex\_bayes\_linear}
  \;\;\equiv\;\;
  \mathtt{let}\,m = \mathtt{sample}\,\mu\,\mathtt{in}\;
  \mathtt{let}\,\_= \mathtt{score}\,f\,m\,\mathtt{in}\;
  m
  \;\;:\;\;
  \mathtt{tR}
\]
(note the source type $\mathtt{tR}$, not $\mathtt{tprob}\,\mathtt{tR}$:
the program is a real-valued probabilistic computation, the monad is
implicit) exercises \rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.ne_score} on the bound
variable $m$. Its denotation $\mathtt{ex\_bayes\_linear\_denot}$
reduces, by
\rocq{Icones.programs.examples.ex_bayes_linear_denot_E}, to the outer
single-bind shape
\[
  \mathtt{kbind\_ext}\,
    (\mathtt{eD}\,\mathtt{ex\_bl\_cont})\,
    (\mathtt{sample\_kleisli}\,\mu),
\]
where $\mathtt{ex\_bl\_cont} =
\mathtt{let}\,\_= \mathtt{score}\,f\,m\,\mathtt{in}\,m$ is the
score-then-observe continuation in the context
$(\texttt{"m"}, \mathtt{tR}) :: \mathtt{nil}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{remark}[Honest scope note: unnormalised posterior]%
\label{rem:ppl-bayes-unnormalised}
\autoref{lem:ppl-bayes-linear} interprets the
\emph{unnormalised} posterior: the denotation is a sub-probability
measure whose total mass equals $\int f(m)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(m)$. We
do not claim Bayes-optimality --- we claim only the structural
reduction above, which exposes the prior bind, the score's
post-composition with
$\mathtt{bang\_cofree\_hom}\,\mathtt{score\_lift}$, and the
$m$-projection observation. Normalising the posterior would require a
$\mathtt{qbs\_normalize}$-style downstream pass that we have not
introduced; this is, by design, a different observable than the
prior/score/observe reduction recorded here.
\end{remark}

\begin{proof}
Direct-style reduction: two definitional unfoldings of
\rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.eD_let} expose the prior $\mathtt{ne\_let}$
as a strength-equipped \rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.kbind_ext}, and
\rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.eD_sample} unfolds the prior
$\mathtt{ne\_sample}$ to its constant-Kleisli arrow
\rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.sample_kleisli} (no $\mathtt{eD\_ret}$ step:
direct style has no syntactic $\mathtt{return}$). The inner
continuation $\mathtt{ex\_bl\_cont}$ is itself a $\mathtt{kbind\_ext}$
of the score-then-observe shape, which follows by re-applying
\rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.eD_let} and
\rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.eD_score}; the
\rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.score_lift_dirac} identity
(\autoref{lem:ppl-score-tm-lift}) would give a Dirac-input
point-reduction, but the headline asserts only the outer reduction.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Named-variable direct-style surface syntax.}
The named surface is the \emph{only} syntax of
\texttt{theories/programs/ppl.v}: there is no separate De Bruijn
calculus and no intermediate translation. Variable lookup
\texttt{\#~"x"} uses canonical structures
(\rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.find_nv},
\rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.found_nv},
\rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.recurse_nv}, with
\rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.tagged_nctx},
\rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.found_nctx},
\rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.recurse_nctx} carrying the search state
and \texttt{ne\_var'} delivering the resolved
$\mathtt{ne\_var}$ at a $\mathtt{find\_nv}$ witness)
with \mathcompA{}'s \texttt{infer} typeclass on
\texttt{String.eqb}, so Coq's elaborator infers the context slot,
type, and named-variable witness simultaneously; the
bidirectionality hints $\&$ on every binding / context-shared
constructor of
\rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.named_expr} are crucial for the
canonical-structure resolution to fire on the right metavariable
(exactly Saito--Affeldt's \texttt{Arguments exp\_letin \{g\} \&

\texttt{ppl\_named} supports
\texttt{let "x" := M in N} (the direct-style CBV sequencer, desugaring
to \rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.ne_let} --- NOT to a monadic
$\mathtt{ne\_bind}$)
/ \texttt{\textbackslash "x" ::: A => M}
/ \texttt{fix "f" ::: tfun A B in M} (OCaml-style $\mathtt{let\,rec}$,
desugaring to \rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.ne_fix} and semantically
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix.Yfix_fun_T} of \autoref{thm:cbv-yfix-fun})
/ \texttt{Sample (mu, Hmu)}
/ \texttt{Score \{ f, Hf\_meas, Hf\_ge0, Hf\_le1 \} e} (the
term-level Bayesian-score surface form, the \emph{only} score form,
desugaring to \rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.ne_score})
/ \texttt{True} (desugaring to \rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.ne_true})
/ \texttt{False} (desugaring to \rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.ne_false})
/ \texttt{Bernoulli \{ p, Hp\_ge0, Hp\_le1 \}} (the 2-point
sub-probability sample, desugaring to
\rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.ne_bernoulli})
/ \texttt{if e then M else N} (boolean elimination, desugaring to
\rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.ne_if} and semantically dispatched via the
\rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.case_em} combinator of
\autoref{def:cbv-case-em})
/ \texttt{\# "x"} / \texttt{M @ N}
/ \texttt{M + N} / \texttt{M * N} / \texttt{(e1, e2)}
/ \texttt{fst e} / \texttt{snd e} / \texttt{()} / \texttt{[|r|]}
/ \texttt{\{x\}}-escape.
There is \emph{no} \texttt{Ret} surface form --- direct-style CBV has
no syntactic $\mathtt{return}$ at any layer of the source language.
The six examples shipped in \texttt{theories/programs/examples.v} are
all written exclusively in this direct-style surface notation. Three
are QBS-style headlines ---
\rocq{Icones.programs.examples.ex_random_constant},
\rocq{Icones.programs.examples.ex_random_linear} and
\rocq{Icones.programs.examples.ex_bayes_linear} --- with the
reduction lemmas \autoref{lem:ppl-random-constant} /
\autoref{lem:ppl-random-linear} /
\autoref{lem:ppl-bayes-linear}. The other three are the Phase 4
productive partial-termination programs ---
\rocq{Icones.programs.examples.ex_loop},
\rocq{Icones.programs.examples.ex_geom} and
\rocq{Icones.programs.examples.ex_almost_loop} --- with the structural
reduction lemmas \autoref{lem:ppl-ex-loop} /
\autoref{lem:ppl-ex-geom} / \autoref{lem:ppl-ex-almost-loop}. Each
closes by definitional unfolding of $\mathtt{eD}$ on the surface terms.

\paragraph{Genuine out-of-scope items.}
Adequacy, normalization, and full-abstraction (traditional metatheory,
requiring more than a denotational model). Coproducts beyond the
2-point case of \autoref{sec:cbv-bool} (no general sum type in the
cones model). Posterior \emph{normalisation} ---
\autoref{lem:ppl-bayes-linear} interprets the unnormalised posterior
of total mass $\int f(m)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(m)$
(\autoref{rem:ppl-bayes-unnormalised}); the QBS-paper's
\texttt{qbs\_normalize} divide-by-evidence pass is a downstream
observable that we have not introduced. A two-language Moggi
metalanguage with an explicit value/computation split (or any other
explicitly monadic surface) is not in scope either --- the point of
the direct-style refactor is to demonstrate that the cones model
makes such a split unnecessary at the source level.

\section{The CBV mass-one headline:
\texorpdfstring{$\mathtt{ex\_geom}$}{ex\_geom} via the Bang-level
fixpoint}\label{sec:cbv-geom-mass}

\paragraph{This section is beyond the paper.}
The structural reduction \rocq{Icones.programs.examples.ex_geom_denot_E}
of \autoref{lem:ppl-ex-geom} exposed the outer
$\mathtt{kcomp}\,(\mathtt{app\_pair}\,\_\,\_)$ shape of the
denotation of the geometric program, but \emph{did not} claim a closed
form for the $\mathtt{Yfix\_fun\_T}$ iterate. The companion file
\texttt{theories/programs/infra/em\_fix\_arr.v} lifts the CBV fixpoint
through one further layer --- to the Bang-level
$\mathtt{Yfix\_arr}$ --- on which a closed-form $\FMeas$-mass identity
\emph{is} provable, by the same Kleene-cascade $1 - (1/2)^n$ argument
which we replay on the CBN side in \autoref{sec:cbn-geom}. The result
is the CBV-side mass-one identity \rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix_arr.ex_geom_arr_mass_one},
the first formal mass identity for a non-trivial recursive PPL example
in the integrable-cones model.

\begin{theorem}[CBV headline: $\mathtt{ex\_geom}$ has mass one]%
\label{thm:cbv-ex-geom-arr-mass-one}
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix_arr.ex_geom_arr_mass_one}\rocqok
\uses{thm:cbv-yfix-fun, def:cbv-case-em, thm:cbv-fmeas-lax}
The Bang-level recipe operator $\mathtt{Yfix\_arr}$ applied to the
geometric body of $\mathtt{ex\_geom}$ produces a fixpoint in
$\FMeas(\Robj)$ whose total $\overline{\mathbb{R}}$-mass is $1$:
\[
  \texttt{fmeas\_mu}\,
    (\texttt{Yfix\_arr}\,\texttt{ex\_geom\_body})\,
    [\,\mathit{setT}\,]
  \;=\; 1.
\]
This is the CBV-side counterpart of \autoref{thm:cbn-ex-geom} of
\autoref{ch:cbn}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By a Kleene cascade on the unit-ball $\omega$-CPO at the
$\mathtt{Bang}$ level, exactly mirroring
\autoref{lem:cbn-kleene-cascade} on the $\mathbf{SCones}$ side. The
per-iterate mass is $1 - (1/2)^n$ by induction on $n$ (Bernoulli at
$1/2$ pulls $\delta_0$ with weight $1/2$ and recurses on the
remaining $1/2$ through the unary linear pushforward of the ELSE
branch); the limit is $1$ by monotone convergence in
$\overline{\mathbb{R}}$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{The structural relationship to the diagonal bilinear bridge.}
The closure above does \emph{not} use the diagonal bilinear stability
bridge of \autoref{thm:diag-bilin} (\autoref{ch:cbn}) --- it instead
exploits the unary ``shift-by-Dirac'' shape of $\mathtt{ex\_geom}$'s
ELSE branch (a constant Dirac plus the recursive value is a unary
linear map of the recursive value). The bridge remains the structural
unblocker for \emph{generic} CBV recursion at $\mathtt{ne\_fix}$,
the refined CBN $\mathtt{add}/\mathtt{mul}$ install, and any future
bilinear-into-tensor stable combinator.

\paragraph{What is not interpreted, and why none of it is a deferred
milestone.}
$\mathrm{EM}(\mathord{!})$ is \emph{not} cartesian closed and is not
expected to be --- a structural fact about EM categories of
linear-exponential comonads, not a missing diagram chase. The
Kleisli-exponential structure used in \autoref{sec:cbv-ppl} is what
direct-style CBV actually needs, and that holds axiom-free. The
genuinely out-of-scope items are: adequacy, normalization, and
full-abstraction (traditional metatheory, requiring more than a
denotational model); coproducts beyond the 2-point boolean case of
\autoref{sec:cbv-bool} (no general sum type in the cones model); the
QBS-paper's \texttt{qbs\_normalize} / Bayesian-posterior showcase
(a different observable than the one ported here); and a Moggi
metalanguage with an explicit value/computation split (orthogonal to
the direct-style framing the cones model was designed to enable).

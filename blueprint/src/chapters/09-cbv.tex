\chapter{Beyond the paper: a call-by-value model (Melli\`es \S7.4)}%
\label{ch:cbv}

\paragraph{This chapter is beyond the Ehrhard--Geoffroy paper.}
Their \S9 is the LNL/Seely model of intuitionistic linear logic
(Chapter~\ref{ch:exponential}); a \emph{call-by-value} reading is the
paper's explicitly \emph{stated future work} --- the conclusion writes
that ``in future work we will explain how this model can be used for
interpreting call-by-value or even call-by-push-value $\ldots$
languages.'' The development of this chapter takes a step in that
direction, following Melli\`es' \emph{Categorical Semantics of Linear
Logic}~\cite{mellies-categorical-semantics}, \S7.4: the
\emph{Eilenberg--Moore} (coalgebra) route to the exponential gives a
\emph{call-by-value} model, exactly as the co-Kleisli route gives
call-by-name (the latter is the cartesian closed $\mathbf{SCones}$ of
Chapter~\ref{ch:stable}). Nothing here is filed as a paper-\S\ result; it
is a \emph{beyond-the-paper} construction on top of the axiom-free Seely
category of \autoref{thm:seely}.

\paragraph{What is, and is not, done.}
The CBV \emph{model} is built and \textbf{axiom-free} (only the three
classical \texttt{boolp} axioms): the \emph{full} cartesian
Eilenberg--Moore category, the cartesian $\eta$-rule at the icones level
(Fox 1976 / Melli\`es Proposition 28), and the (lax symmetric) monoidal
adjunction \rocq{Icones.programs.infra.cbv_adjunction.ICones_CBV}. The
cartesianness of $\mathrm{EM}(\mathord{!})$ is \emph{unconditional} ---
the predicate \rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.EMComon} holds on every
$\mathord{!}$-coalgebra (\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.EMComon_all}),
following Melli\`es' Proposition 26--28 / Corollary 17/20 by the
structural retraction-and-lifting argument (\emph{not} the naïve
promoted-point reduction, which only computes on $x!$ and stalls on a
general carrier); $U \dashv \widetilde{\mathord{!}}$ is therefore a
\emph{linear / non-linear} adjunction with \emph{the} category of
$\mathord{!}$-coalgebras as the cartesian non-linear / value side. The
\emph{interpreter} of the higher-order PPL is
\texttt{theories/programs/ppl\_cbv.v}, a linhom-valued, comonoid-primitive
denotation on the shared surface inductive \texttt{named\_expr} of
\texttt{theories/programs/ppl.v}. The chapter culminates in the
conditioning / rejection equivalence
(\autoref{thm:cbv-condition-reject}): the Pyro-style soft
conditioning operator denotes the likelihood-reweighted model output
(\autoref{lem:cbv-condition-law}), and the CBV denotation of a
higher-order, probabilistic, possibly non-terminating rejection
sampler over the \emph{same} arbitrary model is identified in closed
form as its normalisation
(\rocq{Icones.programs.ex_reject_model.reject_normalises_condition};
the hard-coded instance is
\rocq{Icones.programs.ex_reject_headline.ex_reject_is_normalised_posterior}),
through the seeded value-fixpoint combinator of
\autoref{sec:cbv-yfix-fun} and the let-at-sample integral law of
\autoref{sec:cbv-laws} --- paired exactly with the unnormalised
posterior of the score program by the marginal identities of
\autoref{ssec:cbv-marginals}.

\paragraph{Linhom-valued, comonoid-primitive interpretation.}
The interpreter assigns to a term
$\Gamma \vdash M : \tau$ a \emph{linear} morphism in $\ICones$,
\[
  \sem{M}\;\;\in\;\;
  \mathrm{linhom}\bigl(U\sem{\Gamma},\,U\sem{\tau}\bigr),
\]
where $\sem{\Gamma}$ and $\sem{\tau}$ are $\mathord{!}$-coalgebras. The
denotation is \emph{not} a coalgebra morphism: a probabilistic program
need not commute with the comonoid duplicability structure carried by
its source coalgebra. The canonical witness is the let-then-pair
fragment $\,\mathtt{let}\,x \,{=}\, \mathtt{sample}\,\mu\,\mathtt{in}\,(x, x)\,$: the
share-then-measure denotation is a pushforward along the diagonal,
whereas the measure-then-pair denotation is the cartesian product
measure; the two distributions agree iff $\mu$ is a Dirac. Programs are
therefore modelled as linear maps and never asked to be coalgebra
morphisms; what every context coalgebra \emph{does} supply uniformly,
through Melli\`es' Proposition 28 / Corollary 20
(\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.EMComon_all}), is a commutative-comonoid
pair $(\delta_\Gamma, \varepsilon_\Gamma) =
(\mathtt{coalg\_d}, \mathtt{coalg\_e})$ on the underlying iCone, and
that pair is the only structural ingredient the interpreter ever uses on
the context side.

The cofree exponential $\widetilde{\mathord{!}}$ now surfaces at
\emph{exactly two} places, both at the function-type interpretation:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.ne_lam} wraps a curried body
    $\mathrm{linhom}(U\sem{\Gamma} \otimes U\sem{A}, U\sem{B})$ ---
    obtained by SMCC currying --- into the cofree $\widetilde{\mathord{!}}$
    using the cofree adjunction's $\mathtt{adj\_psi}$ applied at the
    coalgebra structure of $\sem{\Gamma}$ (caller-side duplicability of
    function values);
  \item \rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.ne_app} extracts a function value from
    its $\widetilde{\mathord{!}}$-wrap via the counit
    $\mathrm{der} : \widetilde{\mathord{!}}L \to L$ on the function-cone
    $L = U\sem{A} \multimap U\sem{B}$ before tensor-uncurrying into
    evaluation.
\end{itemize}
Every other compositional clause uses only the SMC primitives (tensor,
braid, unitors, identity, composition) and the comonoid pair
$(\delta_\Gamma, \varepsilon_\Gamma)$. No Moggi monad
$T = \widetilde{\mathord{!}} \circ U$ ever appears in the codomain of a
type or in the interpretation: there is no $\mathtt{Tobj}$, no
$\mathtt{tunit\_eta}$, no Kleisli composition. The function type
\[
  \sem{\mathtt{tfun}\,A\,B}
    \;=\;
    \widetilde{\mathord{!}}\bigl(U\sem{A} \multimap U\sem{B}\bigr)
\]
carries a \emph{single} cofree $\widetilde{\mathord{!}}$ on the outside;
the codomain is bare.

\paragraph{The shared surface syntax.}
The surface inductive \rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.named_expr} is a
\emph{direct-style} (no value/computation term split, no \texttt{tprob}
type marker, no syntactic \texttt{return}, no syntactic \texttt{bind}),
\emph{single-sort}, \emph{multi-variable}, \emph{named-variable}
probabilistic calculus --- its calculus \emph{shape} mirrors the
canonical higher-order probabilistic calculus of
Heunen--Kammar--Staton--Yang, \emph{A Convenient Category for
Higher-Order Probability Theory} (only the surface-calculus shape is
borrowed; the semantics here is the integrable-cones model) --- in
the style of Saito and Affeldt's APLAS 2023 named-variable
embedding\footnote{Saito and Affeldt, \emph{Towards a practical embedding
of a probabilistic programming language in Coq}, APLAS 2023.}. It is
defined once in \texttt{theories/programs/ppl.v} and consumed by the
linhom-valued CBV interpreter of
\texttt{theories/programs/ppl\_cbv.v} described in this chapter. (A
SCones-valued call-by-name interpretation of the same surface syntax
is preserved on the \texttt{cbn-track} branch; \texttt{main} is
CBV-only.) The surface programs
(\texttt{theories/programs/examples.v}) include
\rocq{Icones.programs.examples.ex_random_constant} (the
distribution-over-a-function-space example),
\rocq{Icones.programs.examples.ex_random_linear} (a distribution over
$\lambda x.\,m\cdot x + b$ for $m, b \sim \mu$), the
prior/score/return example
\rocq{Icones.programs.examples.ex_score_posterior}, the higher-order
Bayesian linear regression
\rocq{Icones.programs.examples.ex_bayes_linear}
(\autoref{ssec:cbv-marginals}), the recursive
\rocq{Icones.programs.examples.ex_loop} / \texttt{ex\_geom} /
\texttt{ex\_almost\_loop}, the mutual-recursion witness
\rocq{Icones.programs.examples.ex_even_odd_pair}, and the
rejection-sampling headline
program \rocq{Icones.programs.examples.ex_reject}
(\autoref{sec:cbv-geom-mass}).

\section{The Seely comonoid on \texorpdfstring{$\mathord{!}A$}{!A} (LC2--LC4)}\label{sec:cbv-comonoid}

$\mathord{!}A$ with the commutative-comonoid structure of a Melli\`es
\emph{linear category} (conditions LC2--LC4 of
\cite{mellies-categorical-semantics}, \S7.4), transported from the
cartesian diagonal / terminal of $(\mathbin{\&}, \top)$ through the Seely
isomorphisms $\mathrm{Seely2}$ / $\mathrm{Seely0}$ of
\autoref{def:seely2}. As throughout \autoref{ch:exponential}, the laws
are diagram chases on promoted points, reduced by the
$\texttt{bang\_ext}$ / $\texttt{tens\_excl\_charact}$ extensionality and
the $\mathrm{Seely2E}$ / $\mathrm{Seely0E}$ computation laws.

$\mathrm{Seely0}^{-1} \circ \mathord{!}(\langle\rangle_A)$ for the
terminal map (\rocq{Icones.homs.em_seely_comonoid.term}). Their
computation laws on a promoted point $x!$ (for $\cnorm{x} \leq 1$) are
$d_A(x!) = x! \otimes x!$ (\rocq{Icones.homs.em_seely_comonoid.d_bang_prom})
and $e_A(x!) = 1$ (\rocq{Icones.homs.em_seely_comonoid.e_bang_prom}).
\end{definition}

\section{The cartesian \texorpdfstring{$\mathrm{EM}(\mathord{!})$}{EM(!)} via the linear \texorpdfstring{$\otimes$}{tensor}}\label{sec:cbv-cartesian}

This section equips the \emph{full} Eilenberg--Moore category
$\mathrm{EM}(\mathord{!})$ with a cartesian structure whose binary
product is carried by the \emph{linear} tensor $\otimes$ (\textbf{not} the
cartesian $\mathbin{\&}$) and whose terminal object is the tensor unit
$1$. This is the hallmark of the EM/CBV reading: in the co-Kleisli (CBN)
category the product is $\mathbin{\&}$, while in the Eilenberg--Moore
(CBV) category the product is $\otimes$. The non-trivial point is that
this works on \emph{every} $\mathord{!}$-coalgebra, not only on a rich
sub-class --- Melli\`es' Proposition 26--28 and Corollary 17/20 ---
proved here by his structural retraction-and-lifting argument.

\begin{definition}[The lax-monoidal map, the product and terminal
coalgebras]\label{def:cbv-prod}
\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.m_bang}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.EM_prod}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.EM_term}\rocqok
\uses{thm:cbv-cofree-adj, lem:cbv-lc34, lem:tens-excl-charact}
The lax symmetric-monoidal structure map of $\mathord{!}$ is
$m_{A,B} : \mathord{!}A \otimes \mathord{!}B \lin \mathord{!}(A \otimes B)$
(\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.m_bang}), with
$m(x! \otimes y!) = (x \otimes y)!$
(\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.m_bang_prom}); it is a coalgebra morphism
(\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.m_bang_is_coalg_mor}). The product
coalgebra is $(A \otimes B,\ m \circ (a \otimes b))$
(\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.EM_prod}) and the terminal coalgebra is
$(1, \cdot)$ (\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.EM_term}, the
$\texttt{unit\_cofree}$ coalgebra). The Eq-88 transported comonoid maps
on \emph{every} coalgebra are
\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.coalg_d} /
\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.coalg_e}; these definitions are general,
but their laws are per-object (next).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[The comonoidality predicate $\mathtt{EMComon}$]%
\label{def:cbv-comon}
\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.EMComon}\rocqok
\uses{def:cbv-prod}
A coalgebra $P$ is \emph{comonoidal} ---
\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.EMComon} holds of it --- when the
transported maps \rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.coalg_d} /
\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.coalg_e} make its carrier a commutative
comonoid \emph{and} are coalgebra morphisms (Melli\`es' Propositions 26 /
27 and Corollary 20). This is the per-object property the cartesian
construction needs --- and it turns out to hold on every coalgebra
(\autoref{lem:cbv-emcomon-all}).
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[$\mathtt{EMComon}$ is unconditional --- Melli\`es
Proposition 26--28]\label{lem:cbv-emcomon-all}
\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.EMComon_all}\rocqok
\uses{def:cbv-comon, lem:cbv-lc2, lem:cbv-lc34}
For \emph{every} $\mathord{!}$-coalgebra $P = (A, a)$, the transported
maps \rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.coalg_d} /
\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.coalg_e} satisfy all the comonoid /
coalgebra-morphism laws --- i.e.\ \rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.EMComon_all}.
The cofree coalgebras $\widetilde{\mathord{!}}B$
(\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.EMComon_cofree}) and the Theorem-9.7
coalgebras $\FMeas(X)$
(\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.EMComon_FMeas}) are corollaries.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The naïve approach --- reduce everything to \emph{promoted points} via
\rocq{Icones.homs.em_seely_comonoid.d_bang_prom} --- cannot work for a
general $P = (A, a)$, because an image $a\,x$ is not promoted: the
defining computation of $d_A$ on $x!$ does not apply, and the laws fail
to reduce. We follow Melli\`es' \emph{structural} proof instead, a
retraction-and-lifting argument that never computes on points.

The first ingredient is that every coalgebra $P = (A, a)$ is a
\emph{retract} of its cofree coalgebra $\widetilde{\mathord{!}}\,UA$
inside $\mathrm{EM}(\mathord{!})$ (Melli\`es' Proposition 26): the
structure map $a : P \to \widetilde{\mathord{!}}\,UA$ is a coalgebra
morphism by the coassociativity of $P$, and the counit
$\varepsilon = \mathrm{der}_A$ retracts it
($\mathrm{der}_A \circ a = \mathrm{id}_A$ is the coalgebra counit law).
\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.diagram81} records the matching square
$(a \otimes a) \circ \mathrm{coalg\_d}\,P = d_{\mathord{!}A} \circ a$
on the nose --- not by point computation but by instantiating the
\emph{generally} valid \rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.coalg_mor_d} at
the coalgebra morphism $a$, then folding through
\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.coalg_d_cofree}.

The second ingredient is that retracts of commutative comonoids are
commutative comonoids (Proposition 27). The four laws --- counit, left
counit, cocommutativity, coassociativity --- are transported along the
retraction by generic SMC cancellation
(\texttt{lcancel\_mono}) combined with the naturality of the associator,
braiding, and unitors against Diagram (85)
(\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.diagram81} above) and the cofree
comonoid laws on $\widetilde{\mathord{!}}\,UA$
(\autoref{lem:cbv-lc2}).

The hard step is showing the transported diagonal $\mathrm{coalg\_d}\,P$
is itself a coalgebra morphism into the product coalgebra $P \otimes P$
--- Melli\`es' Corollary 20, the one he flags as ``not so immediate''.
We package it as a generic \emph{lifting lemma}
\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.coalg_mor_lift} (Melli\`es' \S6.11
Proposition 20): if $i : X \to Y$ is a coalgebra morphism with a
retraction $r$ on the underlying carrier, and a candidate $f$ satisfies
$i \circ f$ is a coalgebra morphism, then $f$ is a coalgebra morphism.
Instantiated at $i = a \otimes a$ (a coalgebra morphism with retraction
$\mathrm{der} \otimes \mathrm{der}$) and $f = \mathrm{coalg\_d}\,P$, the
hypothesis $i \circ f = d_{\mathord{!}A} \circ a$ is exactly
\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.diagram81}, and a composite of two
coalgebra morphisms ($d_{\mathord{!}A}$ on the cofree side from
\autoref{lem:cbv-lc34}, and $a$); hence
$\mathrm{coalg\_d}\,P : P \to P \otimes P$ is a coalgebra morphism
(\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.coalg_d_is_mor_gen}). The easy half for
the counit is a composite of coalgebra morphisms
(\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.coalg_e_is_mor_gen}). Bundling the
four comonoid laws and the two coalgebra-morphism facts gives
\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.EMComon_all}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[$\mathrm{EM}(\mathord{!})$ is cartesian]%
\label{thm:cbv-cartesian}
\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.ICones_EM_cartesian}\rocqok
\uses{def:cbv-prod, lem:cbv-emcomon-all, def:cbv-em-cat}
The linear tensor $\otimes$ is the categorical product of the full
$\mathrm{EM}(\mathord{!})$ and the tensor unit $1$ is terminal. The
data --- projections \rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.em_proj1_mor} /
\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.em_proj2_mor}, pairing
\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.em_pair} (no $\mathtt{EMComon}$
hypothesis needed, by \autoref{lem:cbv-emcomon-all}), the
$\beta$-laws \rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.em_proj1_pair} /
\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.em_proj2_pair}, and the terminal
universal property \rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.em_term_mor} /
\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.em_term_unique} --- is bundled in the
record \rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.EM_Cartesian} with witness
\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.ICones_EM_cartesian} (axiom-free), whose
product/terminal field types reference the \emph{linear} $\otimes$, not
$\mathbin{\&}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Each coalgebra $P$ is comonoidal by \autoref{lem:cbv-emcomon-all}, so
Melli\`es' Corollary 17 applies: the projections, diagonal and pairing
out of $P$ are built from the comonoid data of $P$, $Q$. The
$\beta$-laws and terminal uniqueness follow from the comonoid laws of
\autoref{lem:cbv-lc2} as transported by
\autoref{lem:cbv-emcomon-all}.
\end{proof}

\section{The cartesian \texorpdfstring{$\eta$}{eta}-rule on
\texorpdfstring{$\mathrm{EM}(\mathord{!})$}{EM(!)} (Fox 1976 /
Melli\`es Prop 28)}\label{sec:cbv-cart-eta}

The $\beta$-laws \rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.em_proj1_pair} /
\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.em_proj2_pair} of
\autoref{thm:cbv-cartesian} establish the universal property of the
binary product \emph{out of} an arbitrary coalgebra $Z$. To complete the
cartesian structure we also need the $\eta$-rule
$\langle \pi_1, \pi_2 \rangle = \mathrm{id}$ at the linear level
$\ICones$. This is genuinely additional content --- Fox's 1976 theorem
that the EM category of a linear-exponential comonad is cartesian
under the linear tensor (Melli\`es Proposition 28 at the icones
level). The proof follows the same retract-and-lift recipe as
\autoref{lem:cbv-emcomon-all}: reduce to the cofree case, then transport
along the retraction.

\begin{lemma}[$\eta$-rule, cofree case]\label{lem:cbv-cart-eta-cofree}
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.cbv_adjunction.em_pair_mor_proj_id_cofree}\rocqok
\uses{thm:cbv-cartesian, lem:tens-excl-charact}
For every $A, B : \ICones$, the pair-of-projections on the cofree
product equals the identity at the icones level:
\[
  \mathtt{em\_pair\_mor}\;(\pi_1)\;(\pi_2)
  \;=\;
  \mathrm{id}_{\mathord{!}A \otimes \mathord{!}B}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By \rocq{Icones.homs.em_seely_comonoid.tens_excl_charact}, it suffices
to check both sides agree on the promoted pure tensor $x! \otimes y!$
for $\cnorm{x} \leq 1$, $\cnorm{y} \leq 1$. On the cofree pair the
diagonal $\mathtt{coalg\_d}$ unfolds via
\rocq{Icones.homs.em_seely_comonoid.d_bang_prom} /
\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.m_bang_prom} /
\rocq{Icones.homs.bang.dig_prom} to
$(x! \otimes y!) \otimes (x! \otimes y!)$, and the projections
\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.em_proj1_mor} /
\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.em_proj2_mor} reduce on
$x! \otimes y!$ via the unitor + counit + \rocq{Icones.homs.em_seely_comonoid.e_bang_prom}
to $x!$ / $y!$ respectively.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The cartesian $\eta$-rule
$\langle \pi_1, \pi_2 \rangle = \mathrm{id}$]\label{thm:cbv-cart-eta}
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.cbv_adjunction.em_pair_mor_proj_id}\rocqok
\uses{lem:cbv-cart-eta-cofree, lem:cbv-emcomon-all}
For every pair $(P, Q)$ of $\mathord{!}$-coalgebras, the
pair-of-projections of the binary product $P \otimes Q$ in
$\mathrm{EM}(\mathord{!})$ equals the identity on the underlying carrier:
\[
  \mathtt{em\_pair\_mor}\;(\pi_1)\;(\pi_2)
  \;=\;
  \mathrm{id}_{cP \otimes cQ}
  \quad : \quad
  \mathrm{Hom}_{\ICones}(cP \otimes cQ,\, cP \otimes cQ).
\]
Combined with the $\beta$-laws of \autoref{thm:cbv-cartesian}, this
completes the universal property of the EM(!) binary product
$(P \otimes Q,\, \pi_1,\, \pi_2,\, \langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The structure maps
$(\mathtt{coalg\_str}\,P,\,\mathtt{coalg\_str}\,Q) : (P, Q) \to
(\widetilde{\mathord{!}}\,cP,\,\widetilde{\mathord{!}}\,cQ)$
are coalgebra morphisms (the coassociativity of $P$ and $Q$), and the
counit $\mathrm{der}_{cP} \otimes \mathrm{der}_{cQ}$ on the carrier
side retracts them ($\mathrm{der}_{cA} \circ a = \mathrm{id}_{A}$ is
the coalgebra counit). The tensored
$\mathtt{coalg\_str}\,P \otimes \mathtt{coalg\_str}\,Q$ on
$cP \otimes cQ$ is therefore a split mono with retraction
$\mathrm{der}_{cP} \otimes \mathrm{der}_{cQ}$. The $\eta$-rule on
$(P, Q)$ then follows from the cofree case
(\autoref{lem:cbv-cart-eta-cofree}) by naturality of $\pi_1, \pi_2$
along this retraction: $\langle \pi_1, \pi_2 \rangle$ at $(P, Q)$ is
the transport of $\langle \pi_1, \pi_2 \rangle$ at the cofree pair,
which equals the identity by \autoref{lem:cbv-cart-eta-cofree}, and the
retraction transports identities to identities.
\end{proof}

(\autoref{lem:cbv-emcomon-all}), so $U \dashv \widetilde{\mathord{!}}$
is a genuine linear / non-linear adjunction with the (full) category of
$\mathord{!}$-coalgebras as the cartesian non-linear / value side.
\end{proof}

\section{From the Moggi-CBV monad to the linhom-valued reading}%
\label{sec:cbv-calculus}

An earlier iteration of the formalisation accompanied
\autoref{thm:cbv-model} with a small first-order Moggi-CBV calculus
(intrinsically-typed Rocq inductives for unit, a measurable base
$\base\,X$, and binary products; values, computations
$\creturn V$ / $\clet x = M \mathbin{\mathtt{in}} N$ / $\csample V$),
interpreted into the CBV monad $T = \widetilde{\mathord{!}} \circ U$
of \autoref{thm:cbv-cofree-adj} with its Kleisli composition and
its $\mathtt{tunit\_eta}$ unit. That presentation has been retired:
the file \texttt{theories/programs/cbv.v} (which carried
$\mathtt{Tobj}$, $\mathtt{tunit\_eta}$, $\mathtt{kcomp}$, $\mathtt{kbind\_ext}$
and the three monad laws) is no longer part of the codebase, subsumed
by the linhom-valued, comonoid-primitive reading of
\autoref{sec:cbv-ppl}.

The two presentations agree on the underlying \emph{denotation} of
every closed term of the first-order calculus, but differ on the
\emph{shape} of the recursion. The Kleisli-monad reading interprets
every term as a coalgebra morphism into $T\,\sem{\tau}$ and recovers
identification rules ($\mathtt{let}$/$\mathtt{return}$, sample = the
integral) as monad-equational consequences. The linhom-valued reading
interprets every term as a \emph{linear} morphism
$U\sem{\Gamma} \to U\sem{\tau}$ (with no $T$ on the codomain) and
realises the sequencer $\mathtt{ne\_let}$, the pairing $\mathtt{ne\_pair}$
and the application $\mathtt{ne\_app}$ from the comonoid pair
$(\delta_\Gamma, \varepsilon_\Gamma)$ of every context coalgebra. The
$\mathord{!}$-induced monad on a measure type collapses the
$\csample V$ vs $\creturn V$ identification automatically: at
$\base\,X \mapsto \FMeas(X)$ the underlying iCone already carries
the Dirac inclusion, so a probabilistic sample from a Dirac and the
implicit return of its scalar value share the same linear morphism into
$\FMeas(X)$. The first-order calculus is therefore subsumed by the
higher-order linhom-valued interpreter of \autoref{sec:cbv-ppl}, and
no separate Moggi-CBV soundness theorem is recorded.

\section{The CBV value-fixpoint: degeneracy of the zero-seeded
operator, and the seeded combinator $\mathtt{fix\_comb}$}%
\label{sec:cbv-yfix-fun}

The CBV interpreter resolves $\mathtt{ne\_fix}$ (and
$\mathtt{ne\_fix\_mr}$ at \emph{every} free body type --- directly at
function types, through the Seely transport of
\autoref{ssec:cbv-fix-mr} at products of free types) to the
value-fixpoint \emph{combinator}
\[
  \mathtt{fix\_comb}
  \;:\;
  \widetilde{\mathord{!}}\bigl(\mathord{!}L \multimap \mathord{!}L\bigr)
  \;\longrightarrow\;
  \widetilde{\mathord{!}}L
  \qquad\text{in } \mathrm{EM}(\mathord{!}),
\]
built in \texttt{theories/programs/infra/em\_fix\_value.v}, where
$L = U\sem{A} \multimap U\sem{B}$ is the function-value cone of
$\sem{\mathtt{tfun}\,A\,B} = \widetilde{\mathord{!}}L$. This section
records three things. First, the \emph{degeneracy} of the naive
zero-seeded iteration: the Kleene step of
\texttt{theories/programs/infra/em\_fix.v} is linear in the previous
approximant, so seeding its chain at the cone-zero of the linhom cone
makes every iterate --- and the supremum --- the zero linhom
(\autoref{thm:cbv-yfix-degenerate}; the operator this iteration used
to define has been removed from \texttt{em\_fix.v}). The bottom of a CBV
function-\emph{value} type is not the cone-zero of $\mathord{!}L$; it is
the promoted zero $0^!$, the diverging-function value (of
$e$-mass one). Second, the repaired combinator: an \emph{interleaved}
Kleene chain through the $\mathrm{prom}$/$\mathrm{der}$ pair, seeded at
the genuine bottom $0$ of the value cone, whose supremum is a
\emph{stable} map by assembly from existing $\mathbf{SCones}$
morphisms (the \S9.2 $\mathrm{Yfix}$ of \autoref{lem:yfix},
the CCC structure of \autoref{thm:scones-ccc}, and the
linear-to-stable lift), and which the $\mathrm{lin}$ hom-bijection of
the SAFT $\mathord{!}$ plus the $\mathtt{adj\_psi}$ of the cofree
adjunction (\autoref{thm:cbv-cofree-adj}) package into an
Eilenberg--Moore morphism --- coalgebra-morphism-ness is by
construction. The CBV recursion thereby rests on the paper-\S9.2
stable-fixpoint core: the chain supremum of $\mathtt{fix\_comb}$ is
computed through the $\mathrm{Yfix}$ of \autoref{lem:yfix}. Third,
the \emph{Seely transport} for mutual recursion
(\autoref{ssec:cbv-fix-mr}): a product of free types is not
literally cofree, but it is isomorphic to a cofree coalgebra through
the EM-level Seely-2 iso, and conjugating $\mathtt{fix\_comb}$ by
that iso makes the $\mathtt{ne\_fix\_mr}$ fixpoint genuine at every
free body type.

\subsection{The naive linear Kleene step $\mathtt{Phi\_fun}$}%
\label{ssec:cbv-phifun}

We first record the step whose zero-seeded iteration is the degeneracy
contrast (the operator that iteration used to define has been removed
from \texttt{em\_fix.v}; its last consumer, the
$\mathtt{ne\_fix\_mr}$-at-$\mathtt{tprod}$ clause, now goes through
the genuine Seely transport of \autoref{ssec:cbv-fix-mr}). The step
operator works at the linhom
cone $\mathrm{linhom}_\Ar(\Gamma,\,B)$, parametric in any icone-level
diagonal $\delta$ on $\Gamma$; the codomain enters only through the
body morphism $M : \Gamma \otimes B \to B$.

\begin{definition}[$\mathtt{Phi\_fun}$ --- the CBV Kleene step]%
\label{def:cbv-phi-fun}
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix.Phi_fun}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix.Phi_fun_safe}\rocqok
\uses{thm:cbv-cartesian, def:cbv-prod}
For cones $\Gamma, B : \ICones$, a diagonal
$\delta : \Gamma \to \Gamma \otimes \Gamma$ and a body
$M : \Gamma \otimes B \to B$ (both packaged as $\ICones$ morphisms), the
step operator
\[
  \mathtt{Phi\_fun}\,M\,\mathit{prev}
  \;=\;
  M
    \;\circ\;
    \mathtt{tensor\_mor}\,(\mathrm{id}_\Gamma,\,\mathit{prev})
    \;\circ\;
    \delta
  \;\;:\;\;
  \Gamma \to B
\]
takes a previous approximant $\mathit{prev} : \Gamma \to B$ in the unit
ball of $\mathrm{linhom}_\Ar(\Gamma, B)$, duplicates the context through
$\delta$, runs the approximant on the second copy of $\Gamma$, and
applies the body. The total
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix.Phi_fun} is defined on all of
$\mathrm{linhom}_\Ar(\Gamma, B)$ via $\mathtt{pselect}$: it agrees with the
safe variant
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix.Phi_fun_safe} on the unit ball, and
returns $\mathtt{precone\_zero}$ off it (irrelevant for the Kleene
chain, which lives in the unit ball;
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix.Phi_fun_unit}).
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Ball preservation, monotonicity, and $\omega$-continuity
of $\mathtt{Phi\_fun}$]\label{lem:cbv-phi-fun-laws}
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix.Phi_fun_ball}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix.Phi_fun_incr}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix.Phi_fun_omega_cont}\rocqok
\uses{def:cbv-phi-fun}
On the unit ball,
$\mathtt{Phi\_fun}$ preserves the ball
(\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix.Phi_fun_ball}: if
$\cnorm{\mathit{prev}} \leq 1$ then
$\cnorm{\mathtt{Phi\_fun}\,M\,\mathit{prev}} \leq 1$, by composing the
three layer-norm bounds), is monotone
(\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix.Phi_fun_incr}: a precone-order
inequality $\mathit{prev}_1 \leq \mathit{prev}_2$ in the unit ball
transports through the three linear layers via
\rocq{Icones.cones.basic_lemmas.linear_increasing} and the layer-level
monotonicity witnesses, in particular
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix.tensor_mor_R_lin_incr}), and is
$\omega$-continuous on the unit ball
(\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix.Phi_fun_omega_cont}: it commutes with the
unit-ball supremum
\rocq{Icones.cones.cone.cone_sup_ball} of a unit-ball
chain of approximants, through the three layer-level continuity
witnesses
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix.tensor_mor_omega_cont_R} on the
tensor layer,
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix.linhom_pre_icones_sup} on the
$\delta$ pre-composition, and
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix.linhom_post_icones_sup} on the $M$
post-composition).
\end{lemma}

\subsection{The naive zero-seeded Kleene supremum is degenerate}%
\label{ssec:cbv-yfix-sup}

The three laws of \autoref{lem:cbv-phi-fun-laws} make the unit-ball
$\omega$-CPO of $\mathrm{linhom}_\Ar(\Gamma, B)$ exactly the data the
generic linhom-level Kleene engine
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix.linhom_lfp} needs.

\begin{definition}[The linhom-level Kleene chain
$\mathtt{kleene\_lin}$ and its supremum
$\mathtt{linhom\_lfp}$]\label{def:cbv-kleene-lin}
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix.kleene_lin}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix.linhom_lfp}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix.linhom_lfp_norm_le1}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix.linhom_lfp_fixpoint}\rocqok
\uses{lem:cbv-phi-fun-laws}
For any endo-operator $\Phi$ on $\mathrm{linhom}_\Ar(C, D)$ satisfying
the three laws of \autoref{lem:cbv-phi-fun-laws} (ball preservation,
monotonicity, $\omega$-continuity), the Kleene iterates
$\mathtt{kleene\_lin}\,\Phi\,n = \Phi^n(\mathtt{precone\_zero})$
form a chain in the unit ball
(\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix.kleene_lin_chain},
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix.kleene_lin_ball}); their
unit-ball supremum is the least fixpoint
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix.linhom_lfp}, with norm at most one
(\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix.linhom_lfp_norm_le1}) and the
fixpoint equation
$\Phi\,(\mathtt{linhom\_lfp}\,\Phi) = \mathtt{linhom\_lfp}\,\Phi$
(\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix.linhom_lfp_fixpoint}). This mirrors,
at the linhom cone, the SCones-side \rocq{Icones.stable.fixpoint.lfp} /
\rocq{Icones.stable.fixpoint.Yfix_fix} of paper \S9.2
(\autoref{lem:yfix}).
\end{definition}

Instantiating the engine at the step $\mathtt{Phi\_fun}\,M$ of
\autoref{def:cbv-phi-fun} yields the naive zero-seeded CBV
value-fixpoint candidate
$\sup_n (\mathtt{Phi\_fun}\,M)^n\,\mathtt{precone\_zero}$ --- the
operator that used to be \texttt{em\_fix.v}'s CBV value-fixpoint. Its
ball bound and Kleene fixpoint equation (instances of
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix.linhom_lfp_norm_le1} and
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix.linhom_lfp_fixpoint}) are correct
but vacuous, as the next theorem shows: the iteration never produces
anything but zero. The operator was removed after the degeneracy
proof; the record survives as the linearity content
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix_value.Phi_fun_zero} plus the
generic least-fixpoint collapse lemma
\rocq{Icones.stable.fixpoint.lfp_eq0}.

\begin{theorem}[Degeneracy: the naive zero-seeded iteration is $0$,
always]%
\label{thm:cbv-yfix-degenerate}
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix_value.Phi_fun_lfp_eq0}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix_value.Phi_fun_zero}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix_value.kleene_lin_Phi_fun_eq0}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.stable.fixpoint.lfp_eq0}\rocqok
\uses{def:cbv-phi-fun, def:cbv-kleene-lin}
For \emph{every} diagonal $\delta$ and \emph{every} body $M$,
\[
  \mathtt{linhom\_lfp}\,(\mathtt{Phi\_fun}\,M)
  \;=\;
  \mathtt{precone\_zero}
  \quad : \quad
  \mathrm{linhom}_\Ar(\Gamma, B).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The step $\mathtt{Phi\_fun}\,M$ is \emph{linear} in the previous
approximant: each of its three layers ($\delta$ pre-composition,
$\mathrm{id}_\Gamma \otimes \mathit{prev}$, $M$ post-composition) is a
linear map of the approximant. In particular it preserves the cone-zero:
$\mathrm{id}_\Gamma \otimes 0 = 0$ at the linhom level
(\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix_value.tensor_mor_R_lin_zero}, by
linearity of the pure-tensor map in its right argument), hence
$\mathtt{Phi\_fun}\,M\,0 = 0$
(\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix_value.Phi_fun_zero}). The Kleene
chain is seeded at $\mathtt{precone\_zero}$, so by induction every
iterate is zero
(\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix_value.kleene_lin_Phi_fun_eq0}), and
the supremum of the constantly-zero chain is zero --- the collapse is
the generic lemma \rocq{Icones.stable.fixpoint.lfp_eq0}: any Kleene
step that preserves the zero seed has zero least fixpoint.
\end{proof}

The diagnosis: the bottom of a CBV function-\emph{value} type is not
the cone-zero of $\mathord{!}L$ but the \emph{promoted} zero $0^!$ ---
the diverging-function value, a unit-ball point of $e$-mass one
($e(x^!) = 1$, \autoref{def:cbv-d-e}) --- and a productive fixpoint
iteration must pass through the analytic (non-linear) promotion
$\mathrm{prom} = \mathtt{nl}$, which a step that is linear in the
approximant never does. The repaired combinator of the following
subsections does exactly that.

\subsection{The seeded Kleene core}%
\label{ssec:cbv-seeded-kleene}

\begin{definition}[Seeded Kleene chain and supremum]%
\label{def:cbv-kleene-from}
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix_value.kleene_from}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix_value.lfp_from}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix_value.lfp_from_fixpoint}\rocqok
\uses{def:cbv-kleene-lin}
On any cone $B$, given $f : B \to B$ and a seed $b_0 \in B$ with $f$
monotone and ball-preserving on the unit ball, $\cnorm{b_0} \leq 1$ and
$b_0 \leq f(b_0)$, the seeded chain is
$\mathtt{kleene\_from}\,f\,b_0\,n = f^n(b_0)$ --- a unit-ball chain
(\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix_value.kleene_from_chain},
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix_value.kleene_from_ball}) --- with
unit-ball supremum
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix_value.lfp_from}; if $f$ is moreover
$\omega$-continuous, the supremum is a fixpoint
(\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix_value.lfp_from_fixpoint}). The
hypothesis $b_0 \leq f(b_0)$ replaces the $\mathtt{precone\_le0}$ base
of the zero-seeded chains (\autoref{def:cbv-kleene-lin},
\rocq{Icones.stable.fixpoint.lfp}); it is what admits the promoted-zero
seed $0^!$ of the coalgebraic-case simplification
(\autoref{thm:cbv-fix-coalg}).
\end{definition}

\subsection{The interleaved chain}%
\label{ssec:cbv-fix-chain}

Fix an iCone $A$ and write $\mathord{!}A$ for its SAFT exponential and
$LL = \mathord{!}A \multimap \mathord{!}A$ for the cone of linear
endo-bodies.

\begin{definition}[The interleaved Kleene chain $\mathtt{fix\_chain}$]%
\label{def:cbv-fix-chain}
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix_value.fix_chain}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix_value.fix_chain_S}\rocqok
\uses{def:cbv-kleene-from, def:struct-maps}
For a body $F \in LL$, the interleaved chain in $A$ is
\[
  x_0 = 0,
  \qquad
  x_{n+1} = \mathrm{der}\bigl(F(x_n^!)\bigr),
\]
with $\mathtt{fix\_chain}\,F\,n = x_n$ and per-iterate access law
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix_value.fix_chain_S}. Each step
promotes the previous \emph{value} approximant, runs the body once,
and derelicts back to a value.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Ball preservation and monotonicity of the interleaved
chain]\label{lem:cbv-fix-chain-laws}
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix_value.fix_chain_ball}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix_value.fix_chain_chain}\rocqok
\uses{def:cbv-fix-chain}
For any $F$ in the unit ball of $LL$, the chain lives in the unit ball
of $A$ (\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix_value.fix_chain_ball}) and
is increasing
(\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix_value.fix_chain_chain}).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The step $x \mapsto \mathrm{der}(F(x^!))$ is monotone on the unit ball:
$\mathrm{prom} = \mathtt{nl}$ is totally monotone (analytic,
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix_value.prom_incr}), and $F$ and
$\mathrm{der}$ are linear, hence increasing
(\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix_value.fix_step_incr}). The base
case is $0 \leq x_1$. This $\mathrm{prom}\circ\mathrm{der}$ sandwich is
the point of the construction: the iteration starts at the genuine
bottom $0$ of the \emph{value} cone $A$, and productivity does not
require the body $F$ to be a coalgebra morphism --- monotonicity comes
from the analytic $\mathrm{prom}$, for \emph{any} unit-ball linear
body.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Stability by assembly: the value map $\mathtt{fix\_value}$}%
\label{ssec:cbv-fix-value}

The assignment $F \mapsto \sup_n x_n$ must be shown to extend to a
genuine morphism out of $\mathord{!}LL$. Rather than re-proving
stability of the chain supremum by hand, the map is \emph{assembled}
from existing $\mathbf{SCones}$ morphisms, so total monotonicity,
$\omega$-continuity and path-measurability come for free.

\begin{definition}[The stable body and the value map]%
\label{def:cbv-fix-value}
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix_value.fix_lts}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix_value.fix_step}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix_value.fix_body}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix_value.fix_value}\rocqok
\uses{lem:cbv-fix-chain-laws, thm:scones-ccc, lem:yfix}
In $\mathbf{SCones}$:
\begin{itemize}
  \item $\mathtt{fix\_lts} : LL \to (\mathord{!}A \Rightarrow_s A)$
    sends $F$ to the linear-to-stable lift of $\mathrm{der} \circ F$
    (\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix_value.fix_lts_fun}, via
    \rocq{Icones.stable.diag_bilinear_tensor.linhom_to_stablehom});
  \item $\mathtt{fix\_step} : LL \times A \to A$ is
    $\mathrm{Ev} \circ \langle \mathtt{fix\_lts} \circ \pi_1,\
    \mathtt{nl} \circ \pi_2 \rangle$ --- on unit-ball points it
    computes $(F, x) \mapsto \mathrm{der}(F(x^!))$
    (\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix_value.fix_step_E});
  \item $\mathtt{fix\_body} = \mathrm{curry}\,\mathtt{fix\_step} :
    LL \to (A \Rightarrow_s A)$, the CCC currying of
    \autoref{thm:scones-ccc};
  \item $\mathtt{fix\_value} = \mathrm{Yfix}_A \circ \mathtt{fix\_body}
    : LL \to A$, post-composing with the \S9.2 stable fixpoint operator
    \rocq{Icones.stable.fixpoint.Yfix} of \autoref{lem:yfix}.
\end{itemize}
$\mathtt{fix\_value}$ is a single $\mathbf{SCones}$ morphism
(\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix_value.fix_value}), so it is a
\emph{stable} map by construction.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Computation and unfolding of the value map]%
\label{thm:cbv-fix-value}
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix_value.Yfix_kleeneE}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix_value.fix_value_E}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix_value.fix_value_unfold}\rocqok
\uses{def:cbv-fix-value, lem:cbv-fix-chain-laws}
For unit-ball $F$:
\[
  \mathtt{fix\_value}\,F \;=\; \sup_n\,\mathtt{fix\_chain}\,F\,n
  \qquad\text{and}\qquad
  \mathrm{der}\bigl(F\bigl((\mathtt{fix\_value}\,F)^!\bigr)\bigr)
  \;=\; \mathtt{fix\_value}\,F.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The identification
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix_value.Yfix_kleeneE} first computes
the \S9.2 $\mathrm{Yfix}$ at a unit-ball $f : A \Rightarrow_s A$ as the
supremum of the plain Kleene chain $n \mapsto f^n(0)$: evaluation at
$f$ is a linear $\omega$-continuous map on the stablehom cone, so it
passes through the $\mathtt{sfix}$ supremum of the
$Z$-combinator iterates. Instantiating $f = \mathtt{fix\_body}\,F$ and
unfolding \rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix_value.fix_step_E}
identifies that plain chain with the interleaved chain
(\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix_value.fix_value_E}); the unfolding
equation \rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix_value.fix_value_unfold} is
the $\mathrm{Yfix}$ fixpoint law
\rocq{Icones.stable.fixpoint.Yfix_fix} read through the same
computation.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The Eilenberg--Moore packaging:
$\mathtt{fix\_lin}$ and $\mathtt{fix\_comb}$}%
\label{ssec:cbv-fix-comb}

\begin{definition}[The linear extension $\mathtt{fix\_lin}$]%
\label{def:cbv-fix-lin}
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix_value.fix_lin}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix_value.fix_lin_promE}\rocqok
\uses{def:cbv-fix-value, def:struct-maps}
A stable map $LL \to A$ \emph{is} a linear map
$\mathord{!}LL \multimap A$ through the $\mathrm{lin}/\Theta$
hom-bijection of the SAFT construction of $\mathord{!}$
(\texttt{homs/bang.v}); set
$\mathtt{fix\_lin} = \mathrm{lin}\,\mathtt{fix\_value} :
\mathord{!}LL \to A$ in $\ICones$. On promoted points it computes the
value map: $\mathtt{fix\_lin}(F^!) = \mathtt{fix\_value}\,F$
(\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix_value.fix_lin_promE}). No fresh
order analysis of the SAFT $\mathord{!}$ is needed: the linear
extension is the universal property.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[The combinator $\mathtt{fix\_comb}$ is an EM-morphism,
with computation law $\mathtt{fix\_prom\_E}$]\label{thm:cbv-fix-comb}
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix_value.fix_comb}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix_value.fix_comb_mor}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix_value.fix_prom_E}\rocqok
\uses{def:cbv-fix-lin, thm:cbv-cofree-adj}
The combinator
\[
  \mathtt{fix\_comb}
  \;=\;
  \mathtt{adj\_psi}\,\mathtt{fix\_lin}
  \;:\;
  \widetilde{\mathord{!}}\,LL
  \longrightarrow
  \widetilde{\mathord{!}}\,A
  \qquad\text{in } \mathrm{EM}(\mathord{!})
\]
is a coalgebra morphism \emph{by construction} ($\mathtt{adj\_psi}$ of
the cofree adjunction \autoref{thm:cbv-cofree-adj} lands in coalgebra
morphisms; its underlying $\ICones$ morphism is
$\mathord{!}(\mathtt{fix\_lin}) \circ \mathrm{dig}$,
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix_value.fix_comb_mor}). On a promoted
unit-ball body it satisfies the defining computation law
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix_value.fix_prom_E}:
\[
  \mathtt{fix\_comb}\,(F^!)
  \;=\;
  (\mathtt{fix\_value}\,F)^!.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{lemma}[Non-degeneracy]\label{lem:cbv-fix-nondegen}
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix_value.fix_prom_neq0}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix_value.prom_neq0}\rocqok
\uses{thm:cbv-fix-comb, def:cbv-d-e}
On \emph{every} promoted unit-ball body, the combinator returns a
promoted point of $\mathord{!}A$, which is never the cone-zero: a
promoted point has $e$-mass one ($e(x^!) = 1$), while the zero has
$e$-mass zero. Contrast \autoref{thm:cbv-yfix-degenerate}.
\end{lemma}

\subsection{Coalgebraic bodies: the literal chain}%
\label{ssec:cbv-fix-coalg}

\begin{lemma}[Coalgebraic bodies are setlike on promoted points]%
\label{lem:cbv-fix-setlike}
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix_value.fix_setlike_prom}\rocqok
\uses{thm:cbv-fix-comb, def:cbv-em-cat}
If the body $F$ (as an $\ICones$ morphism
$\mathord{!}A \to \mathord{!}A$) is itself a morphism of
$\mathord{!}$-coalgebras
$\widetilde{\mathord{!}}A \to \widetilde{\mathord{!}}A$, then it maps
promoted points to promoted points:
$F(y^!) = \bigl(\mathrm{der}(F(y^!))\bigr)^!$ for unit-ball $y$. The
proof applies the coalgebra-morphism square at $y^!$ to get
$\mathrm{dig}(F(y^!)) = (F(y^!))^!$, then pushes
$\mathord{!}(\mathrm{der})$ through and cancels by the comonad counit
law.
\end{lemma}

\begin{theorem}[Coalgebraic-case simplification and unfolding]%
\label{thm:cbv-fix-coalg}
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix_value.fix_coalg_simpl}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix_value.fix_unfold_coalg}\rocqok
\uses{lem:cbv-fix-setlike, def:cbv-kleene-from, thm:cbv-fix-value}
For a coalgebraic unit-ball body $F$, the interleaved chain promotes to

($F^n(0^!) = (\mathtt{fix\_chain}\,F\,n)^!$,
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix_value.fix_iter_promE}), and the
combinator computes its supremum
(\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix_value.fix_coalg_simpl}):
\[
  \mathtt{fix\_comb}\,(F^!)
  \;=\;
  \sup_n F^n(0^!),
\]
the seeded Kleene supremum of \autoref{def:cbv-kleene-from} at the seed
$b_0 = 0^!$ --- whose base case $0^! \leq F(0^!)$ is exactly what the
seeded core provides and the zero-seeded
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix.linhom_lfp} could not. The
$\mathord{!}A$-level unfolding follows:
$F\bigl(\mathtt{fix\_comb}(F^!)\bigr) = \mathtt{fix\_comb}(F^!)$
(\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix_value.fix_unfold_coalg}).
\end{theorem}

\subsection{The identity-body witness}%
\label{ssec:cbv-fix-id}

\begin{lemma}[The fix of the identity body is the diverging value,
and it is not zero]\label{lem:cbv-fix-id}
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix_value.fix_idF}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix_value.fix_id_E}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix_value.fix_id_nontrivial}\rocqok

The identity body
$\mathrm{id} : \mathord{!}A \multimap \mathord{!}A$
(\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix_value.fix_idF}) is a coalgebra

$\mathrm{der}(0^!) = 0$), so by
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix_value.fix_id_E}

  \mathtt{fix\_comb}\,(\mathrm{id}^!) = 0^{!},
\]
the diverging \emph{value} --- a promoted point of $e$-mass one,
provably nonzero
(\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix_value.fix_id_nontrivial}). The
combinator does not collapse; it returns the correct bottom of the
value type. Contrast again \autoref{thm:cbv-yfix-degenerate}, where the
same input produces the zero measure.
\end{lemma}

\subsection{The Seely transport for mutual recursion}%
\label{ssec:cbv-fix-mr}

The surface constructor $\mathtt{ne\_fix\_mr}$ binds one recursive
name at any body type $t$ with
\rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.is_free_coalg_type} --- function types
\emph{and products of free types} (a pair of mutually recursive
functions is a recursive name at
$\mathtt{tprod}\,(\mathtt{tfun}\,A_1\,B_1)\,(\mathtt{tfun}\,A_2\,B_2)$).
The combinator $\mathtt{fix\_comb}$ of \autoref{thm:cbv-fix-comb}
lives on \emph{cofree} coalgebras only, and the CBV interpretation of
a product, $\sem{\mathtt{tprod}\,s\,t} = \mathrm{EM\_prod}(\sem{s},
\sem{t})$, is not literally cofree. The repair, delivered in
\texttt{theories/programs/infra/em\_fix\_mr.v} and the decomposition
layer of \texttt{theories/programs/ppl\_cbv.v}, transports the
combinator along an isomorphism of coalgebras.

\begin{definition}[Isomorphisms of $\mathord{!}$-coalgebras]%
\label{def:cbv-coalg-iso}
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix_mr.coalg_iso}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix_mr.coalg_iso_id}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix_mr.coalg_iso_sym}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix_mr.coalg_iso_trans}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix_mr.coalg_iso_prod}\rocqok
\uses{thm:cbv-cartesian, def:cbv-prod}
A $\mathtt{coalg\_iso}\,P\,Q$ bundles two coalgebra morphisms
$P \to Q$ and $Q \to P$ together with the two round-trip identities
at the underlying-$\mathtt{icones\_hom}$ level. The isos carry
identity, symmetry and transitivity
(\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix_mr.coalg_iso_id} /
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix_mr.coalg_iso_sym} /
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix_mr.coalg_iso_trans}) and the
$\mathrm{EM\_prod}$ congruence
(\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix_mr.coalg_iso_prod}: the tensor of
two coalg isos is a coalg iso of the EM products, via
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix_mr.coalg_hom_prod} and the
functoriality of $\mathtt{tensor\_mor}$).
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[The Seely-2 iso at the EM level:
$\mathrm{EM\_prod}(\widetilde{\mathord{!}}X, \widetilde{\mathord{!}}Y)
\cong \widetilde{\mathord{!}}(X \mathbin{\&} Y)$]%
\label{lem:cbv-seely2-em}
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix_mr.seely2_em_iso}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix_mr.EM_prod_str_cofree2}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix_mr.seely2_fwd_is_coalg_mor}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix_mr.seely2_bwd_is_coalg_mor}\rocqok
\uses{def:cbv-coalg-iso, thm:seely, def:cbv-prod}
The $\mathrm{EM}(\mathord{!})$ product of two \emph{cofree}
coalgebras is itself cofree --- on the cone product
$X \mathbin{\&} Y$ (the $\mathtt{sprod}$ of the base cones; note the
\emph{additive} product $\&$, not the tensor). The carrier of
$\mathrm{EM\_prod}(\widetilde{\mathord{!}}X,
\widetilde{\mathord{!}}Y)$ is $\mathord{!}X \otimes \mathord{!}Y$ and
its structure map is by definition the transport of
$\mathrm{dig}_{X \& Y}$ along the Seely isomorphism
$\mathrm{Seely2} : \mathord{!}X \otimes \mathord{!}Y \cong
\mathord{!}(X \mathbin{\&} Y)$ of \autoref{thm:seely}
(\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix_mr.EM_prod_str_cofree2}), so the
coalgebra-morphism squares for the two directions of
$\mathrm{Seely2}$ are pure transport algebra, with no point
computation
(\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix_mr.seely2_fwd_is_coalg_mor} /
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix_mr.seely2_bwd_is_coalg_mor}); the
packaged iso is
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix_mr.seely2_em_iso}.
\end{lemma}

\begin{theorem}[The transported combinator $\mathtt{fix\_comb\_iso}$]%
\label{thm:cbv-fix-comb-iso}
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix_mr.fix_comb_iso}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix_mr.fix_iso_body_conj}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix_mr.fix_comb_iso_prom_E}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix_mr.fix_comb_iso_norm}\rocqok
\uses{def:cbv-coalg-iso, thm:cbv-fix-comb, thm:cbv-fix-value}
For any coalgebra $P$ with an iso
$\iota : P \cong \widetilde{\mathord{!}}Z$, conjugating
$\mathtt{fix\_comb}_Z$ by $\iota$ gives an EM morphism
\[
  \mathtt{fix\_comb\_iso}\;:\;
  \widetilde{\mathord{!}}\bigl(U P \multimap U P\bigr)
  \;\longrightarrow\;
  P
  \qquad\text{in } \mathrm{EM}(\mathord{!}):
\]
bodies $F : U P \multimap U P$ transport to
$\mathord{!}Z \multimap \mathord{!}Z$ by pre/post-composition with
the iso's underlying linear maps (the hom-functor action
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix_mr.fix_iso_body_conj}), the
genuine fixpoint runs at $Z$, and the result is carried back along
the backward direction --- a composite of coalgebra morphisms, hence
an EM morphism by construction. The computation law on promoted
unit-ball bodies is the analogue of $\mathtt{fix\_prom\_E}$
(\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix_mr.fix_comb_iso_prom_E}): the
value is the backward transport of the promoted interleaved-Kleene
fixpoint value of the conjugated body; the underlying map is
norm-decreasing
(\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.em_fix_mr.fix_comb_iso_norm}).
\end{theorem}

\begin{definition}[The free-type decomposition and
$\mathtt{fix\_mr\_comb}$]\label{def:cbv-fix-mr-comb}
\rocq{Icones.programs.ppl_cbv.free_base}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.ppl_cbv.free_decomp}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.ppl_cbv.fix_mr_comb}\rocqok
\uses{lem:cbv-seely2-em, thm:cbv-fix-comb-iso}
By structural induction on the
\rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.is_free_coalg_type} witness,
\rocq{Icones.programs.ppl_cbv.free_base} computes the base cone of a
free type ($\mathtt{tfun}\,t_1\,t_2 \mapsto U\sem{t_1} \multimap
U\sem{t_2}$; $\mathtt{tprod}\,t_1\,t_2 \mapsto
\mathtt{free\_base}\,t_1 \mathbin{\&} \mathtt{free\_base}\,t_2$) and
\rocq{Icones.programs.ppl_cbv.free_decomp} builds the coalgebra iso
$\sem{t} \cong \widetilde{\mathord{!}}(\mathtt{free\_base}\,t)$: the
$\mathtt{tfun}$ case is the identity iso (the interpretation
\emph{is} $\mathtt{bang\_cofree}$), the $\mathtt{tprod}$ case
composes the children's isos (\autoref{def:cbv-coalg-iso}) with the
Seely-2 EM iso (\autoref{lem:cbv-seely2-em}). The genuine seeded
value-fixpoint combinator at any free type is then
\rocq{Icones.programs.ppl_cbv.fix_mr_comb} ---
$\mathtt{fix\_comb}\,(\mathtt{free\_base}\,t)$ transported along the
decomposition (\autoref{thm:cbv-fix-comb-iso}).
\end{definition}

\subsection{Wiring into the interpreter, and the unfolding laws}%
\label{ssec:cbv-fix-wiring}

\begin{definition}[The $\mathtt{ne\_fix}$ clause:
$\sem{\mathtt{fix}\,s.\,M} = \mathtt{fix\_comb} \circ
\sem{\lambda s.\,M}$]\label{def:cbv-fix-clause}
\rocq{Icones.programs.ppl_cbv.eD_fix_E}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.ppl_cbv.eD_fix_mr_E}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.ppl_cbv.eD_fix_mr_fun_E}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.ppl_cbv.eD_fix_mr_prod_E}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.ppl_cbv.fix_mr_clause}\rocqok
\uses{thm:cbv-fix-comb, def:cbv-fix-mr-comb, thm:cbv-yfix-degenerate}
The interpreter's recursion clause is the composite
\[
  \sem{\mathtt{ne\_fix}\,s\,M}
  \;=\;
  \mathtt{fix\_comb}_{L}
    \;\circ\;
  \mathtt{adj\_psi}\bigl(\mathtt{tensor\_curry}\,\sem{M}\bigr),
  \qquad
  L = U\sem{t_1} \multimap U\sem{t_2},
\]
i.e.\ the self-abstraction is interpreted as an \emph{ordinary} lambda
(the $\mathtt{ne\_lam}$ clause, inlined), landing in
$\widetilde{\mathord{!}}(\mathord{!}L \multimap \mathord{!}L)$ ---
exactly the domain of $\mathtt{fix\_comb}_L$ --- and the combinator is
post-composed (definitional pin
\rocq{Icones.programs.ppl_cbv.eD_fix_E}). The
$\mathtt{ne\_fix\_mr}$ clause dispatches on the (free) body type
through \rocq{Icones.programs.ppl_cbv.fix_mr_clause}: at
$\mathtt{tfun}$ it is the same genuine $\mathtt{fix\_comb}$ composite
(\rocq{Icones.programs.ppl_cbv.eD_fix_mr_fun_E}); at
$\mathtt{tprod}$-of-frees it is the same composite with the
Seely-transported combinator $\mathtt{fix\_mr\_comb}$ of
\autoref{def:cbv-fix-mr-comb}
(\rocq{Icones.programs.ppl_cbv.eD_fix_mr_prod_E}) --- the
mutual-recursion fixpoint is genuine at every free body type
(formerly the honest-scope placeholder was the degenerate zero-seeded
iteration of \autoref{thm:cbv-yfix-degenerate}).
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Semantic unfolding of $\mathtt{ne\_fix}$]%
\label{thm:cbv-fix-unfold}
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.cbv_fix_unfold.eD_fix_at_setlike}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.cbv_fix_unfold.eD_fix_unfold}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.cbv_fix_unfold.eD_fix_at_setlike_neq0}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.cbv_fix_unfold.eD_fix_unfold_closed}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.cbv_fix_unfold.eD_fix_mr_prod_at_setlike}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.cbv_fix_unfold.eD_fix_mr_prod_at_setlike_neq0}\rocqok
\uses{def:cbv-fix-clause, thm:cbv-fix-value, thm:cbv-fix-comb-iso}
At any \emph{setlike} unit-ball context point $\gamma$ (one with
$\mathtt{coalg\_str}\,\Gamma\,\gamma = \gamma^!$ --- the \S9.7
``$\gamma$ is a sub-Dirac'' reading, satisfied in particular by the
unit point $1$ of a closed program,
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.cbv_anchors.coalg_str_one1}), writing
$F_\gamma = \mathtt{tensor\_curry}\,\sem{M}\,(\gamma) :
\mathord{!}L \multimap \mathord{!}L$ for the body's endo-function at
$\gamma$:
\begin{itemize}
  \item the prom-point computation law
    (\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.cbv_fix_unfold.eD_fix_at_setlike}):
    $\sem{\mathtt{fix}\,s.\,M}(\gamma) =
    (\mathtt{fix\_value}\,F_\gamma)^!$, the \emph{promoted} supremum of
    the interleaved Kleene chain --- in particular never the cone-zero
    (\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.cbv_fix_unfold.eD_fix_at_setlike_neq0});
  \item the recursion equation
    (\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.cbv_fix_unfold.eD_fix_unfold}):
    \[
      \Bigl(
        \mathrm{der}\bigl(F_\gamma\,
          (\sem{\mathtt{fix}\,s.\,M}(\gamma))\bigr)
      \Bigr)^{!}
      \;=\;
      \sem{\mathtt{fix}\,s.\,M}(\gamma)
    \]
    --- one more body unfolding at the fixpoint value, re-promoted, is
    the fixpoint value;
  \item the closed-program corollaries against the public linhom
    interpreter
    (\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.cbv_fix_unfold.eD_fix_at_one1},
    \rocq{Icones.programs.infra.cbv_fix_unfold.eD_fix_unfold_closed}),
    and the $\mathtt{ne\_fix\_mr}$-at-$\mathtt{tfun}$ twins
    (\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.cbv_fix_unfold.eD_fix_mr_fun_at_setlike},
    \rocq{Icones.programs.infra.cbv_fix_unfold.eD_fix_mr_fun_unfold});
  \item the $\mathtt{ne\_fix\_mr}$-at-$\mathtt{tprod}$ computation law
    (\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.cbv_fix_unfold.eD_fix_mr_prod_at_setlike}):
    at setlike $\gamma$ the denotation is the backward transport along
    $\mathtt{free\_decomp}$ of the promoted fixpoint value of the
    conjugated body (the
    $\mathtt{fix\_comb\_iso\_prom\_E}$ law of
    \autoref{thm:cbv-fix-comb-iso}), and it is never the cone-zero
    (\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.cbv_fix_unfold.eD_fix_mr_prod_at_setlike_neq0})
    --- the mutual-recursion fixpoint is genuine, in contrast with the
    retired zero-seeded placeholder
    (\autoref{thm:cbv-yfix-degenerate}).
\end{itemize}
The honest caveat: the unfolding is stated \emph{pointwise at setlike
points}, not as a morphism equation --- the $\mathtt{adj\_psi}$
packaging computes through $\mathtt{coalg\_str}$, which is only
promoted-point-shaped on setlike inputs.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Cross-reference to the SCones fixpoint core}%
\label{ssec:cbv-yfix-core}

The CBV recursion rests on the paper-\S9.2 stable-fixpoint core of
\autoref{lem:yfix}: at a bare $\mathbf{SCones}$ function space the
zero seed is the right bottom and \rocq{Icones.stable.fixpoint.Yfix}
serves recursion directly, while at a CBV function-\emph{value} type
the bottom is the promoted zero $0^!$, so $\mathtt{fix\_comb}$
\emph{factors through the same operator} --- its chain supremum is
computed by $\mathrm{Yfix}_A$ applied to the curried stable body
(\autoref{def:cbv-fix-value}), then transported into
$\mathrm{EM}(\mathord{!})$ by $\mathrm{lin}$ and $\mathtt{adj\_psi}$,
with the seeding repaired by the $\mathrm{der}$/$\mathrm{prom}$
interleaving.

\paragraph{Consequence for the recursive surface programs.}
The recursive examples \rocq{Icones.programs.examples.ex_loop},
\rocq{Icones.programs.examples.ex_geom},
\rocq{Icones.programs.examples.ex_almost_loop} and
\rocq{Icones.programs.examples.ex_reject} have honest CBV denotations
through \rocq{Icones.programs.ppl_cbv.eD}: the $\mathtt{ne\_fix}$ slot
is the promoted supremum of the interleaved Kleene chain, not the
constant zero. The closed-form mass identities are proved in
\autoref{sec:cbv-geom-mass}.

\section{The higher-order direct-style PPL: linhom-valued interpretation}%
\label{sec:cbv-ppl}

The clean CBV interpreter \texttt{theories/programs/ppl\_cbv.v}
interprets the shared surface inductive
\rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.named_expr} as a \emph{linear} morphism in
$\ICones$, not as a coalgebra morphism. The shape of every clause is
fixed by the SMC primitives (tensor, braid, unitors, identity,
composition) and by the commutative-comonoid pair
$(\delta_\Gamma, \varepsilon_\Gamma) =
(\mathtt{coalg\_d}, \mathtt{coalg\_e})$ carried by every
$\mathord{!}$-coalgebra (Melli\`es Prop 28 / Cor 20,
\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.EMComon_all}). The cofree
$\widetilde{\mathord{!}}$ surfaces at exactly two clauses, both on the
function type: $\mathtt{ne\_lam}$ wraps via $\mathtt{adj\_psi}$, and
$\mathtt{ne\_app}$ unwraps via $\mathrm{der}$.

\paragraph{Why linhom-valued and not coalgebra-morphism-valued.}
Probabilistic programs do not in general commute with the
$\widetilde{\mathord{!}}$-coalgebra duplicability structure carried by
their source contexts; a probabilistic sample-then-pair has a different
denotation than a pair-then-sample. The minimal witness is the
let-then-pair fragment
\[
  \mathtt{let}\,x = \mathtt{sample}\,\mu\,\mathtt{in}\,(x, x)
  \;:\;
  \mathtt{tprod}\,\mathtt{tR}\,\mathtt{tR},
\]
whose share-then-measure denotation pushes $\mu$ along the diagonal
$r \mapsto (r, r)$ and whose measure-then-pair denotation is the
cartesian product measure $\mu \times \mu$; the two coincide iff $\mu$
is a Dirac. The Theorem-9.7 coalgebra structure on $\FMeas$ encodes
exactly the duplicability of shared samples, so a denotation that
\emph{is} a coalgebra morphism would force the share-then-measure
behaviour. The linhom-valued reading leaves the choice open: the
interpreter places no duplicability requirement on the denotation, and
the share-then-measure rule is the one a CBV-style let in fact gives.

\paragraph{Types and contexts.}
The type interpretation
\rocq{Icones.programs.ppl_cbv.tyD_cbv} sends every type to a coalgebra
of $\mathrm{EM}(\mathord{!})$:
\[
  \begin{array}{r@{\;\;=\;\;}l}
    \sem{\mathtt{tunit}}      & \EMterm \\
    \sem{\mathtt{tbase}\,X}   & \FMeas(X)
        \quad\text{(the Theorem-9.7 coalgebra)} \\
    \sem{\mathtt{tprod}\,t_1\,t_2}
                              & \EMprod\,\sem{t_1}\,\sem{t_2} \\
    \sem{\mathtt{tfun}\,A\,B} &
        \widetilde{\mathord{!}}\bigl(U\sem{A} \multimap U\sem{B}\bigr)
        \quad\text{(single $\widetilde{\mathord{!}}$, no $T$ on $B$)} \\
    \sem{\mathtt{tbool}}      &
        \mathtt{bool\_cone\_coalg}
        \quad\text{(the \S9.7-style 2-point coalgebra,
        \autoref{def:cbv-bool-cone-coalg}).}
  \end{array}
\]
Contexts are interpreted by
\rocq{Icones.programs.ppl_cbv.ctxD_cbv} right-to-left with the head on
the right, on the name-stripped skeleton
\rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.drop_names}~$\Gamma$; the variable witness
in $\mathtt{ne\_var}$ is projected to its De Bruijn index by
\rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.named_var_to_has_var} and then dispatched to
\rocq{Icones.programs.ppl_cbv.var_lookup_cbv}, a chain of
$\pi_1 / \pi_2$ projections over the tensor product context coalgebra.

The function-type clause is the only place where the source language
carries a single $\widetilde{\mathord{!}}$. There is no
$\widetilde{\mathord{!}} \circ U$ Moggi wrap on the codomain $B$: a
function value is a linear morphism
$U\sem{A} \multimap U\sem{B}$, and what is duplicable is the
\emph{value}, not the result of its application. This is the cleanest
reading the SMCC + EM(!) data of \autoref{thm:cbv-model} support.

\begin{remark}[$\mathtt{tbool}$ uses the \S9.7-style 2-point
coalgebra]\label{rem:cbv-tbool}
The clause $\sem{\mathtt{tbool}} = \mathtt{bool\_cone\_coalg}$ is
\emph{not} the cofree fallback
$\widetilde{\mathord{!}}\,\mathtt{bool\_cone\_car}$. It is the
non-cofree \S9.7-shaped $\mathord{!}$-coalgebra
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.bool_cone_coalg.bool_cone_coalg} on the
carrier $\mathtt{bool\_cone\_car}\,Ar$ itself
(\autoref{def:cbv-bool-cone-coalg}), with structure map
$\mathtt{bool\_coalg\_str}\,(p, q) =
p \cdot \mathrm{prom}(\delta_T) + q \cdot \mathrm{prom}(\delta_F)$.
This is the boolean specialisation of paper Theorem~9.7's
$\mathtt{Coalg}_X(\mu) = \int \mathrm{prom}(\delta_x)\,d\mu(x)$ to the
2-point case --- which morally is
$\FMeas(\mathtt{bool\_meas\_space})$, hand-built here without
exposing $\mathtt{bool\_meas\_space} : \mathtt{measurableType}\,R$ as an
\rocq{Icones.mcones.ar.ar_obj} of $\mathbf{Ar}$ (a parameter-supplied
datum on $\mathbf{Ar}$). The transported commutative comonoid pair
$(\delta_{\mathtt{tbool}}, \varepsilon_{\mathtt{tbool}})$ on
$\mathtt{bool\_cone\_coalg}$ then recovers shared-sample semantics for
boolean draws (\autoref{def:cbv-bool-cone-coalg}).
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Term interpretation.}
The interpretation \rocq{Icones.programs.ppl_cbv.eD} assigns to every
$\Gamma \vdash M : \tau$ a linhom
\[
  \sem{M}
  \;:\;
  \mathrm{linhom}\bigl(U\sem{\mathtt{drop\_names}\,\Gamma},\,U\sem{\tau}\bigr),
\]
defined by structural recursion through the icones-hom-valued internal
helper \rocq{Icones.programs.ppl_cbv.eD_cbv}. Composing with
\rocq{Icones.homs.seely.icones_to_linhom} yields the public linhom; the
icones-hom variant lets every clause compose cleanly with
$\mathtt{tensor\_mor}$, $\mathtt{coalg\_d}$, $\mathtt{coalg\_e}$,
$\mathtt{tensor\_curry}$, $\mathtt{tensor\_uncurry}$. The
clause-by-clause shape is summarised below; in every line $\sem{M}$ is
an $\mathtt{icones\_hom}$ from $\sem{\Gamma}$'s carrier to
$\sem{\tau}$'s carrier.

\begin{itemize}
  \item $\mathtt{ne\_var}\,v$:
    \rocq{Icones.programs.ppl_cbv.var_lookup_cbv} of the variable
    witness $v$ --- a projection chain over the tensor product context.
  \item $\mathtt{ne\_tt}$: the comonoid counit
    $\varepsilon_\Gamma = \mathtt{em\_term\_mor}\,\sem{\Gamma}$
    (\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.em_term_mor}).
  \item $\mathtt{ne\_pair}\,M\,N$:
    $\mathtt{em\_pair\_mor}\,\sem{M}\,\sem{N}$
    (\rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.em_pair_mor}); the diagonal
    $\delta_\Gamma = \mathtt{coalg\_d}$ is fired inside
    $\mathtt{em\_pair\_mor}$ to give each of the two sub-terms its own
    copy of the context.
  \item $\mathtt{ne\_fst}\,p$ / $\mathtt{ne\_snd}\,p$: pre-composition
    with \rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.em_proj1_mor} /
    \rocq{Icones.homs.em_cartesian.em_proj2_mor}.
  \item $\mathtt{ne\_lam}\,x\,M$:
    $\mathtt{adj\_psi}\,(\mathtt{tensor\_curry}\,\sem{M})$. The body
    $\sem{M}$ is curried by the SMCC closure of $\ICones$
    (\autoref{thm:thm515}) into
    $\mathrm{linhom}(U\sem{\Gamma}, U\sem{A} \multimap U\sem{B})$, and
    $\mathtt{adj\_psi}$ of the cofree adjunction
    (\autoref{thm:cbv-cofree-adj}) at $\sem{\Gamma}$'s structure map
    promotes the result into
    $\widetilde{\mathord{!}}(U\sem{A} \multimap U\sem{B}) =
    \sem{\mathtt{tfun}\,A\,B}$. This is the first of the two
    $\widetilde{\mathord{!}}$ boundaries.
  \item $\mathtt{ne\_app}\,f\,e$:
    \[
      \sem{f \, e}
      \;=\;
      \mathtt{tensor\_uncurry}\,\mathrm{id}
        \;\circ\;
        (\mathrm{der}_{U\sem{A} \multimap U\sem{B}} \otimes \mathrm{id})
        \;\circ\;
        \mathtt{em\_pair\_mor}\,\sem{f}\,\sem{e}.
    \]
    The pair $\mathtt{em\_pair\_mor}\,\sem{f}\,\sem{e}$ supplies a copy
    of $\Gamma$ to each of $f$ and $e$ via $\delta_\Gamma$; the
    dereliction $\mathrm{der}$ unwraps the function value from its
    cofree $\widetilde{\mathord{!}}$ (the second $\widetilde{\mathord{!}}$
    boundary); $\mathtt{tensor\_uncurry}\,\mathrm{id}$ is the SMCC
    evaluation $(U\sem{A} \multimap U\sem{B}) \otimes U\sem{A} \to
    U\sem{B}$.
  \item $\mathtt{ne\_let}\,x\,M\,K$:
    $\sem{K} \circ \mathtt{em\_pair\_mor}\,\mathrm{id}_\Gamma\,\sem{M}$.
    The diagonal $\delta_\Gamma$ inside $\mathtt{em\_pair\_mor}$ pairs
    the context with the value of $M$, and the continuation $K$ is
    interpreted in the extended context. There is no Moggi-bind
    intermediate.
  \item $\mathtt{ne\_sample}\,\mu$, $\mathtt{ne\_real}\,r$,
    $\mathtt{ne\_true}$, $\mathtt{ne\_false}$,
    $\mathtt{ne\_bernoulli}\,p$: the constant $\mathtt{icones\_hom}$
    \rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.const_icones} out of $\sem{\Gamma}$ into,
    respectively, $\mu \in \FMeas(R_\mathrm{obj})$,
    $\delta_r \in \FMeas(R_\mathrm{obj})$, the Dirac points
    $\delta_T$ / $\delta_F$ in $\mathtt{bool\_cone\_car}$ (no
    $\mathrm{prom}$: the structure map of the
    \rocq{Icones.programs.infra.bool_cone_coalg.bool_cone_coalg}
    sends $\delta_T$ to $\mathrm{prom}(\delta_T)$ when the comonoid
    diagonal fires, but the constant lands in the carrier), and
    $\mathtt{bernoulli}\,p = p \cdot \delta_T + (1-p) \cdot \delta_F
    \in \mathtt{bool\_cone\_car}$. No coalgebra-morphism obligation
    arises: the constant linear morphism into a unit-ball element is
    automatically an icones-hom by
    \rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.const_icones}.
  \item $\mathtt{ne\_add}\,M\,N$ / $\mathtt{ne\_mul}\,M\,N$:
    \rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.add_lift} (resp.\
    \rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.mul_lift}) composed with
    $\mathtt{em\_pair\_mor}\,\sem{M}\,\sem{N}$. The arithmetic lifts
    are obtained from the FMeas lax-monoidal map
    (\autoref{thm:cbv-fmeas-lax}) post-composed with the
    $\FMeas$-functorial action of the measurable
    $\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$ operation.
  \item $\mathtt{ne\_score}\,f\,e$:
    $\mathtt{score\_lift}\,f \circ \sem{e}$, with
    \rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.score_lift} the density lift of
    \autoref{lem:ppl-score-tm-lift}.
  \item $\mathtt{ne\_bernoulli\_f}\,f\,e$ (value-dependent Bernoulli):
    $\mathtt{bern\_lift}\,f \circ \sem{e}$, with
    \rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.bern_lift} the path lift
    $\mu \mapsto (\int f\,d\mu,\ \int (1 - f)\,d\mu)$ of
    \autoref{lem:ppl-bern-lift} --- sample $r \sim \mu$, then flip a
    coin with success probability $f(r)$, landing in
    $\mathtt{bool\_cone\_car}$. The exact $\mathtt{tbool}$-valued
    mirror of the $\mathtt{ne\_score}$ clause
    (\rocq{Icones.programs.ppl_cbv.eD_bernoulli_f_E}). This is the
    accept/reject primitive of the rejection-sampling headline
    (\autoref{sec:cbv-geom-mass}).
  \item $\mathtt{ne\_meas}\,f\,e$ (measurable function application):
    $\mathtt{meas\_lift}\,f \circ \sem{e}$, with
    \rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.meas_lift} the $\FMeas$ functorial
    action (pushforward) at the carrier transport of the measurable
    $f : \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ --- no $[0,1]$ bounds are needed,
    since the pushforward already has operator norm at most one. The
    Dirac identity \rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.meas_lift_dirac}
    ($\delta_r \mapsto \delta_{f(r)}$) and the total-mass identity
    \rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.meas_lift_mass} are the load-bearing
    laws; the clause pin is
    \rocq{Icones.programs.ppl_cbv.eD_meas_E} --- the
    $\FMeas$-functorial mirror of the $\mathtt{ne\_score}$ clause.
  \item $\mathtt{ne\_gaussian}\,e_1\,e_2$ /
    $\mathtt{ne\_uniform}\,e_1\,e_2$ (runtime-parameter
    distributions, surface \texttt{Gaussian( e1 , e2 )} /
    \texttt{Uniform( e1 , e2 )}): the $\mathtt{ne\_add}$ clause shape
    with the two-argument kernel lift in place of the arithmetic
    pushforward,
    $\mathtt{kernel\_lift2}\,k \circ
     \mathtt{em\_pair\_mor}\,\sem{e_1}\,\sem{e_2}$, where
    $\mathtt{kernel\_lift2}\,k = \mathtt{kernel\_lift}\,k \circ
     \mathtt{fmeas\_lax}$ packages the tensored argument pair as a
    joint measure on the product object and feeds it to the
    probability kernel $k$ ---
    \rocq{Icones.programs.distributions.kernel_lift2} at the
    instances \rocq{Icones.programs.distributions.gaussian_kernel}
    ($(m,s) \mapsto \mathcal{N}(m,s)$, totalised at $s = 0$ as the
    Dirac $\delta_m$, the degenerate weak limit) and
    \rocq{Icones.programs.distributions.uniform_kernel}
    ($(a,b) \mapsto \mathcal{U}(a,b)$ for $a < b$, else
    $\delta_a$); clause pins
    \rocq{Icones.programs.ppl_cbv.eD_gaussian_E} /
    \rocq{Icones.programs.ppl_cbv.eD_uniform_E}.
  \item $\mathtt{ne\_if}\,b\,M\,N$:
    $\mathtt{if\_icones}\,\sem{M}\,\sem{N}\,\sem{b}$ of
    \autoref{def:cbv-case-em} --- the six steps use
    \rocq{Icones.programs.infra.bool_case_hom.bool_case_linhom},
    \rocq{Icones.homs.seely.linhom_icones},
    $\mathtt{tensor\_uncurry}$, a braid, and a final
    $\mathtt{em\_pair\_mor}$ with the scrutinee; with the §9.7-style
    $\mathtt{bool\_cone\_coalg}$ on $\sem{\mathtt{tbool}}$, the
    scrutinee lands directly in $\mathtt{bool\_cone\_car}$ (no
    $\mathrm{der}$ step required).
  \item $\mathtt{ne\_fix}\,s\,M$ (OCaml-style recursion at function
    types):
    \[
      \sem{\mathtt{ne\_fix}\,s\,M}
      \;=\;
      \mathtt{fix\_comb}_{L}
        \;\circ\;
      \mathtt{adj\_psi}\bigl(\mathtt{tensor\_curry}\,\sem{M}\bigr),
      \qquad
      L = U\sem{t_1} \multimap U\sem{t_2},
    \]
    the seeded value-fixpoint combinator of \autoref{thm:cbv-fix-comb}
    post-composed with the inlined $\mathtt{ne\_lam}$ packaging of the
    body (\autoref{def:cbv-fix-clause}, definitional pin
    \rocq{Icones.programs.ppl_cbv.eD_fix_E}). The body $\sem{M}$ is
    interpreted in the context extended by the self-reference
    $s : \mathtt{tfun}\,t_1\,t_2$.
  \item $\mathtt{ne\_fix\_mr}\,s\,t\,\_\,M$ (free-coalg-type
    generalisation, for mutual recursion / product-shaped recursive
    values): dispatched on the body type by
    \rocq{Icones.programs.ppl_cbv.fix_mr_clause} --- at
    $\mathtt{tfun}$ the same genuine $\mathtt{fix\_comb}$ composite as
    $\mathtt{ne\_fix}$
    (\rocq{Icones.programs.ppl_cbv.eD_fix_mr_fun_E}); at
    $\mathtt{tprod}$-of-frees the same composite with the
    Seely-transported $\mathtt{fix\_mr\_comb}$
    (\rocq{Icones.programs.ppl_cbv.eD_fix_mr_prod_E},
    \autoref{def:cbv-fix-mr-comb}).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{The two $\widetilde{\mathord{!}}$ boundaries.}
The CBV interpreter never mentions
$\widetilde{\mathord{!}} \circ U$ in the codomain of any type, nor any
$\mathtt{Tobj}$ / $\mathtt{tunit\_eta}$ / Kleisli bind. The cofree
$\widetilde{\mathord{!}}$ appears in $\sem{\mathtt{tfun}\,A\,B}$ on the
outside, and the interpreter touches it exactly twice: $\mathtt{adj\_psi}$
at $\mathtt{ne\_lam}$ and $\mathrm{der}$ at $\mathtt{ne\_app}$. All
other compositional clauses ($\mathtt{ne\_let}$, $\mathtt{ne\_pair}$,
$\mathtt{ne\_fst}$ / $\mathtt{ne\_snd}$, $\mathtt{ne\_if}$,
$\mathtt{ne\_add}$ / $\mathtt{ne\_mul}$, $\mathtt{ne\_score}$) use only
the SMC primitives and the comonoid pair
$(\delta_\Gamma, \varepsilon_\Gamma)$. The diagonal $\delta_\Gamma$ is
what supplies each sub-expression of a compositional node with its own
copy of the context, so multi-use of a bound variable is free: it is a
passenger of the shared context, dispatched by the projection chain of
$\mathtt{var\_lookup\_cbv}$.

\paragraph{Arithmetic, score, and the FMeas lax-monoidal map.}
The interpretation of $\mathtt{ne\_add}$, $\mathtt{ne\_mul}$ and
$\mathtt{ne\_score}$ reuses the cross-cutting helpers
\rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.add_lift},
\rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.mul_lift} and
\rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.score_lift} of the surface module
\texttt{ppl.v}. These are $\ICones$-morphisms built from the FMeas lax
symmetric monoidal map of \autoref{thm:cbv-fmeas-lax} and the paper
\S6 follow-up
\rocq{Icones.homs.bilin.int_to_linhom_pres_path_in_cone}. Each binary
arithmetic clause composes the pair
$\mathtt{em\_pair\_mor}\,\sem{M}\,\sem{N}$ (whose diagonal is
$\delta_\Gamma$) with the lax-monoidal arithmetic lift; the score
clause $\mathtt{ne\_score}\,f\,e$ post-composes the density lift
$\mathtt{score\_lift}\,f$ with $\sem{e}$, producing the unit-cone
factor that scores the surrounding computation.

\begin{theorem}[The FMeas lax symmetric monoidal map at \texorpdfstring{$\ICones$}{ICones}]%
\label{thm:cbv-fmeas-lax}
\rocq{Icones.homs.fmeas_lax.fmeas_lax}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.homs.fmeas_lax.fmeas_lax_E}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.homs.fmeas_lax.fmeas_lax_dirac}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.homs.bilin.int_to_linhom_pres_path_in_cone}\rocqok
\uses{thm:cbv-model}
For every $X, Y \in \mathbf{Ar}$ there is an $\ICones$ morphism
$\mathtt{fmeas\_lax}\,X\,Y :
   \FMeas(X) \otimes \FMeas(Y) \;\to\; \FMeas(X \times Y)$
that sends the pure tensor $\mu \otimes \nu$ to the cartesian
product measure $\mu \times \nu$ (\rocq{Icones.homs.fmeas_lax.fmeas_lax_E}),
and so sends Dirac point masses to the Dirac at the paired point,
$\mathtt{fmeas\_lax}\,(\delta_x \otimes \delta_y) = \delta_{(x,y)}$
(\rocq{Icones.homs.fmeas_lax.fmeas_lax_dirac}).
Construction. $\mathtt{fmeas\_lax}$ is obtained by
\rocq{Icones.homs.tensor_construct.tensor_uncurry} of the outer-linhom
$\FMeas(X) \multimap (\FMeas(Y) \multimap \FMeas(X \times Y))$
built by paper Theorem 6.1
(\rocq{Icones.homs.bilin.int_to_linhom}) twice: at the inner level
$\nu \mapsto \mathtt{fmeas\_lax\_pre}\,\delta_x\,\nu$ is a path
into $\FMeas(X \times Y)$ via the Dirac-pushforward path
$\mathtt{dirac\_lax}$, and at the outer level $x \mapsto (\nu \mapsto \cdots)$
is a path into the iCone $\FMeas(Y) \multimap \FMeas(X \times Y)$
of linhoms, which is the
\emph{path-preservation-in-the-cone-variable} step now discharged
as \rocq{Icones.homs.bilin.int_to_linhom_pres_path_in_cone} ---
the paper \S6 follow-up to Theorem 6.1 that the previous header of
\texttt{bilin.v} flagged as deferred.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\rocq{Icones.homs.fmeas_lax.fmeas_lax_E} reduces, on the pure
tensor, to the load-bearing Pettis/Tonelli identity
$\mathtt{fmeas\_lax\_pre\_iterated}$, expressing the pushforward
measure as the iterated icone-integral of $\mathtt{dirac\_lax}$.
The Dirac identity \rocq{Icones.homs.fmeas_lax.fmeas_lax_dirac}
then collapses to the function-level
$\mathtt{fmeas\_lax\_pre\_dirac}$, which is the elementary identity
$\delta_x \times_{\mathrm{meas}} \delta_y =
\delta_{(x,y)}$ along the measurable carrier cast
$\mathtt{ar\_prod\_cast}$. Construction of the outer linhom relies on
\rocq{Icones.homs.bilin.int_to_linhom_pres_path_in_cone}, which is
proved by packaging the cone-variable and the integrand into the
joint state $\mathbf{Ar}(Z) \times \mathbf{Ar}(Y)$ of
\rocq{Icones.icones.icone_integral.icone_integral_joint_measurable}
and specialising via the diagonal $(z, r) \mapsto (z, (z, r))$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The term-level score lift]\label{lem:ppl-score-tm-lift}
\rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.score_lift}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.score_lift_dirac}\rocqok
\uses{thm:cbv-fmeas-lax}
For every measurable $f : \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ with
$0 \le f(r) \le 1$ for all $r$, there is an $\ICones$ morphism
$\mathtt{score\_lift}\,f :
   \FMeas(R_{\mathrm{obj}}) \;\to\; \mathtt{cone\_one\_car}$
of operator norm at most one, satisfying the Dirac identity
$\mathtt{score\_lift}\,f\,(\delta_{\mathtt{R\_to\_carrier}\,r})
= f(r) \cdot \mathtt{one1}$
(\rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.score_lift_dirac}).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The path $r \mapsto f(r) \cdot \mathtt{one1}$ is a measurable path
into $\mathtt{cone\_one\_car}$ of norm at most one (pointwise
$f(r) \le 1$), so by paper Theorem 6.1
(\rocq{Icones.homs.bilin.int_to_linhom}) it lifts to an
$\FMeas(R_{\mathrm{obj}}) \multimap \mathtt{cone\_one\_car}$ linhom
$\mathtt{int\_to\_linhom}\,\mathtt{score\_path}$; the
$\ICones$-morphism packaging $\mathtt{linhom\_icones}$ then yields
$\mathtt{score\_lift}$. The Dirac identity follows by integration
against a Dirac evaluating the path at the carrier point.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The value-dependent Bernoulli lift]%
\label{lem:ppl-bern-lift}
\rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.bern_lift}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.bern_lift_dirac}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.bern_lift_E}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.bern_lift_mass}\rocqok
\uses{lem:ppl-score-tm-lift, def:cbv-bool-cone}
For every measurable $f : \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ with
$0 \le f(r) \le 1$ for all $r$, there is an $\ICones$ morphism
$\mathtt{bern\_lift}\,f :
   \FMeas(R_{\mathrm{obj}}) \;\to\; \mathtt{bool\_cone\_car}$
sending a measure $\mu$ on the reals to the 2-point sub-probability
\[
  \Bigl(\int f \, d\mu,\ \int (1 - f)\, d\mu\Bigr)
\]
--- sample $r \sim \mu$, then flip a coin with success probability
$f(r)$. It satisfies the Dirac identity
$\mathtt{bern\_lift}\,f\,(\delta_r) =
\mathtt{bernoulli}\,(f\,r)$
(\rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.bern_lift_dirac}), the componentwise
integral identity \rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.bern_lift_E} against the
$\mathtt{bool\_int}$ of \texttt{bool\_cone.v}, and the total-mass
identity $\cnorm{\mathtt{bern\_lift}\,f\,\mu} = \mu(\mathbb{R})$
(\rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.bern_lift_mass}: the coin is norm-$1$
pointwise, so the lift preserves total mass).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
A verbatim clone of the \autoref{lem:ppl-score-tm-lift} path route:
package $r \mapsto \mathtt{bernoulli}\,(f(r))$ as a measurable path
into $\mathtt{bool\_cone\_car}$ (the three tests of the bool cone
evaluate along the path to $f$, $1 - f$ and the constant $1$), then
promote with paper Theorem 6.1
(\rocq{Icones.homs.bilin.int_to_linhom}). The integral identity is
Pettis uniqueness against the componentwise integral; the mass and
Dirac identities are corollaries.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Runtime-parameter distributions: the
probability-kernel layer]\label{def:cbv-pkernel}
A \emph{probability kernel} from $X$ to $Y$ (both objects of
$\mathbf{Ar}$) is a family of sub-probability measures
$k : X \to \FMeas\,Y$ together with the measurability of every
evaluation $x \mapsto k(x)(U)$ ($U$ measurable) and the unit-ball
bound $\cnorm{k(x)} \le 1$ --- the record
\rocq{Icones.programs.distributions.pkernel}. Such a family is a
measurable path (paper Definition~3.7,
\rocq{Icones.programs.distributions.pkernel_is_path}), so
Theorem~6.1 promotes it to the linear morphism
$\mathtt{kernel\_lift}\,k : \FMeas\,X \multimap \FMeas\,Y$,
$\nu \mapsto \int k(x)\,\nu(dx)$ (a Pettis integral), with the
evaluation law $(\mathtt{kernel\_lift}\,k\,\nu)(U) =
\int k(x)(U)\,\nu(dx)$
(\rocq{Icones.programs.distributions.kernel_lift_E}), the mass law
for pointwise-probability kernels
(\rocq{Icones.programs.distributions.kernel_lift_mass}) and the
Dirac reduction $\mathtt{kernel\_lift}\,k\,\delta_x = k(x)$
(\rocq{Icones.programs.distributions.kernel_lift_dirac}). The
surface forms \texttt{Gaussian( e1 , e2 )} /
\texttt{Uniform( e1 , e2 )} consume two-argument kernels on the
product object through
\rocq{Icones.programs.distributions.kernel_lift2}; the semantic
anchors (\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.kernel_anchors.eD_gaussian_at},
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.kernel_anchors.eD_gaussian_dirac_E},
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.kernel_anchors.eD_gaussian_mass},
agreement with the bundled-sample surface at literals
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.kernel_anchors.eD_gaussian_sample_agree},
and the \texttt{Uniform} mirrors) compute the draws on point-mass
arguments. The hierarchy demo
\rocq{Icones.programs.examples.ex_gaussian_walk} ---
\texttt{let s = Gaussian(0,1) in Gaussian(s,1)}, the runtime
parameter being the sampled value --- denotes the hierarchy integral
$U \mapsto \int \mathcal{N}(r,1)(U)\,d\mathcal{N}(0,1)(r)$
(\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.kernel_anchors.ex_gaussian_walk_E}) and
is a probability of total mass one
(\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.kernel_anchors.ex_gaussian_walk_mass}).
\end{definition}

\section{The let-at-sample integral law and the semantic anchors}%
\label{sec:cbv-laws}

Two blocks of supporting semantic content feed the headline analyses
of \autoref{sec:cbv-geom-mass}: a Pettis-integral reading of the CBV
$\mathtt{let}$-at-$\mathtt{sample}$ fragment, and a family of
equational \emph{anchors} that pin the operational reading of the
interpreter (shared-sample diagonal vs.\ independent tensor, the
$\beta$-rule, the if-orientation) so that a refactor of the \S7/\S9
cartesian machinery that silently flipped the semantics would fail to
compile. The section closes with their first consumers
(\autoref{ssec:cbv-marginals}): the closed-form CBV identities of the
non-recursive basic sampling/scoring examples, including the
unnormalised score posterior with its exact pairing with the
rejection sampler, and the model evidence of the higher-order
Bayesian linear regression.

\subsection{The let-at-sample Pettis integral law}%
\label{ssec:cbv-let-sample}

\begin{theorem}[The let-at-sample integral law]%
\label{thm:cbv-let-sample}
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.let_sample_law.eD_let_sample_int}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.let_sample_law.eD_let_sample_collapse}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.let_sample_law.ptensor_icone_integral}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.let_sample_law.icone_integral_dirac_fmeas}\rocqok
\uses{thm:cbv-cartesian, def:int-to-linhom}
For any context point $\gamma$ --- \emph{arbitrary}, with no unit-ball
or setlike restriction --- the CBV denotation of a let on a sample is
the Pettis integral of the continuation's denotation over the Diracs
of the sampled measure:
\[
  \sem{\mathtt{let}\,x = \mathtt{sample}\,\mu\,\mathtt{in}\,K}(\gamma)
  \;=\;
  \int \sem{K}(\gamma \otimes \delta_r)\;\mu(dr).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Four steps, each driven by a genuine $\mathrm{linhom}$ /
$\mathtt{icones\_hom}$ field that holds on the whole cone. (1) The
sample-let collapses to
$\sem{K}(\gamma \otimes \mu)$
(\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.let_sample_law.eD_let_sample_collapse}),
via the comonoid counit law through the constant-pairing computation
of $\mathtt{em\_pair\_mor}$. (2) $\mu = \int \delta_r\,\mu(dr)$ with
the bare Dirac integrand
(\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.let_sample_law.icone_integral_dirac_fmeas},
a re-spelling of the Theorem 6.1 Dirac approximation). (3) Tensoring
with a fixed point preserves Pettis integrals,
$\gamma \otimes \int \beta\,d\mu = \int (\gamma \otimes \beta_r)\,
\mu(dr)$
(\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.let_sample_law.ptensor_icone_integral},
from the integral-preservation field of the pure-tensor map). (4) The
integral-preservation field of $\sem{K}$ pushes the denotation under
the integral.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Per-$U$ evaluation and the measure-on-$U$ corollary]%
\label{lem:cbv-let-sample-mu}
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.let_sample_law.icone_integral_fmeas_E}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.let_sample_law.eD_let_sample_mu_E}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.let_sample_law.let_sample_var_E}\rocqok
\uses{thm:cbv-let-sample}
An $\FMeas$-valued Pettis integral evaluates per measurable set:
$\bigl(\int \beta\,d\mu\bigr)(U) = \int \beta_r(U)\,\mu(dr)$ for
\emph{arbitrary} measurable $U$
(\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.let_sample_law.icone_integral_fmeas_E}).
Fused with \autoref{thm:cbv-let-sample} at result type $\mathtt{tR}$
this gives the measure-on-$U$ reading
\[
  \sem{\mathtt{let}\,x = \mathtt{sample}\,\mu\,\mathtt{in}\,K}
    (\gamma)(U)
  \;=\;
  \int \sem{K}(\gamma \otimes \delta_r)(U)\;\mu(dr)
\]
(\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.let_sample_law.eD_let_sample_mu_E}) ---
the exact shape the rejection-sampling mass recurrence consumes
(\autoref{lem:cbv-reject-recurrence}). Sanity:
$\sem{\mathtt{let}\,x = \mathtt{sample}\,\mu\,\mathtt{in}\,x}(1) =
\mu$
(\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.let_sample_law.let_sample_var_E}).
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[The general let-law: arbitrary bound computation]%
\label{lem:cbv-let-general}
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.let_sample_law.eD_let_collapse_setlike}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.let_sample_law.eD_let_int}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.let_sample_law.eD_let_mu_E}\rocqok
\uses{thm:cbv-let-sample, lem:cbv-let-sample-mu}
The let-at-sample law is the special case $M = \mathtt{sample}\,\mu$
of the general CBV sequencing law: for an \emph{arbitrary} bound
computation $M : \mathtt{tR}$ and any \emph{setlike unit-ball}
context point $\gamma$,
\[
  \sem{\mathtt{let}\,x = M\,\mathtt{in}\,K}(\gamma)
  \;=\;
  \int \sem{K}(\gamma \otimes \delta_r)\;\sem{M}(\gamma)(dr)
\]
--- the bound \emph{sub-distribution} $\sem{M}(\gamma)$ replaces the
constant prior
(\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.let_sample_law.eD_let_int}); the fused
measure-on-$U$ form
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.let_sample_law.eD_let_mu_E} reads it per
measurable set. This is the law the rejection-sampling
\emph{combinator} (\autoref{ssec:cbv-reject-model}) consumes, with
$M = m\,@\,a$ the model applied to the input.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Only the step-1 collapse changes against
\autoref{thm:cbv-let-sample}: the pairing
$\langle\mathrm{id}, \sem{M}\rangle$ now feeds $\gamma$ to
\emph{both} legs, so the comonoid copy must compute --- at setlike
unit-ball points the diagonal is the literal pure tensor
$\gamma \mapsto \gamma \otimes \gamma$ (the setlike-point kit,
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.cbv_anchors.coalg_d_setlike}), giving
$\sem{\mathtt{let}\,x = M\,\mathtt{in}\,K}(\gamma) =
\sem{K}(\gamma \otimes \sem{M}(\gamma))$
(\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.let_sample_law.eD_let_collapse_setlike}).
The setlike restriction is harmless: in every consumer the let sits
under binders whose environments are setlike by construction. Steps
2--4 (Dirac decomposition, tensor-preserves-integrals, the
integral-preservation field of $\sem{K}$) are reused verbatim ---
none of them mention the bound measure.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The CBV marginals and the Bayesian-regression evidence}%
\label{ssec:cbv-marginals}

The non-recursive basic sampling/scoring examples
(\rocq{Icones.programs.examples.ex_random_constant},
\rocq{Icones.programs.examples.ex_random_linear},
\rocq{Icones.programs.examples.ex_score_posterior}, and the
higher-order Bayesian linear regression
\rocq{Icones.programs.examples.ex_bayes_linear}) carry closed-form
CBV identities, proved in
\texttt{theories/programs/infra/cbv\_marginals.v} on top of the
let-at-sample law of \autoref{thm:cbv-let-sample}. The common route:
the law turns each $\mathtt{let}$-$\mathtt{sample}$ prefix into a
Pettis integral over Diracs of the prior, dereliction / evaluation at
setlike test points pushes inside the integral, and the integrand
computes pointwise down to a Dirac integral.

\begin{lemma}[The probability-counit kit]%
\label{lem:cbv-marginal-kit}
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.cbv_marginals.coalg_e_FMeas_prob}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.cbv_marginals.em_proj1_mor_unitE}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.cbv_marginals.em_proj1_mor_probE}\rocqok
\uses{lem:cbv-anchor-diag, thm:cbv-cartesian}
The comonoid counit of the \S9.7 coalgebra on $\FMeas(X)$ sends every
\emph{probability} measure to the unit point
(\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.cbv_marginals.coalg_e_FMeas_prob}:
on Diracs the counit is the unit since Diracs are setlike, and the
Dirac-to-integral lift plus the Pettis equation on the unit cone read
off the total mass). Consequently the first cartesian projection of
$\mathrm{EM}(\mathord{!})$ discards an $\FMeas$-typed probability
component silently
(\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.cbv_marginals.em_proj1_mor_probE}),
and discards a non-setlike $\mathtt{tunit}$-typed component as its
scalar weight
(\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.cbv_marginals.em_proj1_mor_unitE})
--- the mechanism by which a score result becomes a scaling factor.
\end{lemma}

\begin{theorem}[The unnormalised score posterior]%
\label{thm:cbv-score-posterior}
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.cbv_marginals.ex_score_posterior_cbv_E}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.cbv_marginals.ex_score_posterior_cbv_mass}\rocqok
\uses{lem:cbv-let-sample-mu, lem:cbv-marginal-kit}
The closed CBV denotation of $\mathtt{ex\_score\_posterior}$ (sample
$m \sim \mu$, score by a measurable density $f : \mathbb{R} \to
[0,1]$, return $m$ --- the one-dimensional, one-parameter scoring
shape; historically misnamed \texttt{ex\_bayes\_linear}) is the
\emph{unnormalised posterior}: for every measurable $U$,
\[
  \sem{\mathtt{ex\_score\_posterior}}(1)(U) \;=\; \int_U f\,d\mu,
\]
with total mass the evidence $\int f\,d\mu$
(\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.cbv_marginals.ex_score_posterior_cbv_mass}).
The proof applies the measure-on-$U$ law of
\autoref{lem:cbv-let-sample-mu}; at the one-Dirac environment
$1 \otimes \delta_r$ the score evaluates to the scalar $f(r)$
(\rocq{Icones.programs.ppl.score_lift_dirac}), which the
$\mathtt{tunit}$-projection of \autoref{lem:cbv-marginal-kit} turns
into a $\mathtt{precone\_scale}$ weight on the returned $\delta_r$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Rejection sampling normalises the score
posterior]\label{thm:cbv-reject-score}
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.cbv_marginals.ex_reject_normalises_score}\rocqok
\uses{thm:cbv-score-posterior, thm:cbv-reject-master}
At a probability prior ($\mu(\mathbb{R}) = 1$), for every measurable
$U$,
\[
  \Bigl(\int f\,d\mu\Bigr)\cdot
  \sem{\mathtt{ex\_reject}}(1)(U)
  \;=\;
  \sem{\mathtt{ex\_score\_posterior}}(1)(U)
\]
--- the rejection-sampling denotation times the total evidence is
exactly the score denotation: $\mathtt{score}$ computes the
unnormalised posterior, rejection sampling computes the normalised
one, and the two programs are related by precisely the evidence
constant. The proof is one line on top of
\autoref{thm:cbv-score-posterior} and the master identity of
\autoref{thm:cbv-reject-master}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[The random-function marginals]%
\label{thm:cbv-basic-marginals}
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.cbv_marginals.ex_random_constant_cbv_marginal}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.cbv_marginals.ex_random_constant_cbv_marginal_dirac}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.cbv_marginals.ex_random_linear_cbv_marginal}\rocqok
\uses{thm:cbv-let-sample, lem:cbv-marginal-kit, thm:cbv-fix-comb}
The denotation of $\mathtt{ex\_random\_constant}$
($\mathtt{let}\,c = \mathtt{sample}\,\mu\,\mathtt{in}\,\lambda x.\,c$)
is a promoted function value; derelicting it and evaluating at any
\emph{probability} test point $x$ recovers the prior:
\[
  \mathrm{der}\bigl(\sem{\mathtt{ex\_random\_constant}}(1)\bigr)(x)
  \;=\; \mu
  \qquad\text{whenever } x(\mathbb{R}) = 1.
\]
The probability hypothesis is honest: discarding the unused argument
is the comonoid counit, which weighs by the argument's total mass
(\autoref{lem:cbv-marginal-kit}) --- at $x = 0$ the marginal is $0$,
not $\mu$. The Dirac instance is
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.cbv_marginals.ex_random_constant_cbv_marginal_dirac}.
For $\mathtt{ex\_random\_linear}$
($\mathtt{let}\,m = \mathtt{sample}\,\mu\,\mathtt{in}\,
\mathtt{let}\,b = \mathtt{sample}\,\mu\,\mathtt{in}\,
\lambda x.\,m\,x + b$ --- the random affine \emph{model} of the
Bayesian regression below), the marginal at a Dirac test point
$\delta_{r_0}$ is the iterated-integral measure
\[
  \mathrm{der}\bigl(\sem{\mathtt{ex\_random\_linear}}(1)\bigr)
    (\delta_{r_0})(U)
  \;=\;
  \int\!\!\int \delta_{m r_0 + b}(U)\;\mu(db)\,\mu(dm)
\]
(\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.cbv_marginals.ex_random_linear_cbv_marginal},
with the one-sample inner layer
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.cbv_marginals.rl_inner_marginal}):
two applications of \autoref{thm:cbv-let-sample}, the arithmetic
Dirac rules, and the per-$U$ evaluation of an $\FMeas$-valued Pettis
integral.
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{The Bayesian linear regression.}
The paper-faithful Bayesian-regression program
\rocq{Icones.programs.examples.ex_bayes_linear} (the shape of
Staton--Yang--Heunen--Kammar--Wood, arXiv 1701.02547 \S2.1) samples
the random affine model \emph{once} --- the bound expression is
literally $\mathtt{ex\_random\_linear}$ --- binds it to the name
$\mathtt{"f"}$, then folds a \emph{series} of observations: each
observation (a record \rocq{Icones.programs.examples.obs}, $\mathtt{MkObs}$
of a known input point $\mathtt{obs\_x}$, a likelihood deviation
$\mathtt{obs\_s}$, and an observed datum $\mathtt{obs\_y}$, whose
observation density is the envelope-normalised Gaussian likelihood
$\mathtt{obs\_d}\,o := \mathtt{gauss\_obs\_density}\,(\mathtt{obs\_s}\,o)\,
(\mathtt{obs\_y}\,o) : \mathbb{R} \to [0,1]$) contributes one clause
$\mathtt{let\,\_ = observe\,Gaussian}\,\{\mathtt{obs\_s}\,o,
\mathtt{obs\_y}\,o\}\,
(\#\mathtt{"f"} \mathbin{@} [\![\mathtt{obs\_x}\,o]\!])
\,\mathtt{in}\,\cdots$, scoring the model's value at the known input;
the program returns $\#\mathtt{"f"}$ --- the posterior over
\emph{functions}. The fold over a meta-level observation list is the
iterated-conditioning Rocq fixpoint
\rocq{Icones.programs.examples.iter_condition} (the custom notation
entry cannot recurse over a meta-level \texttt{seq}); the historical
raw score-fold reading is the derived
\rocq{Icones.programs.examples.ex_bayes_linear_obs_fold} via
\rocq{Icones.programs.examples.obs_fold}, a definitional
\texttt{Check} pins the 2-observation instance to the literal
surface sugar, and
\rocq{Icones.programs.examples.ex_bayes_linear3} is the concrete
3-observation instance. The CBV reading is the point: the sampled
\emph{function value} is bound once and shared --- duplicated by the
comonoid $\delta$ at a prom-point of the function-type cone --- across
every observation and the return, so all observations score the
\emph{same} sampled function.

\begin{theorem}[The model evidence of the Bayesian linear regression]%
\label{thm:cbv-bayes-evidence}
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.cbv_marginals.ex_bayes_linear_cbv_evidence}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.cbv_marginals.ex_bayes_linear_cbv_evidence2}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.cbv_marginals.obs_fold_at}\rocqok

For a \emph{general} observation list $l$, the comonoid counit
(``total mass'') of the function-space denotation of
$\mathtt{ex\_bayes\_linear}\,l$ is the \textbf{model evidence} ---
the iterated integral, over the priors on slope and intercept, of the
likelihood of all observations at the model's values:
\[
  \varepsilon\bigl(\sem{\mathtt{ex\_bayes\_linear}\,l}(1)\bigr)
  \;=\;
  \int\!\!\int
    \prod_{o \in l} (\mathtt{obs\_d}\,o)
      \bigl(m \cdot (\mathtt{obs\_x}\,o) + b\bigr)
  \;\mu(db)\,\mu(dm),
\]
with the literal 2-observation product form
(\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.cbv_marginals.ex_bayes_linear_cbv_evidence2}).
The workhorse is the fold computation
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.cbv_marginals.obs_fold_at}: at the
depth-$n$ observation environment over a fixed $(m, b)$, the
denotation of the remaining fold is the promoted model closure scaled
by $\prod_{o \in l}(\mathtt{obs\_d}\,o)(m \cdot \mathtt{obs\_x}\,o +
b)$ --- each scored observation evaluates the shared closure at a
Dirac argument
(\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.cbv_marginals.obs_score_E}) and factors
out as a scalar weight; two applications of the let-at-sample law
(\autoref{thm:cbv-let-sample}) then integrate the weights against the
two priors
(\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.cbv_marginals.obs_evidence_inner}).
\end{theorem}

\section{The rejection-sampling headline and the CBV mass identities}%
\label{sec:cbv-geom-mass}

This section assembles the full semantic analysis of rejection
sampling: a \emph{higher-order}, \emph{probabilistic} (continuous
$\mathtt{sample}$ plus value-dependent coin $\mathtt{ne\_bernoulli\_f}$),
\emph{non-terminating} (rejection recurses, and may loop forever)
program whose CBV denotation is identified in closed form. The story
is told twice: first for the hard-coded sampler $\mathtt{ex\_reject}$
(the original headline --- the normalised posterior of the prior
given the soft acceptance predicate), then in full generality as a
higher-order \emph{combinator} over an arbitrary probabilistic model
(\autoref{ssec:cbv-reject-model},
\texttt{theories/programs/ex\_reject\_model.v}), with the hard-coded
sampler recovered as its simplest instance
(\autoref{thm:cbv-reject-model-instance}). Everything is
axiom-free (the three classical \texttt{boolp} axioms only). The same
reduction-chain skeleton then yields, as riders, the mass identities
for the geometric counter and the parameterised almost-loop ---
completing the mass-identity programme for the recursive surface
programs.

\begin{definition}[The surface program $\mathtt{ex\_reject}$]%
\label{def:cbv-ex-reject}
\rocq{Icones.programs.examples.ex_reject}\rocqok
\uses{def:cbv-fix-clause, lem:ppl-bern-lift}
Given a bundled prior $m$ (a \texttt{pmeas}: a sub-probability on
the reals, here assumed of unit mass) and a bundled acceptance
density $d : \mathtt{udensity}$ (write $f := \mathtt{ud\_f}\,d :
\mathbb{R} \to [0, 1]$), the program is
\[
  \mathtt{ex\_reject}
  \;=\;
  \mathtt{let\,rec}\;\textit{rs}\;\textit{accept} =
    \mathtt{let}\;x = \mathtt{sample}\,m\;\mathtt{in}\;
    \mathtt{if}\;\mathtt{Bernoulli}\,d\,x\;
    \mathtt{then}\;\textit{accept}\;x\;
    \mathtt{else}\;\textit{rs}\;\textit{accept}
  \;\;\mathtt{in}\;\;
  \textit{rs}\;(\lambda y.\,y),
\]
a closed term of type $\mathtt{tR}$ written in the direct-style
surface notation (the OCaml-style \texttt{let rec} sugar, over the
bundled sub-probability prior $m$), with the recursive sampler
applied to the identity acceptance continuation in the
\texttt{let rec} continuation. Write
$\nu = \sem{\mathtt{ex\_reject}}(1) \in \FMeas(R_\mathrm{obj})$ for
its closed CBV denotation, $\mu$ for the measure carried by $m$,
$I_f = \int f\,d\mu$ and $I_f^U = \int_U f\,d\mu$.
\end{definition}

\subsection{The scalar affine cascade and the sup-mass bridge}%
\label{ssec:cbv-affine-cascade}

\begin{lemma}[Affine iteration: closed form and limit]%
\label{lem:cbv-affine-cascade}
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.affine_cascade.affine_iter_closed}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.affine_cascade.affine_iter_geom}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.affine_cascade.affine_iter_cvg}\rocqok
\uses{def:cone}
For reals $a, q \geq 0$ and a sequence $x$ with $x_0 = 0$ and
$x_{n+1} = a + q\,x_n$:
$x_n = a \sum_{i < n} q^i$
(\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.affine_cascade.affine_iter_closed});
for $q \neq 1$, $x_n = a\,(1 - q^n)/(1 - q)$
(\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.affine_cascade.affine_iter_geom});
for $q < 1$, $x_n \to a / (1 - q)$
(\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.affine_cascade.affine_iter_cvg_real},
and in $\overline{\mathbb{R}}$
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.affine_cascade.affine_iter_cvg}); the
degenerate case $q = 1, a = 0$ gives $x_n = 0$
(\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.affine_cascade.affine_iter_deg_eq0}).
The instance $a = q = 1/2$ has limit $1$
(\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.affine_cascade.affine_iter_cvg_half}).
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[The sup-mass bridge]\label{lem:cbv-sup-mass}
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.affine_cascade.fmeas_kleene_sup_U_cvg}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.affine_cascade.fmeas_kleene_sup_U_E}\rocqok
\uses{def:fmeas-carrier}
For a unit-ball $\omega$-chain $\nu : \mathbb{N} \to \FMeas(X)$ and a
measurable $U$, the masses $\nu_n(U)$ converge to the mass of the
unit-ball supremum at $U$
(\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.affine_cascade.fmeas_kleene_sup_U_cvg});
hence any limit of $\nu_n(U)$ \emph{is} that mass, by Hausdorff
uniqueness
(\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.affine_cascade.fmeas_kleene_sup_U_E}).
The bridge is definitional at $\FMeas$: the cone supremum is
$\mathtt{fmeas\_sup\_ball}$ of the HB instance.
\end{lemma}

\subsection{The reduction chain}\label{ssec:cbv-reject-chain}

\begin{lemma}[Steps 1--4: from the program to the per-iterate
branch dispatch]\label{lem:cbv-reject-chain}
\rocq{Icones.programs.ex_reject_headline.ex_reject_app_E}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.ex_reject_headline.ex_reject_sup_E}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.ex_reject_headline.ex_reject_iter_S}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.ex_reject_headline.ex_reject_inner_at_dirac}\rocqok
\uses{def:cbv-ex-reject, thm:cbv-fix-unfold, thm:cbv-fix-value,
def:cbv-case-em}
Writing $W_0$ for the body's endo-function at the empty context and
$a_0$ for the promoted identity continuation:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item the outer application of the promoted fixpoint to the identity
    continuation collapses --- $\mathrm{der} \circ \mathrm{prom}$
    cancels before any continuity argument --- leaving
    $\nu = (\mathtt{fix\_value}\,W_0)(a_0)$
    (\rocq{Icones.programs.ex_reject_headline.ex_reject_app_E});
  \item $\nu$ is the unit-ball supremum of the per-iterate measures
    $\nu_n = (\mathtt{fix\_chain}\,W_0\,n)(a_0)$
    (\rocq{Icones.programs.ex_reject_headline.reject_iter}), because
    evaluation at a point commutes with the Kleene supremum ---
    linhom-cone sups are pointwise
    (\rocq{Icones.programs.ex_reject_headline.linhom_fun_sup_ball},
    \rocq{Icones.programs.ex_reject_headline.ex_reject_sup_E});
  \item the Kleene step is the inner let-if body evaluated at the
    extended setlike environment
    (\rocq{Icones.programs.ex_reject_headline.ex_reject_iter_S});
  \item at the environment $\gamma \otimes \delta_r$ the branch
    dispatch computes
    (\rocq{Icones.programs.ex_reject_headline.ex_reject_inner_at_dirac}):
    the scrutinee is the coin
    $\mathtt{bernoulli}(f\,r)$, THEN gives the accepted sample
    $\delta_r$, and ELSE --- the recursive call at the \emph{same}
    continuation --- is the previous iterate $\nu_n$, so the dispatch
    is
    $\mathtt{bool\_case}\,(\mathtt{bernoulli}(f\,r))\,
    (\delta_r)\,(\nu_n)$.
\end{enumerate}
The generic setlike kit
(\rocq{Icones.programs.ex_reject_headline.adj_psi_at_setlike},
\rocq{Icones.programs.ex_reject_headline.if_icones_at}, and the
anchors of \autoref{ssec:cbv-anchors}) drives every pointwise
computation.
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[Step 5: the mass recurrence]%
\label{lem:cbv-reject-recurrence}
\rocq{Icones.programs.ex_reject_headline.ex_reject_iter_mass}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.ex_reject_headline.reject_case_mass}\rocqok
\uses{lem:cbv-reject-chain, lem:cbv-let-sample-mu}
For every measurable $U$,
\[
  \nu_{n+1}(U)
  \;=\;
  \int_U f\,d\mu
  \;+\;
  \bigl(1 - I_f\bigr)\cdot \nu_n(U).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The let-at-sample measure-on-$U$ law
(\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.let_sample_law.eD_let_sample_mu_E},
\autoref{lem:cbv-let-sample-mu}) turns step 3 into an integral over
$r \sim \mu$ of the dispatched branch mass of step 4, which is
$f(r)\cdot\mathbf{1}_U(r) + (1 - f(r))\cdot\nu_n(U)$
(\rocq{Icones.programs.ex_reject_headline.reject_case_mass});
splitting the integral and using the unit mass of the prior
($\int (1 - f)\,d\mu = 1 - I_f$,
\rocq{Icones.programs.ex_reject_headline.reject_int_onem}) gives the
affine recurrence.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The headline theorems}\label{ssec:cbv-reject-thms}

\begin{theorem}[Rejection sampling denotes the normalised
posterior]\label{thm:cbv-reject-posterior}
\rocq{Icones.programs.ex_reject_headline.ex_reject_is_normalised_posterior}\rocqok
\uses{lem:cbv-reject-recurrence, lem:cbv-affine-cascade,
lem:cbv-sup-mass}
If $0 < I_f$ then for every measurable $U$,
\[
  \nu(U) \;=\; \frac{\int_U f\,d\mu}{\int f\,d\mu}
\]
--- the normalised posterior of the prior $\mu$ given the soft
acceptance predicate $f$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The per-iterate masses $x_n = \nu_n(U)$ satisfy $x_0 = 0$ and the
affine recurrence of \autoref{lem:cbv-reject-recurrence} with
$a = I_f^U$ and $q = 1 - I_f < 1$; by
\autoref{lem:cbv-affine-cascade} they converge to
$I_f^U / I_f$, and the sup-mass bridge
(\autoref{lem:cbv-sup-mass}) identifies that limit with the mass of
the supremum $\nu$ at $U$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The division-free master form, and the
corollaries]\label{thm:cbv-reject-master}
\rocq{Icones.programs.ex_reject_headline.ex_reject_master}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.ex_reject_headline.ex_reject_posterior_simple}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.ex_reject_headline.ex_reject_mass_one}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.ex_reject_headline.ex_reject_zero}\rocqok
\uses{thm:cbv-reject-posterior}
Unconditionally --- graceful at $I_f = 0$ --- for every measurable
$U$:
\[
  I_f \cdot \nu(U) \;=\; I_f^U.
\]
Corollaries: if $I_f = 1$ then $\nu(U) = I_f^U$, the reweighted prior
itself
(\rocq{Icones.programs.ex_reject_headline.ex_reject_posterior_simple});
if $0 < I_f$ then $\nu(\mathbb{R}) = 1$ --- the sampler terminates
almost surely whenever acceptance has positive mass
(\rocq{Icones.programs.ex_reject_headline.ex_reject_mass_one}); and if
$f \equiv 0$ then $\nu = 0$ --- certain rejection diverges
(\rocq{Icones.programs.ex_reject_headline.ex_reject_zero}).
\end{theorem}

\subsection{The rejection-sampling combinator over any model}%
\label{ssec:cbv-reject-model}

The headline generalises from the hard-coded sampler to a
higher-order \emph{combinator} over an arbitrary probabilistic model
(\texttt{theories/programs/ex\_reject\_model.v}): a model is any
function value $m : t_a \to \mathtt{tR}$ --- itself a lambda-written
program, free to contain samples, scores and recursion --- and the
combinator takes the model and an input and computes the
corresponding normalised distribution. The theorems are
\emph{sub-probability honest}: the model may itself diverge, and its
missing mass enters the normaliser.

\begin{definition}[The surface combinator
$\mathtt{ex\_reject\_comb}$]\label{def:cbv-ex-reject-comb}
\rocq{Icones.programs.examples.ex_reject_comb}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.examples.ex_sampler}\rocqok
\uses{def:cbv-fix-clause, lem:ppl-bern-lift, def:cbv-ex-reject}
For an \emph{arbitrary} PPL input type $t_a$ and a bundled acceptance
density $d : \mathtt{udensity}$ (write $f := \mathtt{ud\_f}\,d :
\mathbb{R} \to [0,1]$), the combinator is the
closed program of type
$(t_a \to \mathtt{tR}) \to (t_a \to \mathtt{tR})$
\[
  \mathtt{ex\_reject\_comb}
  \;=\;
  \mathtt{fix}\;\textit{rs}.\;\lambda m.\;\lambda a.\;
    \mathtt{let}\;x = m\;a\;\mathtt{in}\;
    \mathtt{if}\;\mathtt{Bernoulli}\,d\,x\;
    \mathtt{then}\;x\;
    \mathtt{else}\;\textit{rs}\;m\;a
\]
--- run the model at the input, accept the produced value with
probability $f(x)$, recurse at the \emph{same} model and input on
rejection. The simplest model is the lambda-written sampler
$\mathtt{ex\_sampler} = \lambda\_.\,\mathtt{sample}\,\mu$
(\rocq{Icones.programs.examples.ex_sampler}), of type
$\mathtt{tunit} \to \mathtt{tR}$.

The theorems quantify over the model VALUE and the input VALUE: the
model argument is the promoted point $g^!$ of an arbitrary unit-ball
linear map $g : U\sem{t_a} \multimap \FMeas$ --- every lambda-written
model denotes such a point, since the $\lambda$-clause promotes the
curried body at setlike environments
(\rocq{Icones.programs.ex_reject_model.sampler_val_E} is the worked
instance) --- and the input is an arbitrary setlike unit-ball point
$a_0 : U\sem{t_a}$. Write
$\nu_M = g(a_0)$ for the model's output \emph{sub}-distribution at
the input (\rocq{Icones.programs.ex_reject_model.reject_model_dist}),
$m_0 = \nu_M(\mathbb{R})$ for its total mass,
$I_f = \int f\,d\nu_M$, $I_f^U = \int_U f\,d\nu_M$, and $\nu$ for the
CBV denotation of $(\mathtt{ex\_reject\_comb}\;m)\;a$
(\rocq{Icones.programs.ex_reject_model.reject_model_denot}).
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[The parameterised reduction chain]%
\label{lem:cbv-reject-model-chain}
\rocq{Icones.programs.ex_reject_model.reject_comb_val_E}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.ex_reject_model.reject_model_sup_E}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.ex_reject_model.reject_model_iter_S}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.ex_reject_model.reject_model_if_at_dirac}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.ex_reject_model.rm_model_app_E}\rocqok
\uses{def:cbv-ex-reject-comb, lem:cbv-reject-chain,
thm:cbv-fix-unfold, thm:cbv-fix-value, def:cbv-case-em}
The reduction chain of \autoref{lem:cbv-reject-chain},
parameterised by the model and the input:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item the closed fixpoint program denotes the promoted fixpoint
    value
    (\rocq{Icones.programs.ex_reject_model.reject_comb_val_E}), and
    the two CBV applications --- to the model $g^!$, then the input
    $a_0$ --- strip promotions by
    $\mathrm{der} \circ \mathrm{prom} = \mathrm{id}$ before any
    continuity argument;
  \item $\nu$ is the unit-ball supremum of the per-iterate measures
    $\nu_n$
    (\rocq{Icones.programs.ex_reject_model.reject_model_sup_E}):
    evaluation at $g^!$, the counit $\mathrm{der}$, and evaluation at
    $a_0$ all commute with the Kleene supremum --- two pointwise
    linhom-sup readings plus the $\omega$-continuity field carried by
    every $\Cones$ morphism
    (\rocq{Icones.programs.ex_reject_model.Lfun_sup_ball});
  \item the Kleene step is the inner let-if body at the extended
    setlike environment
    $((1 \otimes \textit{rs}_n^!) \otimes g^!) \otimes a_0$
    (\rocq{Icones.programs.ex_reject_model.reject_model_iter_S});
  \item the let-bound \emph{model application} computes to the
    model's output distribution, $\sem{m\;@\;a} = \nu_M$
    (\rocq{Icones.programs.ex_reject_model.rm_model_app_E}), and at
    the Dirac extension the branch dispatch computes
    (\rocq{Icones.programs.ex_reject_model.reject_model_if_at_dirac}):
    THEN returns the accepted candidate $\delta_r$, ELSE --- the
    recursive call at the \emph{same} model and input --- is the
    previous iterate $\nu_n$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[The sub-probability mass recurrence]%
\label{lem:cbv-reject-model-recurrence}
\rocq{Icones.programs.ex_reject_model.reject_model_iter_mass}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.ex_reject_model.rm_case_mass}\rocqok
\uses{lem:cbv-reject-model-chain, lem:cbv-let-general}
For every measurable $U$,
\[
  \nu_{n+1}(U)
  \;=\;
  \int_U f\,d\nu_M
  \;+\;
  \bigl(m_0 - I_f\bigr)\cdot \nu_n(U).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The general let-law (\autoref{lem:cbv-let-general}, with bound
computation $M = m\,@\,a$) turns the iterate into an integral over
$r \sim \nu_M$ of the dispatched branch mass
$f(r)\cdot\mathbf{1}_U(r) + (1 - f(r))\cdot\nu_n(U)$
(\rocq{Icones.programs.ex_reject_model.rm_case_mass}); splitting the
integral, the rejected-AND-model-terminated weight is
$\int (1 - f)\,d\nu_M = m_0 - I_f$
(\rocq{Icones.programs.ex_reject_model.rm_int_onem}) --- the model's
own divergence mass $1 - m_0$ never re-enters the loop.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Rejection sampling over any model denotes the
normalised distribution]\label{thm:cbv-reject-model}
\rocq{Icones.programs.ex_reject_model.reject_model_master}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.ex_reject_model.reject_model_is_normalised}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.ex_reject_model.reject_model_mass}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.ex_reject_model.reject_model_mass_one}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.ex_reject_model.reject_model_zero}\rocqok
\uses{lem:cbv-reject-model-recurrence, lem:cbv-affine-cascade,
lem:cbv-sup-mass}
Unconditionally, for every measurable $U$, the
\emph{sub-probability-honest} master identity
\[
  \bigl(1 - m_0 + I_f\bigr) \cdot \nu(U) \;=\; I_f^U
\]
(\rocq{Icones.programs.ex_reject_model.reject_model_master}): a
single trial is rejected-and-retried with probability $m_0 - I_f$
(the model terminated AND the coin rejected), so the
success-per-trial mass is $1 - (m_0 - I_f)$. Corollaries: under loop
progress $0 < 1 - m_0 + I_f$ (automatic as soon as $0 < I_f$, and
also whenever the model is a strict sub-probability),
\[
  \nu(U) \;=\; \frac{I_f^U}{1 - m_0 + I_f},
  \qquad
  \nu(\mathbb{R}) \;=\; \frac{I_f}{1 - m_0 + I_f}
\]
(\rocq{Icones.programs.ex_reject_model.reject_model_is_normalised},
\rocq{Icones.programs.ex_reject_model.reject_model_mass}); for a
\emph{probability} model ($m_0 = 1$) with positive acceptance
($0 < I_f$), $\nu(\mathbb{R}) = 1$ --- almost-sure termination, with
the classical normaliser $I_f$
(\rocq{Icones.programs.ex_reject_model.reject_model_mass_one}); and
$f \equiv 0$ gives $\nu = 0$ --- certain rejection diverges, whatever
the model does
(\rocq{Icones.programs.ex_reject_model.reject_model_zero}).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The per-iterate masses satisfy the affine cascade of
\autoref{lem:cbv-reject-model-recurrence} with $a = I_f^U$ and
$q = m_0 - I_f$; here $0 \le q \le 1$ ($I_f \le m_0$ since
$f \le 1$, and $m_0 \le 1$ since the model value and the input are
unit-ball). When $q < 1$ the cascade converges to $a / (1 - q)$
(\autoref{lem:cbv-affine-cascade}) and the sup-mass bridge
(\autoref{lem:cbv-sup-mass}) closes the identity; the degenerate
corner $q = 1$ forces $m_0 = 1$ and $I_f = 0$ --- the loop never
terminates --- and both sides vanish (the chain is constantly zero).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The instance bridge: the hard-coded sampler is the
simplest model]\label{thm:cbv-reject-model-instance}
\rocq{Icones.programs.ex_reject_model.ex_reject_comb_sampler_E}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.ex_reject_model.ex_reject_comb_sampler_master}\rocqok
\uses{thm:cbv-reject-model, thm:cbv-reject-master,
def:cbv-ex-reject-comb}
At $t_a = \mathtt{tunit}$ and the sampler model
$\mathtt{ex\_sampler} = \lambda\_.\,\mathtt{sample}\,\mu$ with a
unit-mass prior, $\nu_M = \mu$
(\rocq{Icones.programs.ex_reject_model.sampler_out_E}) and
$m_0 = 1$: the combinator applied to the sampler model and the unit
input denotes \emph{the same measure} as the original
$\mathtt{ex\_reject}$ of \autoref{def:cbv-ex-reject}
(\rocq{Icones.programs.ex_reject_model.ex_reject_comb_sampler_E}) ---
the two programs have different surface shapes (one abstracts over an
acceptance continuation, the other over a model), but their Kleene
chains satisfy the same per-iterate mass cascade from $0$
(\rocq{Icones.programs.ex_reject_model.inst_iter_massE}), so the
suprema coincide by uniqueness of limits. The classical master
identity $I_f \cdot \nu(U) = I_f^U$ is re-derived through the bridge
(\rocq{Icones.programs.ex_reject_model.ex_reject_comb_sampler_master}).
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Soft conditioning, and the equivalence with rejection
sampling}\label{ssec:cbv-condition}

The declarative counterpart of the rejection combinator: the
Pyro-style \emph{soft conditioning} operator takes a model and
returns the conditioned model --- run the model, \emph{score} the
produced value by the likelihood, return it. The capstone theorem of
this section identifies the two combinators' semantics: rejection
sampling computes the conditioned model's \emph{normalised}
distribution.

\begin{definition}[The condition combinator
$\mathtt{ex\_condition\_comb}$]\label{def:cbv-ex-condition}
\rocq{Icones.programs.examples.ex_condition_comb}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.examples.ex_condition}\rocqok
\uses{def:cbv-ex-reject-comb, lem:ppl-score-tm-lift}
For an arbitrary PPL input type $t_a$ and a bundled likelihood
density $d : \mathtt{udensity}$ (write $f := \mathtt{ud\_f}\,d :
\mathbb{R} \to [0,1]$), the soft conditioning operator is
the closed program of type
$(t_a \to \mathtt{tR}) \to (t_a \to \mathtt{tR})$
\[
  \mathtt{condition}
  \;=\;
  \lambda m.\;\lambda a.\;
    \mathtt{let}\;x = m\;a\;\mathtt{in}\;
    \mathtt{let}\;\_ = \mathtt{Score}\,d\,x\;\mathtt{in}\;
    x
\]
--- run the model at the input, weigh the trace by the likelihood of
the produced value, return the value
(\rocq{Icones.programs.examples.ex_condition_comb}). The applied form
$\mathtt{condition}\;M$ --- the conditioned MODEL, again of type
$t_a \to \mathtt{tR}$ --- is
\rocq{Icones.programs.examples.ex_condition}, with the surface
notation \texttt{Condition \{ d \} M}. No recursion is
involved: the combinator is a plain double lambda.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[The conditioning law]\label{lem:cbv-condition-law}
\rocq{Icones.programs.ex_reject_model.condition_model_E}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.ex_reject_model.condition_model_mass}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.ex_reject_model.condition_E}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.ex_reject_model.condition_prog_evidence}\rocqok
\uses{def:cbv-ex-condition, lem:cbv-let-general,
lem:ppl-score-tm-lift}
In the set-up of \autoref{def:cbv-ex-reject-comb} --- a unit-ball
model value $g^!$, a setlike unit-ball input $a_0$, and the model's
output sub-distribution $\nu_M = g(a_0)$ --- the conditioned model's
output at the input is the likelihood-reweighted measure: for every
measurable $U$,
\[
  \sem{(\mathtt{condition}\;m)\;a}(U)
  \;=\;
  \int_U f\,d\nu_M
\]
(\rocq{Icones.programs.ex_reject_model.condition_model_E}); at
$U = \mathbb{R}$ the total mass is the \emph{model evidence}
$I_f = \int f\,d\nu_M$
(\rocq{Icones.programs.ex_reject_model.condition_model_mass}). This
generalises the unnormalised score posterior of
\autoref{thm:cbv-score-posterior} from the hard-coded
$\mathtt{sample}\,\mu$ to an arbitrary model. The readable
restatements
\rocq{Icones.programs.ex_reject_model.condition_E} and
\rocq{Icones.programs.ex_reject_model.condition_prog_evidence} phrase
the law under the denotation brackets, over an arbitrary thunked
model program.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The reduction chain of \autoref{lem:cbv-reject-model-chain} minus the
fixpoint: the closed double lambda denotes a promoted curried stage
and the two applications strip promotions by
$\mathrm{der} \circ \mathrm{prom} = \mathrm{id}$; the general let-law
(\autoref{lem:cbv-let-general}, bound computation $M = m\,@\,a$)
turns the body into an integral over $r \sim \nu_M$; and at each
Dirac $\delta_r$ the score-and-return continuation computes to the
weighted point mass $f(r)\cdot\delta_r$ (the score scalar via the
Dirac identity of \autoref{lem:ppl-score-tm-lift}, discharged into a
$\mathrm{precone\_scale}$ weight through the non-setlike
$\mathtt{tunit}$ projection
\rocq{Icones.programs.infra.cbv_marginals.em_proj1_mor_unitE}).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Rejection sampling computes the conditioned model's
normalised distribution]\label{thm:cbv-condition-reject}
\rocq{Icones.programs.ex_reject_model.reject_normalises_condition}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.ex_reject_model.reject_prog_computes_condition}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.ex_reject_model.reject_normalises_condition_prob}\rocqok
\uses{lem:cbv-condition-law, thm:cbv-reject-model}
Over the same arbitrary model program and the same input, write
$Z = 1 - \nu_M(\mathbb{R}) + \int f\,d\nu_M$ for the
sub-probability-honest normaliser. Then, unconditionally, for every
measurable $U$,
\[
  Z \cdot \sem{\mathtt{reject}\;M\;f}(U)
  \;=\;
  \sem{\mathtt{condition}\;M\;f}(U)
\]
(\rocq{Icones.programs.ex_reject_model.reject_normalises_condition});
under loop progress $0 < Z$, the division form
\[
  \sem{\mathtt{reject}\;M\;f}(U)
  \;=\;
  \frac{\sem{\mathtt{condition}\;M\;f}(U)}{Z}
\]
(\rocq{Icones.programs.ex_reject_model.reject_prog_computes_condition});
and for a \emph{probability} model ($\nu_M(\mathbb{R}) = 1$) the
normaliser is the conditioned model's total mass --- the model
evidence --- so
\[
  \sem{\mathtt{condition}\;M\;f}(\mathbb{R})
  \cdot \sem{\mathtt{reject}\;M\;f}(U)
  \;=\;
  \sem{\mathtt{condition}\;M\;f}(U)
\]
(\rocq{Icones.programs.ex_reject_model.reject_normalises_condition_prob}).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Both sides equal $\int_U f\,d\nu_M$: rewrite the right-hand side by
the conditioning law (\autoref{lem:cbv-condition-law}) and apply the
master identity of \autoref{thm:cbv-reject-model} (in its readable
restatement). The probability-model form additionally identifies
$Z = \int f\,d\nu_M = \sem{\mathtt{condition}\;M\;f}(\mathbb{R})$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The Bayesian regression is iterated conditioning]%
\label{lem:cbv-bayes-iter-condition}
\rocq{Icones.programs.examples.condition_at}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.examples.iter_condition}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.examples.obs_fold_is_iter_condition}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.examples.ex_bayes_linear_is_iter_condition}\rocqok
\uses{def:cbv-ex-condition, thm:cbv-bayes-evidence}
Each observation step of the Bayesian linear regression
(\autoref{thm:cbv-bayes-evidence}) is a soft conditioning of the
model's value at the observation point: scoring the model application
at the known input by the observation density is the score clause of
\autoref{def:cbv-ex-condition} (the only difference is A-normal form
--- the regression scores the application directly and returns the
\emph{function} at the end). Packaging one such step as
\rocq{Icones.programs.examples.condition_at} and its fold as
\rocq{Icones.programs.examples.iter_condition}, the regression is
\emph{defined} as the random affine model bound once, then
conditioned on each observation in turn --- the named agreement
anchor

\end{lemma}

\begin{theorem}[The geometric counter has CBV mass one]%
\label{thm:cbv-ex-geom}
\rocq{Icones.programs.ex_reject_headline.ex_geom_cbv_mass_one}\rocqok
\uses{lem:cbv-reject-chain, lem:cbv-affine-cascade, lem:cbv-sup-mass}

\rocq{Icones.programs.examples.ex_geom} is a probability

$\sem{\mathtt{ex\_geom}}(1)(\mathbb{R}) = 1$. The same

$\nu_{n+1} = \tfrac12\,\delta_0 + \tfrac12\,(\delta_1 + \nu_n)$,

(\texttt{add\_lift\_mass}) reduces the cascade to

and the atom at the embedded natural $k$ carries mass exactly
$(1/2)^{k+1}$ --- the geometric PMF
(\rocq{Icones.programs.ex_reject_headline.ex_geom_cbv_pmf}\rocqok).

\begin{theorem}[Dichotomy for the parameterised almost-loop]%
\label{thm:cbv-ex-almost-loop}
\rocq{Icones.programs.ex_reject_headline.ex_almost_loop_cbv_mass_one}\rocqok
\rocq{Icones.programs.ex_reject_headline.ex_almost_loop_cbv_zero}\rocqok

For \rocq{Icones.programs.examples.ex_almost_loop} at flip probability
$p$ (a $\mathtt{tunit}$-valued program, whose denotation is a point of
the unit cone with mass the termination probability): if $0 < p$ the
denotation has full mass
(\rocq{Icones.programs.ex_reject_headline.ex_almost_loop_cbv_mass_one});
if $p = 0$ it is the zero point --- certain divergence
(\rocq{Icones.programs.ex_reject_headline.ex_almost_loop_cbv_zero}).
For $0 < p$ the denotation is moreover pinned as the \emph{element}
$\mathtt{one1}$ --- the unit point, the Dirac on the one-point space ---
strengthening the norm identity to the cone element itself
(\rocq{Icones.programs.ex_reject_headline.ex_almost_loop_cbv_dirac}\rocqok),
since a norm-one element of the one-dimensional unit cone is
$\mathtt{one1}$.
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{What is not interpreted.}
$\mathrm{EM}(\mathord{!})$ is not cartesian closed and is not expected
to be --- a structural fact about EM categories of linear-exponential
comonads, not a missing diagram chase. The linhom-valued reading of
\autoref{sec:cbv-ppl} is what the SMCC + EM(!) data of
\autoref{thm:cbv-model} naturally supports, and that holds axiom-free.
The genuinely out-of-scope items are: adequacy, normalization, and
full-abstraction (traditional metatheory, requiring more than a
denotational model); coproducts beyond the 2-point boolean case of
\autoref{sec:cbv-bool} (no general sum type in the cones model); and a
Moggi metalanguage with an explicit value/computation split
(orthogonal to the linhom-valued framing adopted here). A general
$\mathtt{normalize}$ / Bayesian-posterior \emph{pass} is also not
interpreted, but the conditioning / rejection equivalence
(\autoref{thm:cbv-condition-reject}, with the hard-coded instance
\autoref{thm:cbv-reject-posterior}) shows the model computes the
conditioned model's normalised distribution for the soft-likelihood
shape: normalisation there is performed by the rejection program's
own recursion, not by a metalanguage primitive.
